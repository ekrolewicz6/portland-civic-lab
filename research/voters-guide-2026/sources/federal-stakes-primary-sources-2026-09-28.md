Notes for the card:

### c. "Without the detention and oversight conditions carried in annual spending bills": what the primary record supports

Supported by primary sources, with care in wording:

1. The Secure America Act text contains no detention standards, inspection, congressional-access, or reporting conditions (full text read; see above).
2. CRS describes how the ICE-related oversight provisions left the annual bill (R48874 v4, p. 9): "The Senate passed an amended version of H.R. 7147 by voice vote on March 27, 2026, which did not include funding for the U.S. Border Patrol or U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, nor directive and oversight provisions related to those entities. The Senate majority indicated they would fund those components through an FY2026 reconciliation package."
3. CRS on process (p. 2): "The partial funding of the department through the Department of Homeland Security Appropriations Act, 2026, as well as the provision of operating funds for CBP and ICE in a reconciliation measure that did not go through the appropriations committee were both unprecedented actions."
4. Text comparison, House-passed vs. enacted annual bill:
   - The January 22, 2026 House-passed compromise (H.R. 7147 EH, https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr7147eh/html/BILLS-119hr7147eh.htm) had an ICE heading and ICE-specific detention conditions, e.g. Sec. 212 (no ICE funds to continue a 287(g) delegation "if the Department of Homeland Security Inspector General determines that the terms of the agreement governing the delegation of authority have been materially violated") and Sec. 213 (no ICE funds "to continue any contract for the provision of detention services if the two most recent overall performance evaluations received by the contracted facility are less than 'adequate'").
   - The enacted annual law, P.L. 119-86 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ86/html/PLAW-119publ86.htm), has no ICE heading and neither of those sections. Its Sec. 4 zeroes out ICE and Border Security Operations: amounts "under the heading 'U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement' in the 'Department of Homeland Security Act, 2026' table shall all be $0."
   - P.L. 119-86 does keep department-wide conditions that by their terms reach only "funds made available in this Act": Sec. 527 (no restraints on pregnant women in DHS custody, with exceptions), Sec. 528 (no destroying records of deaths or abuse in custody), Sec. 546 (no funds to block a Member of Congress or designated staff from entering a DHS detention facility for oversight, no prior notice required for Members), Sec. 109 ($20,000,000 "for the procurement, deployment, and operations of body-worn cameras for agents and officers performing enforcement activities"), and $20,000,000 to the Inspector General "for additional inspections and oversight of detention facilities."
5. Senate floor record: oversight amendments to S. 2 were offered and rejected, e.g. Vote 150, Booker Amdt. 5803 "To support the work performed by the Immigration Detention Ombudsman," waiver rejected 46-53 (June 4); Vote 146, Kim Amdt. 5545 "To make appropriations for the conduct of inspections of the Delaney Hall Detention Facility," rejected 46-53 (June 4); Vote 157, Padilla Amdt. 5808 "To make appropriations for transparency and accountability," rejected 46-53 (June 5). Source: https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/roll_call_lists/vote_menu_119_2.htm

Not confirmed: the Council fact sheet's specific item about "language limiting the sharing of data between ICE and the Office of Refugee Resettlement's unaccompanied migrant children program." I did not find it in the House-passed H.R. 7147 text or in P.L. 119-86. Do not use that item without a primary cite.

Suggested wording that stays inside the record: "The law sets no detention or oversight conditions on the money. The annual Homeland Security spending law, enacted April 30, left out ICE and Border Patrol along with the oversight provisions tied to them (Congressional Research Service)."

### 10,000 acres and 120 days: what the text actually says

- 10,000 acres. The House bill raises the acreage cap on existing categorical exclusions rather than creating a new one named for firesheds. H.R. 471 Sec. 106(b)(1): amends the Healthy Forests Restoration Act "in section 603(c)(1) (16 U.S.C. 6591b(c)(1)), by striking '3000 acres' and inserting '10,000 acres'"; the same change in section 605(c)(1); section 606(g) from "4,500 acres" to "10,000 acres"; and IIJA section 40806(d)(1) from "3,000 acres" to "10,000 acres." Sec. 106(a)(3)(B) then makes fireshed projects eligible: "Fireshed management projects carried out under this section shall be considered authorized projects under the following categorical exclusions" (listing HFRA 603(a), 605(a), 606(b), IIJA 40806(b) and the Lake Tahoe Restoration Act). The Senate committee version keeps 10,000 acres for 603, 605 and 40806 but sets 7,500 acres for the sage-grouse and mule deer exclusion (606).
- 120 days. House bill only. H.R. 471 Sec. 121(d): "a claim arising under Federal law seeking judicial review of a covered agency action shall be barred unless ... such claim is filed not later than 120 days after the date of publication of a notice in the Federal Register of agency intent to carry out the fireshed management project ..." The Senate committee version (S. 1462 RS, Sec. 131(c)) uses 150 days: "such claim is filed not later than 150 days after the date of publication of a notice in the Federal Register ..."
- CRS summary of H.R. 471 (as introduced): "the bill expedites the review of certain forest management projects under the National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) of 1969 and exempts certain activities from NEPA review. ... Finally, it limits litigation involving fireshed management projects and limits remedies that courts may provide." The CRS summary does not state the acreage or day figures; those come from the bill text.

## Could not confirm or needs a wording change

1. Card 1, "without the detention and oversight conditions": no primary source uses that phrasing. The law text has no such conditions, and CRS says the enacted annual bill dropped ICE and Border Patrol "nor directive and oversight provisions related to those entities." Suggested wording is under 1c.
2. Card 1, the Council's ICE-ORR data-sharing item: not found in H.R. 7147 (House-passed) or P.L. 119-86.
3. Card 1, "76-day": computed from the law's February 14 start and April 30 enactment (inclusive count) and stated by NPR; no government document gives the number. It ended the lapse for most of DHS, not ICE or Border Patrol.
4. Card 1, cause of the lapse: news only (NPR, Roll Call). CRS describes the sequence but not the motive.
5. Card 2, "about 60 countries": USTR says 60 economies (EU counted once, Taiwan included).
6. Card 3, "120-day limit": true of the House-passed bill; the Senate committee version says 150 days.
7. Card 3, "10,000-acre fireshed categorical exclusion": the bill raises caps on existing HFRA and IIJA exclusions to 10,000 acres and applies them to fireshed projects.
8. Card 3, "Roadless Rule repeal in August 2026": wrong as stated. It was a proposed rescission published August 20, 2026. Comments close October 6, 2026, and no final rule has been published.
