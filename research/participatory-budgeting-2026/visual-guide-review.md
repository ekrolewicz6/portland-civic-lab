Guide release: October 2, 2026. The original research remains dated September 17–20; moving it does not imply that every historical source received a new audit.

## Editorial scope

The guide has about 1,860 visible words (including navigation, labels and citations; closed details excluded), compared with the much longer original report. Original explanatory paragraphs scored approximately grade 9.5 using a basic Flesch–Kincaid estimate (heuristic syllable counts, not a validated comprehension test). An assistant editorial pass checked sentence structure, definitions, conditional claims, campaign attribution, and the difference between requirements and predictions. This is not a record of independent human editorial sign-off.

The guide uses seven reading sections, three exact excerpts per campaign, original inline SVG illustrations, and one small client component for the allocation scenarios. The whole research report—including its introduction, all 14 sections, tables, reference links and supplied documents—is rendered at `/deep-dives/participatory-budgeting/research`. The source Markdown is unchanged. No database or public API changed.

## Source verification

- Official amendment: prior-year **adopted General Fund discretionary ongoing expenses**, a minimum 2%, annual allocation including administration, funding by FY2027–28, process by July 2028, staff feasibility review, oversight and auditing. Exact formula is visible; definitions are expandable.
- Official ballot filing: $16.4M is the preliminary FY2027–28 allocation, not a verified current appropriation. The differing July 2027 summary date is explicitly distinguished from the amendment's two milestones.
- YES home: `Puts the power in residents’ hands` checked against the campaign page.
- YES FAQ question 1: `We believe all residents can and should have a greater direct voice and vote` verified by opening its accordion in a browser.
- Supplied policy sheet, page 2: `participatory budgeting (PB) redistributes power rather than prescribes outcomes.` verified against extracted PDF text.
- NO home: `You can believe in a bigger voice for Portlanders and vote NO on Measure 26-267.` verified in the current page.
- NO measure page: residency/minimum-age excerpt verified in current page.
- NO FAQ: `through a pilot like the one City Club recommended.` verified in current page.
- PB Oregon: the $1M administration estimate is labeled an advocate's estimate. $2M and $3M are explicitly illustrative alternatives. Arithmetic: 15.4/14.4/13.4 million remains from 16.4 million; administration shares 6.1/12.2/18.3%.
- Metro, July 21, 2026: 23 Community Choice projects, $3M; Milwaukie Bay Park river-access award $166,400. Checked current primary-source page. This is an award announcement, not a completion report.
- Cambridge, March 19, 2026: cycle 12; 10,172 voters; $100,000 sidewalk/tree project. Described as selections, not completed outcomes.
- Seattle: 2023 selection date preserved in the September 17 primary-source archive. December 10, 2024 city release directly verifies authorization in the 2025–26 budget and $2M for crisis response. Guide links this accessible release; no assertion that these projects remain undelivered today.

The case-study illustrations are conceptual artwork, not photographs or maps of actual sites. Campaign statements are attributed and linked; explanatory paragraphs are explicitly identified as summaries. The two campaign panels use the same layout, typography and three-argument structure.
