# Capacity diagnosis: a place for everyone?

Verified September 8, 2026. Research only; no page code changed. Eight primary sources below. Dates, units, and program scope must stay beside the graphics.

## Direct answer

**No. The last comparable countywide count had far more people experiencing homelessness than shelter and transitional beds. The 2026 funding decisions remove additional capacity. And a listed bed is not necessarily an available, suitable offer.**

That answer has three distinct parts: an historical physical shortfall, current reductions, and access restrictions. Do not present the January 2025 arithmetic as the exact number of beds needed tonight. Permanent housing, prevention, services, and appropriate care can also resolve need; building that many interchangeable adult shelter beds is not the conclusion.

## 1. Same geography, same count: physical capacity falls short

**[HUD 2025 OR-501 Housing Inventory Count, p. 1](https://files.hudexchange.info/reports/published/CoC_HIC_CoC_OR-501-2025_OR_2025.pdf)** — January 2025, Portland/Gresham/Multnomah County CoC. Read directly from downloaded HUD PDF; web parser could not retrieve it. Local verification copy: `/private/tmp/continuum-hic-or501-2025.pdf`.

| Inventory | Year-round beds |
|---|---:|
| Emergency shelter | 3,350 |
| Safe Haven | 20 |
| Transitional housing | 817 |
| **Combined** | **4,187** |
| Seasonal / overflow-voucher additions | 0 / 0 |

The combined figure comprises 514 family beds, 3,656 adult-only beds, and 17 child-only beds. It is operating inventory on the count night, not unoccupied vacancies. HUD excludes beds under development and warns it did not independently verify submissions; inadequately specified programs can be omitted.

**Important geography correction:** the same report's **6,973 PSH beds and 2,663 rapid-rehousing beds are Multnomah County figures, not tri-county figures.** Permanent-housing inventory serves people already housed; adding it to the shelter total does not establish openings for people currently homeless. The 14,541 grand total is therefore not a supply of places waiting to be filled.

**[PSU/HSD 2025 Tri-County PIT report, pp. 8–13, 23–25](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2025-Tri-County-PITC-Report-11.04.25.pdf)** — submitted November 4, 2025; count night January 22, 2025.

| Multnomah County, same count night | People |
|---|---:|
| Sheltered: emergency, Safe Haven, transitional | 3,614 |
| Unsheltered, street-count survey | 1,822 |
| Presumed unsheltered, administrative by-name records | 5,090 |
| **Total** | **10,526** |

This is a snapshot with substantial methodological uncertainty: presumed status is not a verified encounter on count night. The count predates subsequent funding changes; it is not a September 2026 headcount. Do not interpret the increase since 2023 entirely as newly occurring homelessness.

**Derived comparison:** 10,526 people versus 4,187 shelter/transitional beds leaves an arithmetic difference of **6,339**. It shows inventory could not fit everyone simultaneously, even before household matching. It is not an actionable current construction target. Even excluding all 5,090 presumed-unsheltered records leaves 5,436 people, 1,249 above this inventory; this sensitivity check does not establish a better census estimate. The 573 difference between inventory and sheltered count is **not proof of 573 usable vacancies**.

## 2. Current funding removes places; it does not solve the shortfall on its own

**[HSD FY27 adopted budget, pp. 49–50](https://multco.us/file/homeless_services_department-0/download)** — adopted June 5, 2026; FY27 = July 2026–June 2027.

- Announces reductions of **605 adult shelter units and 90 family scattered-site vouchers**.
- Page 49 reports **1,794 County-funded units**, including **75 under City contracts and 52 through Health**; only those 75 City units are included. It also includes a motel closing midyear.
- Page 50's HSD target is **1,742 units**: adult 1,371; family 106; domestic/sexual violence 180; youth 85. The 52-unit difference matches the separately funded Health inventory.
- Page 49 explains the funding tradeoff between maintaining shelter levels and paying for housing assistance/services. It identifies one-time placement funding and phased ramp-down funding.

Use the 1,742 target and 1,794 broader count only with those scopes. They are budget units, not all countywide beds or confirmed September vacancies. Do not add full City capacity without removing overlap. FY26 estimated 2,362 to FY27 target 1,742 is a different accounting comparison from the announced reductions; do not silently equate them.

**[HSD shelter-closure updates](https://hsd.multco.us/2026/05/06/shelter-updates/)** — live page checked September 8; originally May 6, subsequently updated with August events.

Confirmed current changes: Chestnut Tree and VIMo closed; Laurelwood program closed June 30 and property returned September 1; Walnut Park closed July 14; River District Navigation Center closed August 31. Laurelwood's earlier closure was prioritized to maintain safe staffing across simultaneous closures. Bybee's County-funded capacity fell **100 units to 175**, through attrition; the County says nobody was asked to leave for that reduction. Beacon remains operational without County funding and anticipates closure in spring 2027. **Roseway is not accepting new participants** and is closing by October 30.

The specific facility updates are fresher than generic summer schedules and the July shelter directory. The page reports **1,742** remaining County-funded units. Its broad “by December 31” Roseway schedule is superseded by its specific October 30 update. Closure notices describe transfers and housing placements but do not provide a complete, linked outcome count for all displaced residents.

**[City shelter-service changes](https://www.portland.gov/shelter-services/news/2026/7/21/changes-city-shelter-services)** — updated August 24, 2026.

| City-reported change | Previous | Expected after changes | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Regular adult overnight beds, winter | 876 | 580 | −296 (−33.8%) |
| Alternative shelter person capacity | 867 | 718 | −149 (−17.2%) |

These are projections, not today's live inventory. Northrup's 200 overnight beds remain listed until September 18; Nazarene goes from 50 summer beds to 100 in fall. Reedway and North Portland RV changes and pod transfers have summer/fall timing. **718 is people capacity, not units.** The City says 24/7/alternative sites have limited vacancies and require designated outreach referrals. Its listing still includes RDNC; use the newer County confirmation that it closed August 31. Flex capacity is not proof of staffed, operating beds.

## 3. A bed can exist without being a usable door

**[HSD shelter directory](https://hsd.multco.us/emergency-shelters/list-of-shelters/)** — directory marked updated July 2026; access rules checked September 8, with newer closure updates taking precedence.

| Example | Published access limitation |
|---|---|
| Banfield, 58 rooms; Stark, 43 rooms | Referrals from congregate shelter; no outside referrals |
| Rockwood Bridge, 42 rooms | Permanent housing already approved; outside-agency referral |
| Oak Street, 29 units | Specific outreach teams; no self-referrals or third-party referrals |
| Clark, 90 beds; Doreen, 90 beds | Men; drug/alcohol-free setting, TB documentation, waitlist entry |
| Bybee, 175 County-funded beds | Referral; commitment to sobriety; excludes registered sex offenders |

These are documented routing and eligibility restrictions, not evidence all such rules are unjustified. Some settings are deliberately specialized. They show why aggregate capacity cannot establish a suitable offer for any particular household. Several listings explain how to join a queue without giving its length or expected wait. The directory does not give a dated, staffed-vacancy count across programs.

## 4. The exit is constrained by housing money and unequal access to it

**[HSD Adult Shelter Review FY25, pp. 47, 91–94](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf)** — final December 17, 2025; published January 2026; 31 reviewed adult programs, FY25 activity.

The review reports **0.39 permanent-housing exits per bed/unit/year** and **88% average annual occupancy**. Both are historical program measures, not today's vacancies or a countywide placement rate. All ten programs with the fewest housing exits per bed/unit had little or no additional placement funding; most of the ten highest had direct access. Additional placement/inreach programs were associated with **33% of housing exits**. This is observational evidence, not proof of causation.

The review identifies inadequate funding and housing subsidies as common exit barriers and says housing-focused services/positions were reduced or eliminated for FY26. This directly supports a diagnosis of **shelter expansion without sufficient attached housing resources**. Its “house more with fewer beds” idea is explicitly theoretical, not a demonstrated result. Roughly half of exit destinations are unknown; conflicting totals in the document preclude a precise 100-person outcome graphic without reconciliation.

## 5. Affordable housing intake is a queue, not a move-in

**[Home Forward 2026 waitlists](https://www.homeforward.org/waitlists/)** — checked September 8, 2026.

Applications for five property waitlists are scheduled for **September 9–21, 2026**. Home Forward will randomly select enough applicants for an anticipated **three years of openings**, based partly on turnover and existing queues. Applicants are subsequently selected as units become vacant and must pass eligibility screening. Joining a waitlist does not guarantee housing or rental assistance. Medical/ADA-priority paths have separate rules.

This verifies a staged, scarce-access process. It does not establish a current waitlist total, median wait, number of available homes, or universal three-year individual wait. Avoid the outdated blanket claim that all Home Forward waitlists are closed. Do not substitute the existence of subsidized apartments or a lottery application for actual move-ins.

## Proposed diagnosis and visual treatment

These are editorial/design recommendations, not source quotations or measured effects.

1. **Show the capacity mismatch before the flowchart.** Two aligned January 2025 bars: people (split sheltered, surveyed unsheltered, presumed unsheltered) and shelter/transitional beds (split by category). Date/geography visible; pattern the presumed group. Keep current closures in a separate dated panel.
2. **Put the diagnosis at the relevant point.** Shelter: insufficient places and contracting capacity. Intake: referral restrictions, suitability, and unreported vacancies. Shelter-to-housing: subsidy/placement resources are a documented constraint. Housing: lottery, eligibility, vacancy, lease, then ongoing support. An assessment alone produces none of those resources.
3. **Use a real closed-door example.** Roseway can remain in a physical inventory while accepting no new participants. Show “120 listed rooms / closing / no new intake,” without calling the rooms vacant. It makes the distinction between a building and an available offer concrete.
4. **Show the funding bundle at the housing exit.** Available home + rent resource + move-in work + required continuing support. Mark each missing or waiting piece; do not invent quantities. Compare placement resources alongside outcomes within comparable programs before attributing performance to building type.
5. **Treat remaining capacity as a service commitment.** Publish funded → staffed/operable → occupied/reserved → accepting intake → suitable/held for this household. Unknown stages must say unknown, not zero. A timestamp and receiving program should accompany any claim of an available place.

## What still prevents a definitive “tonight” answer

- No verified, same-date countywide census of current people needing each placement type and staffed, operable, unreserved, intake-open spaces suitable for them.
- No reconciled City/HSD/Health/private inventory with person capacity versus units, overlaps, closure dates, and emergency/flex activation status.
- No current deduplicated waitlist, median/time-tail waits, or denial reasons across adult/family/youth and care requirements.
- No linked closure cohort showing every resident's destination, arrival, subsequent housing status, and unknown follow-up.
- No matched housing queue showing delays separately for subsidy, property vacancy, inspection, eligibility/documents, tenancy approval, or continuing services.

These gaps should be explicit accountability requests. They must not become a reason to hide the already documented shortfall, closures, access restrictions, and uneven housing resources.
