Amendment Clark 2: Description 
This amendment adds operations and maintenance responsibilities as part of the pre-
development functions to be considered for the financial plan. 
It also acknowledges the reality that the city owns the Keller Auditorium and adds language 
that ensures the city continues to provide investment and plan for the future of the facility 
in tandem with all planning happening at PSU. 
Accept the Future of Large-Scale Performing Arts recommendations to advance a new 
Broadway-capable venue as part of the proposed Performing Arts + Culture Center at 
Portland State University, and initiate planning for the future of the Keller Auditorium 
(Resolution) 
WHEREAS, the City of Portland recognizes the need for a comprehensive and collaborative 
approach to downtown revitalization that celebrates investments in creative placemaking 
that supports arts and cultural opportunities to restore and enhance our Central City as a 
vibrant, inclusive, and thriving urban center; and 
WHEREAS, in 2017, a private design competition was held to conceptualize what might be 
possible for a grand scale Keller renovation on site; and 
WHEREAS, the March 2020 Keller Seismic Analysis Summary Report identified that the 
Keller Auditorium was not built to withstand major earthquakes and requires significant 
structural enhancements to weather a major seismic event; and the report outlined the 
structural deficiencies of the Keller Auditorium and potential options for remedy, including 
Option 1b: limited building renovation that addresses only seismic upgrades; Option 2: a 
major renovation including new additions; and Option 3: a new facility on the existing site 
or an alternative site; and 
WHEREAS, an association of neighborhood property owners and design professionals, the 
Halprin Landscape Conservancy, became interested in continuing this work and further 
refining Option 2 with a large-scale renovation concept to see if it would be feasible; and 
WHEREAS, in a joint letter dated September 14, 2022, between the City of Portland and 
Metro, the City and Metro each committed $200,000 in funding toward the Halprin 
Landscape Conservancy while requiring an additional private match of $200,000 to further 
develop a large-scale renovation option to determine its feasibility; and 
WHEREAS, on September 27, 2023, Halprin Landscape Conservancy presented their 
concept for a renovated Keller Auditorium to Portland City Council; and

WHEREAS, in order to better understand the possibilities of replacing the theater with a 
new state-of-the-art facility on an alternate site as outlined in Option 3 of the Seismic 
Analysis, the City released a Request for Expression of Interest (RFEI) on July 12, 2023 to 
explore potential new performing arts facility sites in Portland; and 
WHEREAS, on August 11, 2023, the City received eight proposals in response to the RFEI 
and narrowed them down to five on September 25, 2023 for further discussion; and 
WHEREAS, further discussions were conducted the week of October 23, 2023 with the five 
proposers and on November 9, 2023, the top two proposals were selected for further study: 
Urban Renaissance Group at Lloyd Center and Portland State University; and 
WHEREAS, on January 31, 2024, the two proposers were each awarded $50,000 grants 
from the City's Spectator Venues Program to develop conceptual designs and test the 
feasibility of their sites for a new performing arts facility; and 
WHEREAS, on May 22, 2024, the City received an economic impact study to better 
understand the impacts of a closure of the Keller Auditorium for a potential future 
renovation which showed that the combined economic impact of a venue closure, with no 
alternative location for shows, would be profound; and 
WHEREAS, on May 28, 2024, an independent cost consultant with extensive experience in 
performing arts facilities retained by Metro, estimated the costs of the three proposals: 
Keller renovation, $290 million; Lloyd Center, $429 million and Portland State University, 
$447 million; and 
WHEREAS, on May 29, 2024, all three groups presented their renovation and new facility 
design concepts to City Council and more than 61 community members and interested 
parties signed up to testify and provide written testimony; and

engagement meetings with Metropolitan Exposition and Recreation Commissioners, arts 
organizations, Portland'5 Centers for the Arts Youth Council, Travel Portland, Portland 
Metro Chamber, Portland'5 staff, labor (IATSE Local 28), independent venues, and 
Portland'5 Community Arts and Culture Council, and a monthlong public survey to hear 
community members' values and priorities for a new or renovated premier performing arts 
facility; and 
WHEREAS, on July 8, 2024, the formal public outreach period closed and the City and its 
consultants began analyzing feedback data to create a community engagement summary 
report for City Council; and

WHEREAS, during the outreach period and at the May 29, 2024 City Council presentation, 
two key themes emerged: first, that there is a preference among users, arts groups, 
employees, labor unions, operators, regional tourism interests and Central City business 
interests to avoid any closure period that impacts the ability to hold large productions at 
Keller Auditorium; and second, that there is a high level of attachment to the current site, 
its proximity to the Keller Fountain, and concern about what would happen to the current 
site if large theatrical productions were moved to a new venue; and   
WHEREAS, after evaluating the economic impact study and receiving extensive input from 
users, arts groups, employees, operators, regional tourism interests and Central City 
business interests, it is evident that the negative impacts of a construction closure could 
be avoided if an alternative option exists that would not shutdown operations for any 
extended period of time; and 
WHEREAS, on August 14, 2024, the City Council was presented with the results from 
community engagement and the Mayor directed staff to work with the Halprin Landscape 
Conservancy and Portland State University teams to identify a solution to bring the two 
teams together to form a recommendation that best meets the needs of Portlanders and 
avoids a prolonged closure of Portland's premiere large performing arts theater; and 
WHEREAS, the teams from Halprin Landscape Conservancy and Portland State University 
met nine times over the course of five weeks in 2024, and invited subject matter experts 
from Portland'5 Centers for the Arts, the resident nonprofit arts companies and labor to 
provide input before reaching consensus on a two Broadway-capable venue strategy; and 
WHEREAS, on October 10, 2024, City Council unanimously adopted Resolution #37680 to 
accept the recommendation to pursue a strategy to develop two Broadway-capable 
venues, avoiding a prolonged closure of the Keller Auditorium, while greenlighting the path 
for a traffic study, market feasibility study to better understand the opportunity for a two-
venue strategy and the appropriate seat count for Broadway-capable venues, and 
subsequent financing strategy; and 
WHEREAS, on October 31, 2024, City Council adopted Resolution #37684 establishing a 
performing arts venues workgroup to evaluate management of the Portland'5 Centers for 
the Arts facilities; and 
WHEREAS, on June 25, 2025, the performing arts venues workgroup published final 
recommendations, including the short-term recommendations to address deferred 
maintenance and necessary building modernizations upgrades and future replacement by 
completing facility condition assessments; immediately beginning capital planning; and

Hunden Partners, a nationally recognized consultant with expertise in real estate 
development, tourism development and economic development, from July to December 
2025 that found there is insufficient market demand or comparable market support to 
justify two Broadway-capable venues in Portland, that a new venue should focus on 
maximizing Broadway content and maintaining theatre-style commercial content while 
also being an accessible home to resident companies and non-profits, and that the ideal 
seating capacity of a new venue is 3,200; and 
WHEREAS, in April 2026, the City received an economic impact study conducted by 
Hunden Partners from March to April 2025, informed by the findings from the market 
feasibility study that considered projected stabilized demand, revenue, and economic 
impact projections for a renovated Keller with an assumed 3,000-seat capacity and a new 
Portland State University venue with an assumed 3,200-seat capacity and found that a 
larger new venue at Portland State University is expected to achieve a lift of 8 percent in 
attendance, 12 percent in operating revenues, and 16 percent in economic impacts 
compared to a renovated Keller; and

WHEREAS, in April 2026, the City received a facility condition assessment for the Keller 
Auditorium that found the building no longer meets modern performance expectations, 
many building envelope and theatrical systems show extensive deterioration, and 
investments estimated at $8.5 million to $17.5 million are needed to maintain current 
service levels for the next ten years – in addition to the more significant deferred 
maintenance and seismic needs; and 
WHEREAS, in November 2025, the Deputy City Administrator for Community & Economic 
Development appointed an 11-member steering committee with technical expertise in 
architecture, construction, finance, fundraising, performing arts, public-private 
partnerships, real estate development, urban planning and venue operations, which met 
eight times between December 2025 and June 2026 to review relevant reports and studies 
related to this project; and 
WHEREAS, on June 24, 2026, the steering committee submitted its unanimous 
recommendation to build a new Broadway-capable venue at Portland State University as 
part of the proposed Performing Arts + Culture Center and, simultaneously, create an 
actionable plan to reimagine the Keller Auditorium and/or site based on Portland's historic, 
cultural, and civic values; and 
WHEREAS, in November 2025, Mayor Wilson invited key stakeholders from Portland's 
performing arts ecosystem, including members of the Portland City Council, the 
Multnomah County Board of Commissioners, and the Metro Council, to join his ex officio 
table, which met four times between January and June 2026 to review the details of the 
City's studies related to this project; and 
WHEREAS, on June 26, 2026, a majority of the Mayor's ex officio table submitted  a 
recommendation for a phased approach to build a new Broadway-capable venue at 
Portland State University; maintain Broadway programming at Keller during construction of 
the new venue to avoid economic losses and continue cultural activity; initiate planning for 
the Keller's future as a right-sized 1200 to1800-seat venue that addresses unmet market 
needs; then begin renovation of the Keller Auditorium when the Portland State University 
venue becomes operational; and 
WHEREAS, Prosper Portland has a longstanding Disposition and Development Agreement 
with Portland State University for partnership in the North Macadam Tax Increment Finance 
District, which includes $7.2 million in funding for redevelopment of the University Place 
site. 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED that this resolution with the attached Exhibits A-E is 
accepted by the City Council; and

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City Council affirm the City's commitment to a new 
City-owned approximately 3,000-seat Broadway capable venue as part of the proposed 
Performing Arts + Culture Center at Portland State University at 310/330 SW Lincoln Street, 
with a goal of opening in 2030, capitalizing on the state's funding commitment of $137.5 
million, public private partnerships, and a future forward vision that expands Portland's 
arts ecosystem; and 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Deputy City Administrator is directed to stand up a staff 
project team of technical experts with Portland State University to complete pre-
development functions, including but not limited to: develop a detailed financial plan with 
a fully defined capital stack, funding timelines, and return on invested capital (ROIC) 
and operations and maintenance responsibilities that provides a clear understanding of 
the City's obligations and costs for the project; direct a cohesive, comprehensive 
philanthropic strategy, collaborate with the Portland State University Foundation in its role 
as lead philanthropic strategist, and work together to identify and secure necessary 
funding; provide input into the initial phases of the project and project construction 
management direction; and 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Deputy City Administrator is directed to develop a 
Project Commitment Agreement with Portland State University by December 1, 2026, that 
establishes the scope, schedule, and governance framework for the project and a shared 
commitment between the City and Portland State University to develop and implement the 
full capital funding strategy; and 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Deputy City Administrator is directed to designate a 
pre-development steering committee to participate in the process as needed; and 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that while development of a new Broadway-capable venue at 
Portland State University is ongoing, programming, including Broadway shows, and City 
investment will continue at the Keller Auditorium to avoid economic losses, upgrade the 
facility, and to continue cultural activity; and 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City will identify and pursue 
funding sources, where feasible, to support implementation of an adopted plan for the 
future of Keller Auditorium, including potential funding from City Council, the State 
Legislature, Metro, the federal government, and other appropriate sources. 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Deputy City Administrator is directed to direct staff to
