# Topic sweep · District 3, candidates A–K (September 22, 2026)

Scope: the eleven District 3 candidates whose ids sort A–K (Beaudoin, Morillo, Otero, McCormick, León, Frankenstein, Pham, Corcoran, Sweeney, Legree, Torres) × the eight topic boards (Moda Center deal, new taxes or fees, police staffing, clearing camps, data centers, Street Response, street repair fee, water rates). File edited: `src/lib/voters-guide/race-sheet/content/topic-stances-d3.ts`. Checks: `npx vitest run tests/voters-guide/race-sheet.test.ts` (2,168 passed) and `npx tsc --noEmit -p .` (clean).

Housekeeping, disclosed: the D3 stance file was organised by topic while the brief and the D4 file use one alphabetical section per candidate. Before either sweep agent added entries, the 41 existing stances were regrouped into per-candidate sections (last-name order, as in `delivery-d3.ts`), with no stance text, chip, value or source changed. No existing stance was altered in this sweep.

Method, applied identically to every candidate: the Mercury District 3 questionnaire (read in full as text); the candidate's voters' pamphlet page (PDF text); every page of the candidate's campaign site, including JavaScript-rendered sections read in a browser (Legree's accordion, Pham's bundle); the 350PDX Climate Justice Voter Guide 2026 candidate page; the Rose City Reform 2026 tracker page (endorsements and pledges); the Friends of Portland Street Response 2026 pledge list; the 2024 OPB/Oregonian questionnaire and City Club 2024 survey for candidates who ran in 2024 (Otero, Pham, Sweeney, Morillo); and web searches by full name + "Portland City Council" + each topic's words. A stance was added only after the statement was read at a working URL.

Totals: 12 stances added (5 of them "partial"); 0 changed; 56 gaps remain across the eleven candidates.

Convention for 2024 material: three police-staffing entries (Otero, Pham, Sweeney) come from the September–October 2024 OPB/Oregonian questionnaire, which asked the exact choice ("Would you vote yes on a proposal to fund hundreds more police officers than the City Council has already authorized?"). The label, date and text all say 2024. The K–Z sweep uses the same convention. These are listed under "Least sure" so a reviewer can decide whether a two-year-old answer belongs on a 2026 board.

## Least sure (review these first)

1. **Otero · police staffing (opposes)**, **Pham · police staffing (supports)**, **Sweeney · police staffing (supports)**: all from the 2024 OPB/Oregonian questionnaire. The question matches the board exactly, and each text names the year, but positions can move in two years (Otero's 2024 "no further tax increases" answer differs from her 2026 platform's "progressive taxation when necessary"). Sweeney skipped the specific "hundreds more officers" question and the entry rests on his first-priority answer ("Increase and refresh the Portland Police and Portland Police Reserve").
2. **Morillo · street repair fee (partial)**: her Council page says she "improved" the proposed fee with a study and affordability protections; that reads as conditional acceptance, but the page never says keep or repeal, so it is filed as partial. Her recorded vote renders separately.
3. **Morillo · clearing camps (opposes)**: the sentence is about legislation she "prepared" to strengthen a colleague's Urban Alchemy oversight "while supporting a shift away from costly homeless camp sweeps"; the stance is explicit but sits inside a clause about a different bill.
4. **León · police staffing (partial)** and **Morillo · police staffing (partial)**: both platforms argue for unarmed responders and against a policing-first approach without saying whether sworn staffing should grow; a reviewer might prefer "opposes" for León ("taking a policing approach to public safety does not make us safer").
5. **Legree · Street Response (partial)**: "fully support and scale Portland Street Response" is expansion, but 24/7 is unsaid; the D4 file's Arnold entry treats a similar statement as "supports" with the 24/7 caveat in the text. Either reading is defensible.
6. **Beaudoin · Moda (mixed)**: a two-sentence questionnaire quote, critical of the "packages to a billionaire" but open to "deals where citizens and the communities really benefit"; "opposes" would also be defensible.
7. **Corcoran · Moda (mixed)**: the Values page sentence is conditional on the owners' commitment; his Moda page adds that the full contracts should be public first. The entry cites only the Values page.
8. Not added, flagged for the K–Z agent: the 350PDX page https://350pdx.org/cristal-otero-2026/ shows Tom Sollitt's statement, which says of "a data center or a facility like Zenith" that there should be no secret deals or NDAs and the public should understand energy and water use; a possible data-centers partial for Sollitt if his own page carries the same text.
