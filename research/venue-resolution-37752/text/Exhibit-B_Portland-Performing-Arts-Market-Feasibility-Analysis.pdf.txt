The City of Portland engaged the Hunden Partners team to complete a market feasibility analysis to determine whether the regional 
cultural and entertainment ecosystem can support two Broadway-capable performing arts venues located in downtown Portland, 
Oregon (Project): one at the current Keller Auditorium (Keller) site and another at a proposed site at Portland State University (PSU).
The Keller, the city’s only venue capable of hosting large-scale touring productions, faces urgent capital improvements estimated at 
$290 million. Its closure could prevent performances from Broadway Across America (BAA) for years and cause local arts 
organizations to face significant challenges without a large-scale performing venue. Portland State University’s proposed facility 
would be headlined by a 3,000-seat venue, capable of large-scale performances, but could hinder the long-term feasibility of the 
Portland’5 venues, particularly as two new live entertainment facilities from AEG and Live Nation are to be opened in the coming 
years.
Beyond Hunden’s market analysis, our approach is distinguished by the inclusion of OTJ Architects, a premier architecture and design 
practice renowned for its specialization in performing arts centers and industry insight provided by Mike Isaacson, multi–Tony Award 
winning Artistic Director & Executive Producer of The Muny and 15-year manager of the Broadway series for the Fox Theatre in St. 
Louis, Missouri.

Key Questions
1.Is there sufficient demand for two Broadway-capable 
venues operating concurrently in Portland?
2.What are the optimal user types, event types, and 
programming strategies?
3.What are the ideal seating capacities and technical 
capabilities to meet current and future market needs?
This market feasibility study focuses on answering the key 
question of whether market support exists for two Broadway-
capable venues in Portland. Based on Hunden’s analysis, a 
recommendation for optimal venue capacity and programming 
will provide a basis for projected future demand.

Keller Auditorium
Overview
Keller Auditorium is part of Portland’5, the 5th largest 
performing arts center in the U.S., with more than 700 
events hosted annually across its five venues.
A 2020 seismic analysis of the Keller Auditorium revealed 
significant vulnerabilities that need to be addressed via 
renovation. Additionally, the building’s outdated 
capabilities for visitors and performers has led to pushes 
for a complete redesign of the venue’s infrastructure.
Project Description
The City of Portland has not put forth a proposal for a full 
renovation of the Keller. However, the Halprin Landscape 
Conservancy, with the support of Metro, engaged 
Hennebery Eddy Architects to create a vision for what a 
full Keller renovation could entail. The conceptual 
redesign includes a new façade extended over SW 3rd 
Avenue and has a target seat count of 2,700.
Total Costs
• Seismic deficiencies only: $119 million (est.)
• Full renovation: ~$290 million (est.)

Portland State University
Overview
Portland State University (PSU) is working toward a new 
performing arts center and conference hotel on campus. 
The facility is intended to serve as a multi-use arts and 
culture complex that can spark development in the 
downtown core.
Project Description
The proposed complex would be situated on a 4.25-acre 
site on SW Lincoln Street in downtown Portland, six 
blocks from the Keller. It would include a 3,000-seat 
auditorium to accommodate large-scale performances 
including Broadway, a 1,200-seat auditorium, a 150-room 
hotel, up to 18,000 square feet of conference space, 
educational space, up to 740 on-site parking spaces, a 
full-service restaurant and bar, and up to 40,000 square 
feet of office space for nonprofits.
Total Costs
•1,200-seat venue: $115 million (est.)
•3,000-seat venue: $350 million – $425 million (est.)
•Total all-in costs are estimated at $650 million for both 
performing venues, parking, hotel, and conference 
center

Key Engagement & Feedback Themes
An extended closure of Keller Auditorium would impair local arts 
organizations and push away Broadway Across America for years
Without a large performing venue, arts organizations would lose essential revenue that would cause 
them to require expensive subsidies to continue operation. Additionally, BAA would likely not perform 
in the market for as long as five years in order to accommodate lead time needed for scheduling.
Portland’s existing large-scale performing spaces are severely lacking 
modern capabilities
Both Keller Auditorium and Arlene Schnitzer Concert Hall are limited by outdated design, particularly 
the Keller. Common issues include inadequate backstage functionality, limited loading capacity, poor 
acoustics and sightlines, Americans with Disabilities Act non-compliance, and lack of premium spaces 
and amenities.
A surplus of available venue space could cause widespread financial 
shortcoming at local venues
If multiple large-scale venues operate concurrently, they will each attract shows from other venues in the 
market. Along with the new AEG and Live Nation venues in development, the high supply of 
entertainment space could cause financial shortcomings without an equivalent increase in demand.

Broadway Across America
Hunden held numerous interviews with leadership at Broadway Across America, the presenter of Broadway at Keller Auditorium. Key 
takeaways from those conversations are outlined below.
Key Takeaways:
§Keller Auditorium is fully functional as a venue for touring Broadway, however it could benefit from improvements with accessibility, it has 
a very wide auditorium that creates many limited view seats, and it features outdated amenities, lowering customer experience. 
§A multi-year closure of Keller risks eroding Portland’s Broadway audience and forcing skilled labor to relocate from Portland to other cities 
where they can find employment.
§Private competition from new commercial venues could also siphon concerts and events from the Keller, which creates an additional 
commercial demand reduction if there were to be two large theater-style venues.
§Portland serves as a strong one-week market between San Francisco and Seattle. Portland is not able to sustain two-week runs 
consistently. Slower population growth limits long-term expansion potential.
§Doubling the Broadway-type of venue inventory risks diluted demand across venues and creates weaker weekday attendance. Broadway 
producers prefer markets that operate at a high percentage sold over markets that have multiple weeks at a low percentage sold. It is too 
expensive for the shows to play in half-filled theaters.  
§Other markets are able to index a higher number of Broadway shows due to population density.
§New venues in other markets have resulted in sustained audience and subscription growth, higher customer satisfaction, and improved 
production capability.
§BAA does not recommend developing two Broadway-capable venues in Portland. Rather, maxing out Broadway potential at a single larger 
capacity venue of 3,200–3,400 with a one-week subscription would maximize returns for the venue.

Audience Survey Key Takeaways 
Audience is Predominantly from Oregon and Older
The survey shows that nearly all respondents come from Oregon, with only a small share traveling from 
Washington or elsewhere. The audience also leans significantly older, as more than seventy percent of 
respondents are aged fifty or above, highlighting a mature and highly engaged core base for the region’s 
performing arts.

Key Takeaways:
Safety and Cost Are Major Attendance Barriers
Respondents consistently emphasized that concerns about downtown safety and the rising cost of tickets 
and fees are the two primary factors limiting how often they attend events. Many also noted that high 
concession prices contribute to the overall affordability challenge. Improving safety conditions and 
reducing total costs would meaningfully increase their willingness to participate more frequently.
Keller Facility Issues Are a Consistent Pain Point
Across the survey, respondents highlighted poor sound quality, outdated and uncomfortable seating, 
limited restrooms, steep aisles, and crowded lobbies as major detractors from the experience. Steep ramps 
and a lack of handrails were also noted as mobility and safety concerns. These issues were mentioned 
repeatedly, reinforcing a strong desire for modernization or significant upgrades.
Demand Exists for Broader and Higher-Quality Programming
Respondents showed strong interest in a wider range of performances, including more Broadway tours, 
plays, concerts, comedy, dance, cultural programming, and film-with-orchestra events. There is also clear 
demand for more intimate and mid-size venues, signaling an appetite for expanded and more diverse 
programming beyond current offerings.
Hunden conducted a survey capturing the attendees of Portland’s performing arts venues, gathering more than 5,000 responses to understand audience demographics, 
attendance patterns, interests, and the key factors that influence how often people engage with performances downtown.

Hunden conducted a survey targeting performing arts venue users in Portland to understand current utilization patterns, unmet needs, and barriers to growth. It should 
be noted that the results reflect a relatively small sample size, composed of primarily local non-profit organizations. The feedback highlights a desire for a mid-size, flexible 
venue rather than another large-scale facility, and shows that venue costs, date availability, technical capabilities, and audience experience and amenities are the primary 
factors limiting use of both the Keller and the Schnitz. The survey also indicates that even if these limitations were removed, most organizations would add only one to 
two extra days, underscoring that the core challenge is not demand for more large-venue days but the need for appropriately scaled, affordable, and adaptable spaces.
User Survey Key Takeaways 
Need for Mid-Sized, Flexible Venues or One Modern Large-Scale Facility
Responses consistently point to gaps in 300 to 600, 500 to 1,000, and 1,200 to 1,400 seat venues, with 
organizations seeking spaces that better match audience demand and production needs. A flexible mid-size 
venue would resonate more strongly with respondents than another large-scale facility, and if a large 
venue is pursued, users indicate there should only be one state-of-the-art facility that can meet presenter 
and audience needs for the next several decades.

Key Takeaways:
Affordability and Cost Pressures
Users reported that rising rent, labor, security, and parking costs are major barriers to access. These cost 
pressures limit utilization and disproportionately impact local nonprofits, dance companies, and community 
organizations.
Utilization Limiting Factors
The top limiting factors at both the Keller and the Schnitz were venue costs, technical capabilities, date 
availability, and audience experience and amenities, with venue costs being the most significant. Even if 
these issues were resolved, most respondents would add only one or two additional days, with just a few 
indicating slightly higher increases and none showing demand significantly beyond that.
Importance of Broader Support Systems
Users emphasized the value of additional city financial support, cooperative marketing, and a stronger 
downtown environment with improved safety, amenities, and audience appeal, noting that these factors 
are just as important to long-term market viability as the venues themselves.

comparison to Portland and Keller Auditorium.

Implications
Hunden does not recommend that Portland develop two Broadway-capable venues due to the following:
§Multi-venue markets with similar designated market area (DMA) populations do not perform at a level that supports the capital investment 
required at a redeveloped Keller and new PSU venue.
§Average performance of individual venues in multi-venue, single-week subscription markets is lower than where the Keller performs 
currently. Though overall Broadway demand may increase, this increase is shared among multiple venues, decreasing average performance 
for individual venues.
§Other single venue markets show these venues have the capacity to increase Broadway shows at a single venue. Utilization data shows 
that the Keller or a new PSU theater have the ability to host additional Broadway content without a second venue development, should 
demand increase.
§The supply of current Broadway content at the Keller is lower than comparable markets. The seating capacity at either Portland venue 
outweighs the metric of Broadway seats to designated market area (DMA) population in comparable markets, showing sufficient current 
supply to accommodate capturable demand.
§The proposed new AEG/Monqui Presents and Live Nation venues will capture unaccommodated and new commercial demand in Portland 
and potentially pull from existing commercial content at the Keller. There is not a need for an additional venue for hosting commercial 
content that can offset any sharing of Broadway productions between venues.

Case Study Implications
Primary venues anchor Broadway activity
Across two-venue markets, Broadway activity largely centers around a single primary theater 
such as the Fox in St. Louis, the Ohio Theatre in Columbus, and the Murat Theatre in 
Indianapolis, while secondary venues typically host smaller Broadway productions or limited 
run tours, underscoring that a second venue rarely expands overall Broadway capacity.
Per venue analysis highlights market inefficiencies
On a per venue basis, only multiple subscription week cities Cleveland and St. Louis exceed 
Portland in total Broadway shows and attendance, while the other two venue markets fall 
behind, illustrating the efficiency and strength of Portland’s single venue model in sustaining 
strong demand and maximizing performance output.
Sustained operations depend on institutional support
Demonstrated by markets like Cleveland and Columbus, major annual public and private 
contributions, often between 10 and 20 million, are vital for sustainability, with Cleveland 
supported by the Playhouse Square District Development Corporation and tax credits for tech 
rehearsals, showing that civic and philanthropic investment, not venue count, drives stability.

A new trend in entertainment venue development is temporary and semi-
permanent venues. These purpose-built low-cost venues are designed to be 
deconstructable and be operable for up to 15 or more years. Examples include:

Temporary Venues – Advantages & Disadvantages for Portland
ADVANTAGES
§ Can cost as low as 5-10% of the cost of a traditional large-
scale Broadway capable venue.
§ Broadway content could continue during a Keller renovation, 
mitigating loss of revenue, subscription base, and workforce.
§ These venues are attractive, amenity-rich concepts (onsite 
restaurants and premium areas), generating additional 
revenue streams for the venue and additional economic 
impact to the city.
§ These unique venues are often located in recently 
developed or up and coming mixed-use areas – an 
opportunity to highlight a focus area in Portland, if not in 
same neighborhood as Keller.
DISADVANTAGES
§ A city-controlled site would need to be identified to 
accommodate the venue and associated parking demand.
§ A temporary venue dedicated to large-scale rotating 
Broadway content has not been proven, although several are 
in discussion across the U.S.
§ Constructing and dismantling a temporary venue for a short 
period (two years) can create notable environmental impacts 
due to material use, transportation, and limited reuse 
potential.
§ Cost recovery is difficult to achieve for a venue with a 
limited lifespan, given the significant upfront investment 
needed to build, operate, and remove the facility.
While a semi-permanent venue does offer some advantages, 
such a venue being available during a Keller renovation has an 
end result of having a Keller that still does not have optimal 
seating capacity, attendee amenities, and production support.

1. Is there sufficient demand for two Broadway-capable venues 
operating concurrently in Portland? There is insufficient market

an accessible home to resident companies and non-profits.

meet current and future market needs? The ideal seating

high occupancy at events. The venue should include amenities

P’5 Venues Performance
Hunden also analyzed the financial performance of P’5 venues to determine the impact of Broadway on profitability. While P’5 does not 
allocate revenues and expenses by venue, they have approximated individual venues’ performance by allocating portions of revenues and 
expenses to each. These allocations are based on venue attendance, seating capacity, ticket sales, and number of events. Revenues and 
expenses do not include capital improvement costs, government support, or miscellaneous revenue.
Keller Auditorium was the only profitable venue during fiscal year 2023–2024 with a $1.2-million profit. The Schnitz and Hatfield Hall 
combined generated a $5-million loss that fiscal year. Keller Auditorium’s profitability is due in large part to the presence of Broadway Across 
America, which hosted 76 performances during its 2023–2024 season and 104 performances during its 2024–2025 season. This 
profitability subsidizes losses from the operations at other P’5 venues. A closure of Keller and loss of this revenue would likely lead to 
significant financial shortcomings across all venues that would need to be subsidized.

Historical Performance Implications
Visitation
P’5, including Keller Auditorium, is driven by more localized visitation. Other comparable Broadway-capable 
venues generate a higher share of visitation from more than 25 miles away. A new or renovated venue could 
help drive increased long-distance visitation, increasing spending and impacts to the city. Utilization data shows 
room to grow at the Keller, which would also aid in increasing visitation with additional events hosted.
Events & Attendance
P’5’s events have yet to recover to pre-pandemic levels, primarily due to a decrease in resident company 
and student events. While attendance has remained relatively stable at Keller Auditorium, fiscal year 2024 
attendance at other P’5 venues is approximately 24-percent lower than fiscal year 2019. Increased 
programming at a renovated Keller or new PSU theater could help offset these losses in events and demand.
Financial Performance
Keller Auditorium is the only profitable facility among P’5 venues. These profits help to offset losses 
experienced at other venues. A temporary closure of the Keller would require additional subsidies to fund 
operations at both the Schnitz and venues at Antoinette Hatfield Hall.

Local Stakeholders Feedback
Hunden engaged with several local stakeholders through in-person and virtual meetings, such as city officials, Portland State University, 
Halprin Landscape Conservancy, Downtown Neighborhood Association, Pioneer Courthouse Square, and Portland Clean & Safe, among 
others. A summary of key takeaways from these conversations is included below.
Key Takeaways:
•The strength of Keller Auditorium is its location in downtown Portland and accessibility to parking, restaurants, and other amenities. Its 
challenge is its age and outdated capabilities for modern shows. A remodel should include street-facing retail to further activate the area.
•The possibility of two Broadway-capable theaters is unlikely to work. A new Broadway house could get all the shows rather than a split 
occurring between two venues. The market is unlikely to accommodate enough other Broadway-esque, large commercial, or performing 
arts shows for two large venues.
•There is interest for Keller to evolve into an alternative capacity, such as 1,800 seats, that differentiates itself and supports arts groups,.
•The coming Live Nation and AEG venues will likely pull events from P’5 venues. Talent agencies will often choose to go to newer, more 
modern, and technologically advanced facilities.
•Increased availability at large-scale theaters is not helpful for most arts groups as rental costs are too expensive at that capacity.
•Larger shows cannot use the Schnitz due to inadequate load-in/out capabilities and limited stage depth, so they need to use Keller.
•For PSU, Portland is an emerging center of gravity for performing arts. A new performing arts center could attract new programming and 
offer opportunities to train performing arts professionals. The proposed facility can provide neighborhood activation, placemaking, and 
catalyze development. A potential ecosystem could emerge, with both the proposed center and Keller Auditorium.
•If Keller is closed without a comparable alternative, the loss would push Broadway out of the market for 4–5 years due to scheduling.

Promoters Feedback
Hunden engaged with several local promoters through in-person and virtual meetings, such as Live Nation, Monqui Presents, and True

Key Takeaways:
•It is difficult to get available dates at Keller or Schnitz. Promoters prefer to use Schnitz because its historical feel makes it easier to sell 
tickets. Promoters would use Schnitz if there was more date availability.
•The market needs a new venue with capacities of between 1,500 and 2,500 to accommodate large shows.
•P’5 venues are very expensive due to high labor costs and show load-in/out.
•New AEG and Live Nation venues will host EDM, rock & roll, comedy, and other live entertainment. The venues will mostly attract new

•Due to lack of availability at large venues, the Portland entertainment market has been losing 25–50 commercial shows annually.
•Keller Auditorium and Schnitz Concert Hall have significant physical limitations for performers, including aging dressing rooms/showers, 
inadequate truck bays, limited backstage experience, and lack of three-phase power.

Broadway Across America
Hunden held numerous interviews with leadership at Broadway Across America, the presenter of Broadway at Keller Auditorium. Key 
takeaways from those conversations are outlined below.
Key Takeaways:
§Keller Auditorium is fully functional as a venue for touring Broadway, however it could benefit from improvements with accessibility, it has 
a very wide auditorium that creates many limited view seats, and it features outdated amenities, lowering customer experience. 
§A multi-year closure of Keller risks eroding Portland’s Broadway audience and forcing skilled labor to relocate from Portland to other cities 
where they can find employment.
§Private competition from new commercial venues could also siphon concerts and events from the Keller, which creates an additional 
commercial demand reduction if there were to be two large theater-style venues.
§Portland serves as a strong one-week market between San Francisco and Seattle. Portland is not able to sustain two-week runs 
consistently. Slower population growth limits long-term expansion potential.
§Doubling the Broadway-type of venue inventory risks diluted demand across venues and creates weaker weekday attendance. Broadway 
producers prefer markets that operate at a high percentage sold over markets that have multiple weeks at a low percentage sold. It is too 
expensive for the shows to play in half-filled theaters.  
§Other markets are able to index a higher number of Broadway shows due to population density.
§New venues in other markets have resulted in sustained audience and subscription growth, higher customer satisfaction, and improved 
production capability.
§BAA does not recommend developing two Broadway-capable venues in Portland. Rather, maxing out Broadway potential at a single larger 
capacity venue of 3,200–3,400 with a one-week subscription would maximize returns for the venue.

Users & Arts Groups Feedback
Hunden engaged with several local arts groups and venue users through in-person and virtual meetings, such as Portland Opera, 
Broadway Across America, Oregon Symphony, Oregon Ballet Theatre, Whitebird, Portland Youth Philharmonic, Metropolitan Youth 
Symphony, Literary Arts, Portland Gay Men’s Chorus, and Oregon Children’s Theatre, among others. A summary of key takeaways from 
these conversations is included below.
Key Takeaways:
•Current logistical challenges at The Keller include its loading dock, storage space, depth from back wall to front proscenium, width, 
orchestra pit, acoustics, dressing rooms, front-of-house spaces, restrooms, lobby space, theater rake, and sight lines. The current Keller 
could be difficult to renovate to modern standards.
•Cost headwinds, including labor costs, have led to fewer P’5 events. Some arts organizations have had to give their dates back to P’5 as 
they reconsider their organization’s feasibility to exist. Additionally, organizations don’t have the opportunity to gain revenue from 
premium experiences, concessions, or other ancillary revenues.
•A two-year closure of Keller Auditorium would impair local arts organizations that would lack a large performing venue from which to gain 
essential revenue. To subsidize these organizations throughout a closure period would cost as much as $12 million individually for certain 
organizations during this period.
•There is an insufficient local and regional audience to support three venues with capacity of more than 2,000.
•A smaller, renovated Keller would encourage groups to do more shows downtown. An ideal capacity would be 1,200 to 2,200.

national growth during this period, reflecting the region’s historic ability to attract new 
residents and sustain steady expansion.
Looking ahead, however, projections show much lower population gains, with expected 
increases of approximately 1.4 to 1.5 percent for Portland and Multnomah County

Economic, Demographic, and Tourism Implications
Market Scale & Audience Potential
The area’s population and income levels provide a stable foundation for entertainment demand but reflect a 
relatively modest market size compared to other major Broadway tour stops. This means future venue  
planning must balance ambition with the city’s capacity to consistently fill large venues.
Regional Connectivity & Competition
The city’s central position on I-5 and proximity to Seattle supports routing efficiency for touring productions, 
yet it also underscores the competitive challenge of attracting shows that might otherwise prioritize larger 
nearby markets. Portland must position itself as complementary rather than duplicative in the regional 
touring circuit.
Corporate & Economic Presence
Anchored by Nike, OHSU, and Adidas, Portland’s strong corporate base supports a professional workforce 
and offers opportunities for group sales, sponsorship, and philanthropic backing, which are critical to 
supporting performing arts venues.

Venue Supply Implications
Need for additional investment
Keller Auditorium is in major need of a renovation to address not only structural issues 
and deferred maintenance, but to also provide an enhanced customer and production 
experience. Alternatively, this capital cost could be invested in a new venue at PSU.
Opportunity to drive non-local visitation
Keller Auditorium currently attracts 21 percent of its visits from more than 25 miles 
away. A significant renovation or new venue could encourage more regional and long-
distance visitors to attend shows, increasing its impact to downtown Portland.
Robust and quickly growing entertainment supply 
Downtown Portland has a robust supply of theaters, and its live entertainment ecosystem 
will become significantly more crowded in 2027, when two commercial venues with more 
than 3,500 capacity are expected to open. A new 1,200-seat theater at PSU could also fill 
a market gap.

Hunden conducted a survey targeting performing arts venue users in Portland to understand current utilization patterns, unmet needs, and barriers to growth. It should 
be noted that the results reflect a relatively small sample size, composed of primarily local non-profit organizations. The feedback highlights a desire for a mid-size, flexible 
venue rather than another large-scale facility, and shows that venue costs, date availability, technical capabilities, and audience experience and amenities are the primary 
factors limiting use of both the Keller and the Schnitz. The survey also indicates that even if these limitations were removed, most organizations would add only one to 
two extra days, underscoring that the core challenge is not demand for more large-venue days but the need for appropriately scaled, affordable, and adaptable spaces.
User Survey Key Takeaways 
Need for Mid-Sized, Flexible Venues or One Modern Large-Scale Facility
Responses consistently point to gaps in 300 to 600, 500 to 1,000, and 1,200 to 1,400 seat venues, with 
organizations seeking spaces that better match audience demand and production needs. A flexible mid-size 
venue would resonate more strongly with respondents than another large-scale facility, and if a large 
venue is pursued, users indicate there should only be one state-of-the-art facility that can meet presenter 
and audience needs for the next several decades.

Key Takeaways:
Affordability and Cost Pressures
Users reported that rising rent, labor, security, and parking costs are major barriers to access. These cost 
pressures limit utilization and disproportionately impact local nonprofits, dance companies, and community 
organizations.
Utilization Limiting Factors
The top limiting factors at both the Keller and the Schnitz were venue costs, technical capabilities, date 
availability, and audience experience and amenities, with venue costs being the most significant. Even if 
these issues were resolved, most respondents would add only one or two additional days, with just a few 
indicating slightly higher increases and none showing demand significantly beyond that.
Importance of Broader Support Systems
Users emphasized the value of additional city financial support, cooperative marketing, and a stronger 
downtown environment with improved safety, amenities, and audience appeal, noting that these factors 
are just as important to long-term market viability as the venues themselves.

Venue Trends
Modern performing arts facilities are evolving to meet the demands of a rapidly changing cultural and technological landscape. Today’s venues 
prioritize adaptability, digital connectivity, and immersive experiences while embracing sustainability and fostering community engagement. The 
following design goals reflect these priorities and guide the development of next-generation performance spaces: 
§Flexibility & hybridization: Adaptable seating, tunable acoustics, multipurpose halls;
§Digital integration: Streaming, captioning, remote participation;
§Immersive stagecraft: Intelligent rigging, projection, AR/VR;
§Sustainability: Electrification, low-carbon materials, seismic resilience;
§Community activation: Mixed-use programming to diversify revenue and broaden access. 
Renovation or New Build
A modern performing arts facility must deliver an exceptional audience experience through flexible seating, clear sightlines, and world-class 
acoustics. It requires robust technical infrastructure—including advanced rigging, high-capacity power, touring-standard lighting and audio, and 
a digital backbone for streaming—supported by comprehensive backstage amenities and efficient operations. Sustainability and urban 
integration round out the design, ensuring energy efficiency, resilience, and strong community connectivity. Whether the Keller Auditorium is 
renovated, or the City supports a new build per PSU’s proposed project plan, the following objectives should be included in the design and 
construction.

Keller Assessment
The Keller Auditorium has significant functional limitations, including 
outdated rigging, an undersized orchestra pit, poor acoustics, limited 
ADA seating, and inadequate loading facilities. A full renovation would 
require a 19–28-month closure, jeopardizing Broadway contracts or 
Broadway coming to the Portland area altogether. Seismic retrofitting, 
which is both costly and disruptive, is a complicating factor. Backstage 
constraints at the facility, such as restricted wing space and limited 
loading truck access, fall short of modern standards. Integrating 
advanced systems into the historic structure poses challenging tradeoffs. 
These factors underscore the complexity and risk of pursuing a 
comprehensive upgrade.
§ Seismic retrofit complexity: Costly, disruptive, and difficult to 
achieve without closure.
§ Backstage geometry: Limited wing space and truck access compared 
to modern standards
§ Site constraints: the site, which is a single city block, is prohibitive to 
incorporating larger lobbies, rehearsal spaces, restrooms, patron 
amenities, and loading bays. 
§ Systems modernization: Integrating advanced tech within historic 
fabric risks tradeoffs.
§ Program expansion limits: Site constraints hinder rehearsal, 
education, and mixed-use growth.

PSU Venue
The PSU project program and design includes a 3,000-seat hall, 
1,200-seat secondary venue, six loading bays, LEED Gold 
sustainability, integrated hotel and conference center, and robust 
support spaces. There would be no closure impact on Keller during 
construction.
PSU Proposed Capabilities
§ Broadway-capable theater with modern technical infrastructure.
§ Expanded program including rehearsal studios, classrooms, 
community theater, and mixed-use adjacencies.
§ Large site scale enabling underground parking, hotel, and 
conference facilities.
§ Continuity plan ensures Keller remains operational during 
construction.
Keller Auditorium remains vital to City of Portland’s arts ecosystem 
but is structurally limited due to being seismically at-risk. The 
proposed PSU venue is positioned to meet modern requirements, 
align with industry trends, and ensure Portland’s Broadway pipeline 
continues uninterrupted. Strategic planning should balance Keller’s 
near-term operations with PSU’s long-term vision.

ADVANTAGES
§ Can cost as low as 5-10% of the cost of a traditional large-

mitigating loss of revenue, subscription base, and workforce.

developed or up and coming mixed-use areas – an 
opportunity to highlight a focus area in Portland, if not in

DISADVANTAGES

accommodate the venue and associated parking demand.

potential.

needed to build, operate, and remove the facility.

end result of having a Keller that still does not have optimal 
seating capacity, attendee amenities, and production support.
Rather, development of a large-scale Broadway-capable, 
modern venue at PSU is the ideal result to optimize the market.

Broadway Subscriber Trends
Broadway subscription trends have fluctuated over the past decade, 
influenced by major touring titles, economic conditions, and shifts in 
audience behavior. While many markets have seen declines in long-term 
subscribers, the introduction of blockbuster shows continues to drive 
strong growth, often creating lasting audience gains.
Impact of Blockbusters
High-profile productions such as Hamilton and Wicked 
consistently drive significant surges in new subscriptions. In 
Portland, the announcement tease for Hamilton during the 2016–

substantial increase in subscriptions during the rollout year when 
the production premiered.

patrons who help maintain a stable subscriber base.

Case Study Implications
Primary venues anchor Broadway activity
Broadway activity largely centers around a single primary theater such as the Fox in St. Louis, 
the Ohio Theatre in Columbus, and the Murat Theatre in Indianapolis, while secondary 
venues typically host smaller Broadway productions or limited run tours, underscoring that a 
second venue rarely expands overall Broadway capacity.
Per venue analysis highlights market inefficiencies
On a per venue basis, only the multiple subscription week cities, Cleveland and St. Louis, 
exceed Portland in total Broadway shows and attendance. The other two venue markets that 
Hunden examined have fallen behind, illustrating the efficiency and strength of Portland’s 
single-venue model in sustaining strong demand and maximizing performance output.
Sustained operations depend on institutional support
In markets like Cleveland and Columbus, major annual public and private contributions, often 
between $10 million and $20 million, are vital for sustainability. Cleveland’s support via the 
Playhouse Square District Development Corporation and tax credits for tech rehearsals, shows 
that civic and philanthropic investment, not venue count, drive stability.
