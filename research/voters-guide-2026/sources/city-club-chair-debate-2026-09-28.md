## Positions stated (paraphrased; quotes as transcribed)

### Julia Brim-Edwards
- City-county agreement: "at the outset of the IGA ... be very clear about our goals, our joint goals," then each government's role and aligned budgets; homelessness and public safety both need the city and county.
- Budget: start with core services (homelessness, mental health, addiction treatment, public safety), protect them against federal and state cuts, then cut duplication and layers of administration.
- Office return: "a no plus with an explanation ... it depends on what their job is"; thousands of county workers never left their work sites; the county helps downtown most with mental-health, addiction and homeless services and public safety.
- Deflection: "you can't mandate treatment, but we can make it accessible"; with Singleton and the DA changed the model so participants must engage with treatment. Sobering center: built a plan, won state funding, site identified, under construction with sobering and withdrawal-management beds.
- Homeless services: day one, a "central command" and a leader integrating homeless, mental-health, health-care and addiction services; better data, real goals, diverse shelter types, fund the providers with the best outcomes.
- Camping: no; opposed handing out tents and tarps; with Meieran funded more shelter types including the city's pod villages.
- Preschool for All: "I would not turn it over to the state"; "we need to keep the promise"; believes in universal preschool; the rollout was "seriously flawed"; wants better data so no seats sit empty.
- Moda: supports a county investment; the chair "overcommitted"; she and Commissioner Moyer offered 11 amendments (no General Fund, a county revenue stream, labor and community benefits agreements, clawback); money likely from the motor vehicle rental tax, "right-sized" to the county's benefit. Wants to keep the Blazers.

### Sharon Meieran
- Budget: the county rolls over last year's budget; wants a zero-based budget that ties spending to results.
- Office return: "It's not a yes or no question"; only "if that actually is needed and that's demonstrated"; the county's rollout "was horrible and didn't involve the people impacted."
- Deflection: "a disaster"; argued against funding it from the start.
- Homeless services: consortium model for nonprofits; contracts tied to results; reorganize the five departments that touch homelessness into one hub (fixmultco.com).
- Camping: no; wrote a county plan for an array of shelter types; secured $3 million for alternative shelters.
- Preschool for All: rolled out in a "fatally flawed" way; pay only for filled seats; track children's results; "I don't think this necessarily should have been at the county to begin with ... but it is the will of the voters, so we need to do it right."
- Getting people off the street: not jail for addiction alone; for someone committing a crime, a choice of jail with treatment then housing, or treatment then housing.
- Moda: "Absolutely support contributing, but we have to do it responsibly and accountably. And that was not done"; does not know where the $101 million will come from.

### Shannon Singleton
- City-county agreement: end the existing IGA with Portland and create a multi-city compact including East County cities; cities manage public space and build affordable housing, the county runs homeless services.
- Budget: will not use one-time-only money for ongoing programs such as shelters; build the budget through a racial justice council of the people most affected, around the social determinants of health.
- Office return: "No, I don't support it"; the rule ignored workers; some work needs privacy and mobility; not enough space; frontline workers are not responsible for downtown revitalization.
- Treatment and deflection: requiring treatment for a shelter bed does not work; deflection is "not yet working"; people who ask for treatment should get a shelter bed attached to deflection that night instead of a referral list.
- Homeless services: case staffing so a person has one case manager and one case plan across departments (80%+ of pretrial clients also touched health and homeless services).
- Camping: does not support camping or criminalizing homelessness; organized camps such as Right 2 Dream Too or Dignity Village are options.
- Preschool for All: "implement the will of the voters, which is free universal preschool"; set the true cost of care, real inclusion support, teachers in the advisory structure; against expelling 3- and 4-year-olds.
- Moda: amendments for Albina Vision Trust development rights, a community benefits agreement, solar offset for neighbors, local hiring; proposed the motor vehicle rental tax; "not a blank check."
