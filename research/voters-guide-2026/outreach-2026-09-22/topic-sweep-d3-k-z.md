## Least sure (review these first)

1. Seven of the sixteen entries rest on 2024-race questionnaires (Hilton ×2 OPB; Landgraver ×2 City Club; Parker ×2 OPB, ×1 City Club; Koyama Lane ×1 OPB). Each text says "in 2024" and the evidence date names the 2024 race. They are the only own-words venues those candidates have on the topics. If the guide wants only 2026 statements, drop these and keep the gaps.
2. Terry Parker × street-fee (partial, pamphlet): Policy 8.28 is a cost-sharing principle for transportation, not a statement on the utility fee. The chip could read as support for a user fee; he is arguing that drivers overpay for non-driving infrastructure.
3. Martin Ward × camp-removal (partial, budget-cuts page): the sentence is about responding to "reports of homeless" ("for now we will just have the homeless return to their camp or a shelter depending on what they prefer"); it is one sentence inside a page about cutting mental-health and outreach budgets.
4. Kenneth Landgraver × moda (partial, 350PDX): "Police are nice; Police are necessary; Moda center is nice; well it is nice but neither are within the mandate of the PCEF" is loosely written; the reading that PCEF should not fund the Moda Center is the plain one.
5. Tom Sollitt × moda (partial, Bluesky): a social post, and "direct say" may mean a public vote; he does not say so.
6. Steve Novick × street-fee (Reporting): the quote is from a written statement the Mercury printed a week before the final vote; the Yahoo-syndicated KGW piece has a second quote ("vitally needed for the past 40 years") that was not cited because KGW's own page refused our fetch.
7. Kimberly Tucker × police-staffing marked "supports": her sentence is "While we do need additional armed officers, we also need additional specialists"; a reviewer might prefer "mixed".
8. Patrick Hilton × police-staffing marked "mixed": "use police sparingly" and "more police and budget can make that possible" sit in one 50-word answer.
