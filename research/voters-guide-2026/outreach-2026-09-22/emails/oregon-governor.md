# Governor of Oregon: follow-up emails (drafted September 22, 2026; nothing sent)

Race page: https://www.portlandciviclab.org/voters-guide/oregon-governor · boards: https://www.portlandciviclab.org/voters-guide/oregon-governor#topics

## Brett Smith

Send via: email contact@brett-smith.us

```text
Subject: Portland Civic Lab voters guide: Brett Smith, Governor of Oregon

Hi Brett,

I run Portland Civic Lab, a nonpartisan, volunteer research project publishing a voters guide for every race on the November 3 ballot. Your page is at https://www.portlandciviclab.org/voters-guide/oregon-governor/brett-smith. It is built only from your published words (your filed statement, your site and public statements) and the public record, the same way for every candidate, with a source on every line. It carries no endorsements, rankings or scores. The race page also has a board for each live choice the next governor faces, with every candidate’s statement or a gap: https://www.portlandciviclab.org/voters-guide/oregon-governor#topics.

Right now the page shows 2 of 10 choices on record for you. A gap on a page is a research gap, not a position, and it says so, but readers do compare. The questions below are the gaps. A sentence or two on any of them is enough; we quote or paraphrase with a date and attribute it to your campaign, and we never fill a gap from a general goal.

The four issues every race page tracks
1. Housing and homelessness. How would you get more homes built and more people housed?
2. Crime, drugs and who responds. Your page shows “Civil Defense Force.” We could not find what number, deadline or visible result would show it worked.
3. Roads, transit and climate. Your page shows “Permanent data-center ban.” We could not find how you would deliver it (the funding source, rule change, staffing, sequencing or partner), or what number, deadline or visible result would show it worked.

Choices we found no statement on
4. Transportation taxes. Raise the gas tax and vehicle fees again to fund roads, after voters repealed the 2025 package?
5. Kicker refund. Let the state hold back part of a kicker refund for other uses?
6. Data-center power rates. Should data centers pay a separate, higher electricity rate (the 2025 POWER Act)?
7. Homelessness emergency. Keep the state homelessness emergency and its state-funded shelter beds going?
8. Climate Protection Program. Keep the Climate Protection Program's declining cap on fuel emissions?
9. Sanctuary law. Keep Oregon's sanctuary-law limits on helping federal immigration enforcement?
10. School instructional time. Require districts to keep and add classroom hours even when budgets are short?
11. Interstate Bridge. Build the Interstate Bridge replacement as planned, with tolls, or scale it back?

If any line on your page is wrong, out of date or unfair, say so and I will correct it and note the change in the research log. Anything you send on the record we treat the same as we treat every campaign’s statements. Thanks for your time.

Edan Krolewicz
Portland Civic Lab
edan@portlandciviclab.org
```
