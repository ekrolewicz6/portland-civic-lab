# Workforce and execution failures along the continuum

Reviewed September 8, 2026. Research only; no application code changed. Read alongside `placement-evidence.md` for coordinated-access rules, hospital-discharge eligibility, and existing case-conferencing infrastructure. This file separates documented operating failures, administrative snapshots, provider reports, and proposed investigations. An unreported measure is not a nonexistent service. Budgeted staffing, filled positions, staffed beds, and available placements are different things.

## 1. An existing apartment can still fail to become a placement

**Highest-priority current source:** [FY26 Q4 SHS report, updated August 28, 2026](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Q4-FY26-SHS-Report-FINAL-Updated-8.28.26.pdf). Quarterly period April–June 2026; annual figures cover July 2025–June 2026. Page numbers below are PDF pages, counting the first page as 1; these match the report's printed footers.

- **Page 9, PSH challenges:** HSD identifies inconsistent property management in the veteran system: “This led to delayed unit turnovers, vacancies, and a backlog for veterans waiting for placement.” It also reports staffing/security problems elsewhere. This is a concrete agency-reported execution failure, not a quantified independent causal study.
- **Page 7, provider capacity:** Some smaller site-based PSH buildings struggle to fund 24-hour staffing, security, and behavioral care under per-unit funding. HSD redistributed excess funding toward higher needs and plans a deeper FY27 cost evaluation. Services funding is $15,000/household/year, or $17,500 for specified premium categories—not rent or total project cost.
- **Page 5, narrow access restriction:** SHS-funded Housing Only programs reached capacity and stopped new referrals; existing households continue, with no new FY27 placements anticipated. This does **not** describe all rent assistance or all direct-to-housing pathways.
- **Page 5, outreach coverage:** HMIS still misses some outreach engagements; the narrative uses ArcGIS reporting. Existing outreach coordination is improving, not absent.

**Continuum location:** Housing match → ready unit → keys; support after move-in; outreach → assessment.

**Investigate:** HSD and housing/property-management partners should supply anonymized unit-level dates for vacancy, maintenance completion, inspection, referral, acceptance, lease, and move-in, plus coded delay reasons. Request veteran backlog size and duration, actual onsite staffing by shift, and the FY27 true-cost review. For Housing Only, request current intake rules, funding ceiling, unserved eligible requests, and alternative routes. Never extrapolate this narrow restriction to all programs.

**Proposed repair:** Give each vacant unit a named operational owner and a visible next action. Track days lost separately to repair, inspection, paperwork, staffing, and suitable-match constraints. Compare funding against the building's fixed operating needs and residents' support needs. These are proposals, not claims that no such work currently occurs.

## 2. Shelter expansion was accompanied by cuts to housing work

[Adult Shelter Review, final December 17, 2025, published January 2026](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf), FY25 performance and reported FY26 service reductions.

**Agency/provider evidence:** PDF page 94 says housing-focused services and positions across programs were reduced or eliminated for FY26. Specific profiles identify reduced rapid-rehousing positions and rent/client assistance at **Chestnut Tree Inn** (p126), **Central City Concern housing navigators ending at Clinton Triangle** (p130), and reduced shelter housing services at **Gresham Women's Shelter** (p136). These are specific reductions, not proof every navigator disappeared. The report also describes care complexity exceeding case-management staffing and limited sustained mental-health/substance-use support after placement (p85). It does not estimate how many additional housing exits each eliminated position would have produced.

**Continuum location:** Shelter → assessment/navigation → housing offer; support after move-in.

**Investigate:** Request each FY25–FY27 contract amendment, funded and filled housing-navigation FTE, caseloads, vacancies, client-assistance budgets/spending, mobile inreach coverage, and current replacement staffing. Ask whether FY26 reductions were restored or replaced in FY27 before using present-tense copy.

**Proposed repair:** Treat housing exits as a service requiring staff, rent assistance, suitable units, and continued care. Protect that complete function when changing bed counts; report whether funded resources actually reached each shelter. Compare outcomes with participant needs and resource access before ranking providers.

## 3. Empty beds and uneven caseloads were independently documented

[Portland City Auditor, April 17, 2024 shelter-system audit](https://www.portland.gov/auditor/audit-services/news/2024/4/17/joint-office-homeless-services-sustained-focus-shelter-system), sections “The number, locations, and types of shelters…” and “Shelters offer a range of services…”.

**Independent audit, historical:** Providers interviewed during the audit reported roughly 8–9 to 25 participants per case manager/housing specialist; a County survey ranged from 6 to over 37. Some residents waited for a case manager. The auditor documented that unoccupied beds could be unavailable because of repairs, staffing shortages, or reservations; some providers requested capacity reductions to support residents with greater needs. These are not September 2026 caseloads or a current count of beds closed by staffing.

**Continuum location:** Shelter availability → housing work.

**Investigate:** Request a current staffed-capacity census by site and shift, with licensed/design capacity, beds removed from service and reasons, scheduled versus worked staffing, acuity-adjusted caseloads, and days awaiting assignment. Separate new reservations from beds that remain blocked.

**Proposed repair:** Replace one inventory number with successive availability checks: physically exists → safe/operable → staffed → accepts this referral → appropriate and acceptable → occupied. Publish a reason for unavailable capacity and an owner/date for resolution.

## 4. County staffing data identifies a sharper question than “everyone is understaffed”

[FY27 proposed-budget vacancy report](https://multco.us/file/fy_2027_proposed_budget_vacancy_report/download), **vacancies as of February 9, 2026**, PDF pp1, 3–4. [Updated FY27 HSD budget presentation](https://multco.us/file/fy_2027_homeless_services_department_presentation_-_updated/download), slides126–127.

**Administrative snapshot:** Behavioral Health shows **34.88 vacant FTE / 291.33 proposed FTE (12.0%)**. HSD shows **1 / 98 (1.02%)**. The HSD presentation identifies its one budgeted vacancy as the department director and says hired May 4, 2026. It also shows HSD staffing falling from 114 adopted FY26 to 98 approved FY27 FTE. These counts concern County employees, not contracted shelter, outreach, or housing-provider workers; the snapshot does not establish current vacancies or their effect on access.

**Continuum location:** Clinical access; administration/coordination supporting every transition.

**Investigate:** Ask County Health and HSD for current position-level vacancy durations, frozen versus recruiting positions, duties, program caseload effects, contractor coverage, and FY27 changes. Match vacancies to delayed services before alleging a causal bottleneck.

**Proposed repair:** Show staffing at the service/team level. A low department vacancy percentage cannot substitute for adequate frontline capacity, and a high percentage cannot by itself explain homelessness outcomes.

## 5. The wage study is useful history, not current payroll

[JOHS classification, compensation and benefits study, August 2023](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/JOHS-CCB-Study-August2023.pdf), executive summary PDF pp6–8; methodology pp12–13; burnout analysis p53.

**Study evidence:** Compensation reflected FY22, collected May–August 2022. Median annualized pay was about $46,000; entry-level median $42,795. Only 31% of surveyed employees felt compensation met basic needs; over half intended to look for another job within a year. Median tenure was 2.4 years. Neither intentions nor short tenure measures actual annual turnover. Compensation data excluded unfilled roles. Participation varies by data component; do not give one universal provider sample size. The study explicitly warns its burnout/turnover analysis measured correlation, not causality.

**Continuum location:** Workforce continuity across outreach, shelter, navigation, and tenancy support.

**Investigate:** Request a FY26 update: role-specific hourly wages and benefits, vacancy duration, actual separations, first-year retention, caseloads, overtime, contract reimbursement assumptions, and compensation after grants/COLAs. Distinguish worker movement between local providers from departures from the sector.

**Proposed repair:** Fund and verify a sustainable staffing model with supervision and workable caseloads; evaluate retention and continuity, not just dollars awarded. Label historic pay clearly until current payroll aggregates are verified.

## 6. Workforce grants reported improvement—check the follow-through

[FY25 SHS annual report, updated December 11, 2025](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FINAL-SHS-Annual-Report-Updated-12.11.25.pdf), section **“Organizational Health Grants for Workforce Stabilization.”** The official search index supplied this section; PDF page number could not be verified and should be checked before a page-number citation.

**Agency-reported evaluation:** A June 2025 report found 43% of grantees experienced reduced vacancy rates and 53% improved retention. A second round went to 59 providers in March 2025, with spending permitted through June 2026. Those are shares of grantees reporting improvement, not 43% fewer vacant jobs or 53% fewer departures; no causal control group is supplied in the retrieved section.

**Continuum location:** Continuity throughout the service network.

**Investigate:** Request both grant-round evaluations, participant/provider denominators, absolute rates before/after, dollars spent, permanent wage increases versus one-time bonuses, and FY27 continuation. Has an initially positive result survived funding expiration?

**Proposed repair:** Display “intervention → measured result → unresolved question.” Public analysis should acknowledge this investment while testing whether it produced sustained staffing capacity.

## 7. Sunstone's closure was a concrete workforce disruption, not a measured net regional loss

[Sunstone Way WARN notice, March 27, 2026, PDF p1](https://ccwd.hecc.oregon.gov/Layoff/uploads/LOT9473/WARN%209473%20Sunstone%20Way.pdf); [Oregon HECC WARN listing, record9473](https://ccwd.hecc.oregon.gov/Layoff/WARN?SortOrder=EstDate_desc); [Oregon AFSCME, June 29, 2026](https://oregonafscme.org/news/sunstone-way-closes-doors-amidst-gross-financial-mismanagement).

**Employer notice plus union follow-up:** Sunstone announced termination of approximately **175 workers** by June 30, except a few closing the entity. HECC lists an April 2 notification date. AFSCME subsequently said TPI was hiring some Sunstone workers at transferred programs, including Delta Park Motel, Rockwood Bridge, and Naito Village. The notice is not proof that the region permanently lost 175 service workers. The union is a primary source for its own representation/advocacy, not independent proof of all financial allegations.

**Continuum location:** Operator transition → uninterrupted shelter, navigation, and participant relationships.

**Investigate:** Obtain aggregate staff transfer/rehire/sector-exit counts, participant case transfers, service interruptions, vacancies after transfer, benefit continuity, and unresolved handoffs. Pair with the County Auditor's April21 monitoring memorandum researched separately.

**Proposed repair:** Operator transition plans should transfer participant consented records, named case responsibility, and staffing knowledge before services move. Measure missed appointments and days without assigned support during transition.

## 8. Outreach and aftercare exist; referrals are not completed connections

[PSR Aftercare Impact Report for June 2026, published July 16](https://www.portland.gov/streetresponse/news/2026/7/16/portland-street-response-aftercare-impact-report-june-2026), “About Aftercare” and “Key Outcomes”.

**Administrative activity:** PSR describes ongoing outreach/case management by community health workers and peer specialists. June reporting includes **12 MSST assessments**, **5 family-reunification referrals**, **6 Safe Rest Village referrals**, and **45+ community-resource referrals**. These are different activities, potentially overlapping people. They do not establish a completed housing or treatment placement rate, individual staff caseload, or total unmet outreach demand.

**Continuum location:** Crisis response → ongoing engagement → assessment/offer → actual arrival.

**Investigate:** Ask PSR, Portland Solutions, and HSD for eligible referrals, accepted caseload, assigned worker, repeat contacts, reason for unsuccessful engagement, receiving-agency confirmation, arrival, and subsequent housing/clinical connection. Request staff hours and caseloads separately from contact volume. Link only with appropriate consent and privacy protections.

**Proposed repair:** Extend existing aftercare reporting through completed connections and unresolved next steps. A service referral should remain open until receipt or an explicit reason for failure is verified. Do not label every referral without an outcome “failed”; it may be pending or simply unreported.

## 9. Behavioral-health staffing and coordination have audited problems, but statewide is not Portland-specific

[Oregon Secretary of State audit2025-14, May 2025](https://sos.oregon.gov/audits/Documents/2025-14.pdf), printed pp22–23 (PDF pp26–27).

**Independent statewide audit:** Frequent leadership changes and reorganization impeded coordination. Auditors describe shortages affecting community mental-health programs, workload/turnover, and discontinuity for patients; staffing requirements also affect reimbursement for crisis response. These findings do not quantify Multnomah vacancies, Portland waitlists, or current regional treatment completion. OHA agreed to the recommendations; check implementation and later funding before saying the 2025 conditions remain unchanged.

[OHA letter to Senate committee, January 20, 2026](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025I1/Downloads/CommitteeMeetingDocument/311633), section “Bed Capacity, Staffing, and Utilization,” distinguishes licensed, staffed, and occupied beds. The retrieved excerpt discusses public staffed-capacity reporting for youth; it does not prove equivalent comprehensive adult reporting.

**Continuum location:** Crisis → appropriate clinical setting → continuing care.

**Investigate:** Request adult staffed capacity and actual admissions by level of care, referral disposition/waits, shift coverage, insurance/eligibility barriers, and delayed discharge. Separate capital expansion from operating/clinical workforce readiness.

**Proposed repair:** Publish a current functional-capacity view, coordinated referral status, and stable funding assumptions for the workforce needed to operate new facilities.

## 10. New clinical capacity does not erase referral boundaries

[OHA-authored news release, September 4, 2026, “New Facility Expands Secure Residential Behavioral Health Bed Capacity In Multnomah County”](https://flashalert.net/id/3687). OHA reports Cascadia's **10-bed Mountain View secure residential treatment facility** opened September3, with **$5million in state capital funding**. Hospital partners, including Oregon State Hospital, are expected to be the main referral sources. This is not open-access shelter, ongoing annual cost, or a published real-time vacancy count.

[CCC Recuperative Care provider eligibility page](https://centralcityconcern.org/health-care-location/recuperative-care/), accessed September8, requires independent daily living/medication management and describes recovery expected over two to six weeks. Respite therefore does not meet every personal-care or long-term-care need. Its page does not supply current waitlist or staffed-capacity figures.

**Continuum location:** Hospital discharge → respite, residential treatment, or another appropriate setting; housing planning and support alongside care.

**Investigate:** Request current accepted/declined referrals by reason, days awaiting appropriate discharge placement, staffed beds, ADL-support needs, and onward placements. Ask about available ADVSD, Medicaid, and cross-sector supports before describing a total service gap.

**Proposed repair:** Represent destinations by actual eligibility and support capability, with a confirmed receiving team. Show unmet personal-care needs explicitly without portraying treatment completion as a universal housing prerequisite.

## Recommended public investigation frame

Use an interactive continuum with a second layer for the work needed to make each arrow real. Each bottleneck should show: **documented problem; evidence period and scope; accountable service owner; operating measure still needed; proposed repair; next outcome to verify**.

Start with the strongest current diagnosis—unit-turnover and property-management delays—then shelter housing-service cuts, provider transitions, and care-fit restrictions. Use historical workforce evidence as context. Avoid a single “staffing shortage” badge across the whole system; identify whether the constraint is absent funding, a vacant funded position, an unworkable caseload, a missing skill, blocked property operations, no suitable unit, or an uncompleted handoff.

This research did not establish current comprehensive provider vacancy/turnover rates, an adult treatment/respite waitlist for Portland, or a causal share of homelessness attributable to each barrier. Those are concrete records to pursue, not values to invent or assume zero.
