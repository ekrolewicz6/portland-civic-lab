# Continuum cost audit — September 8, 2026

These are figures suitable for publication with the specific labels below. Budget allocations and payment standards are not invoices or actual spending. A defensible cost visual should offer separate tabs for **operating a place**, **paying rent**, **one-time help**, and **current budgets**. Never rank these four categories on one undifferentiated axis.

## 1. Latest verified adult shelter operating-cost review

[Multnomah County Adult Shelter Review FY25](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf), January 2026; observations July 2024–June 2025. Pages 49, 55–59, 91–94.

| Category | Reported average annual cost/bed or unit | Daily equivalent, annual ÷ 365 | Reported average housing exits/bed or unit/year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| All reviewed adult shelters | about $47,000 | about $129 | 0.39 |
| Congregate | about $37,000 | about $101 | 0.45 |
| Alternative/village | about $51,000 | about $140 | 0.21 |
| Motel | about $62,000 | about $170 | 0.52 |

Scope: 31 programs, $98 million. FY25 reported program costs; separate FY26 budgets appear in profiles. These are the review’s averages, not $98m divided by total capacity. External placement resources are omitted. Annual/365 is arithmetic, **not occupied-person-night cost**. Partial-year operation and double occupancy affect comparisons. Motel figures vary within the document ($62k/$63k; one $70k occurrence); verify its worksheet before greater precision.

**Finding:** housing-resource access complicates rankings: 33% of housing exits involved extra placement programs; motel share was 55%. The review calls congregate the most cost-effective type for housing exits in FY25. A blanket recommendation to replace congregate shelters with motels because motel exit percentages are higher is unsupported.

**BHRC correction:** $111,797/bed includes shelter/bridge costs and some shared day-center expenses. It is not the price of a sobering station or encounter.

## 2. The flagship $16,000 supportive-housing comparison is unverified

The page attributes $16,000/person/year to the [County January 12, 2026 release](https://multco.us/news/news-release-new-report-suggests-change-shelter-investments-could-help-more-people-leave). That release contains no such price; neither does a text search of its linked shelter review. Remove the figure or locate the underlying cost calculation before promoting “three times cheaper” as a quote.

A usable attributed quote from that release, by interim HSD director Anna Plumb: “the availability of housing assistance hasn’t kept up.” Keep the fuller explanatory framing in original editorial language: **A shelter bed cannot become a housing exit without somewhere to move.**

Do not infer that one prevented episode saves one full annual shelter-bed cost, or that freeing a bed automatically releases its average operating cost as cash. Staffing, leases, overhead, and demand remain. Cost avoidance and cash savings require separate calculations.

It describes up to $10,000/unit for PSH services **plus** rent assistance estimates: studio $12,622/year; 1BR $15,832; 2BR $17,886. These are procurement planning assumptions, not 2026 actual costs. **Visual:** “Money authorized → eligible households found → applications approved → rent paid → housed at 6/12 months.” This makes an administrative bottleneck visible and testable without pretending budgeted benefits have been delivered.

## Editorial and measurement recommendations

- Give every number a visible geography, population, period, denominator, and accounting status: actual, budget, target, derived, historical benchmark, or unknown.
- Replace the current mixed-cost bars with comparable units. Distinguish cost per available bed-night, occupied bed-night, person served, placement, and household stably housed at twelve months.
- Plot housing exits per 100 beds against cost per bed, with bubble size for additional housing assistance and color for population served. Acknowledge selection effects; do not claim the scatterplot identifies causality.
- Do not use penalties for unknown exit destinations as an automatic recommendation: providers cannot always establish where someone went. Measure attempted follow-up, cross-provider linkage, destination completeness, and resident privacy before designing incentives.
- Request invoice-backed costs for FY26, occupied bed-nights, linked housing-placement expenditures, service intensity, vacancy duration, and retention cohorts. Broad program budgets are a starting point, not a complete public price list.

Suggested original quote: **“We should buy a complete path to housing, then measure where that path breaks.”**
