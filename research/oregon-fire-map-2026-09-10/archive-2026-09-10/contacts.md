# Oregon fire map: who to contact and why

Compiled September 10, 2026. Names and addresses below were read from official pages on that date unless marked otherwise. Role-level entries are where no name was published. The recommended order is at the end.

## What each group actually does

**ODF Smoke Management Program (Salem).** Runs the statewide registry for prescribed burning on forestland under the Oregon Smoke Management Plan. Its meteorologists issue the daily burn instructions that decide whether registered units may ignite. Its staff maintain the Smoke Data System (registrations and accomplishments), produce the daily and weekly PDFs, the public map, and the annual report. It also runs the Certified Burn Manager program and the Prescribed Fire Liability pilot. This is the office that holds the per-burn history we want.

**ODF district offices.** Twelve protection districts (Astoria, Tillamook, Forest Grove, West Oregon, North Cascade, South Cascade, Western Lane, Southwest, Coos, Douglas FPA, Central Oregon, Northeast, Klamath-Lake) do the field work: they take registrations from landowners, inspect units, relay burn instructions, and enter accomplishments. If the Salem office says the history is held locally, these are the next stop.

**Forest Service ranger districts (fuels staff).** Plan and run burns on national forests: write the burn plans, carry the NEPA decision, register with ODF, and post the planned units to the Region 6 tracker. Each unit in the tracker lists its district phone. The Region 6 office in Portland coordinates and publishes the annual fire summaries.

**BLM district fuels programs.** Same role on BLM land. The OR/WA state office publishes the prescribed fire polygon dataset and the season's planned burns.

**Certified Burn Managers.** People ODF has certified (24 hours of coursework, three training burns, five-year certification) to plan and lead prescribed burns on ODF-protected land. When a landowner uses a CBM and follows the burn plan, both are protected from civil liability (SB 762; OAR 629-042-1000 to 1070). A CBM writes the burn plan (objectives, weather prescription, ignition pattern, holding resources, contingencies), files the smoke management registration with the ODF district, waits for the daily instruction, runs the burn as burn boss, and files the accomplishment. They know why each unit was chosen, and they hold the plans and the photos. The directory columns mean: Insured (carries their own liability insurance), For hire (will burn for other landowners), Willing to mentor (will take trainees on burns, which is how new CBMs get their three training fires).

**Prescribed burn associations.** Landowner cooperatives that pool equipment, people, and know-how to burn each other's land, usually with an OSU Extension fire specialist helping. Seven exist as of January 2026. They are the route to private-land burns and to the landowners' own reasons.

**OSU Extension Fire Program.** Regional fire specialists across the state who help PBAs form, teach the CBM course, and publish the guides. The program manager coordinates them. Neutral, well connected, and used to explaining fire to the public; a natural advisor for the map.

**OSU Institute for Natural Resources and Oregon Explorer.** Built the Oregon Wildfire Risk Explorer, the CWPP planning tool, and the cleaned statewide perimeter history. They have already solved the data-cleaning problems we will hit.

**The Nature Conservancy Oregon.** Runs prescribed fire at Sycan Marsh and elsewhere, hosts research, and employs two of the 23 CBMs including its fire program director. The most experienced non-agency burn program in the state.

**Tribal fire programs.** The Klamath Tribes Wildland Fire Program burns on tribal and adjacent federal land. Warm Springs, Grand Ronde, and Siletz are restoring ecocultural burning. Approach as partners whose burns may or may not belong on a public map, on their terms.

**NWCC (Portland).** The Northwest Geographic Area Coordination Center. Compiles large fire perimeters and daily situation reports for Oregon and Washington and runs Predictive Services.

**DEQ Air Quality.** Co-administers the smoke management plan, designates smoke sensitive receptor areas with ODF, and runs the air monitoring network. Holds the monitor history.

**ODA Smoke Management Program.** Registers and permits Willamette Valley field burning and makes daily burn calls with ODF meteorologists. Holds field-level registrations and accomplishments that are not published.

**Oregon PUC Safety Division.** Reviews the three investor-owned utilities' wildfire mitigation plans and PSPS reports. The route to utility ignition and risk-zone data.

**Oregon Department of Emergency Management.** RAPTOR, the OEM hub, and (through ODHS's contract) Genasys evacuation zones for all counties and nine tribes.

**Oregon Hazards Lab (UO).** Operates the Oregon part of the ALERTWest camera network.

## Should we email all 23 burn managers?

Not yet, and not with a general "would you like to take part." The directory exists so landowners can hire them, and a cold mass email with no concrete ask will get few replies. Two steps work better:

1. **Now: six or seven targeted notes** to the people who sit at hubs and would each open a door to many burns: Stacy McCarter (the data), Carrie Berger (Extension and the PBAs), Kai Sauerbrey (TNC and Sycan), John Punches or Micah Schmidt (Northeast Oregon), Richard Pasquale (Central Oregon co-op), Brent Ross (Willamette Valley), and one contractor such as Daniel Cleveland. Ask each for a 20-minute call. The draft is in outreach-drafts.md.
2. **Later, with a prototype:** one note to the whole directory and to the Prescribed Fire Council list, inviting practitioners to check how their burns appear and to add the reason for each unit in their own words. That is a specific ask that respects their time and gets us the narrative layer.

The tribal programs are a separate conversation, in person if possible, before anything of theirs appears on the map.

## Recommended order

1. Stacy McCarter (ODF): the SDS export and the practitioner introductions. Send the data request first; everything else builds on it.
2. Carrie Berger (OSU Extension): advisor and introductions.
3. Kai Sauerbrey (TNC): credibility with practitioners and the Sycan example.

5. Regional hubs: Punches or Schmidt, Pasquale, Ross, Cleveland.
6. ODA, DEQ, PUC, OEM: records and layers, in parallel, low effort.
7. Tribal programs: after we have something to show and a clear statement of how we will handle their burns.
8. The full CBM directory and the Prescribed Fire Council: after the prototype.
