# Oregon Fire Map — research audit, September 11, 2026

Authors: Edan Krolewicz and Dominic Kuklawood.

The original eight files and 50 source entries are preserved in `archive-2026-09-10/`, explicitly marked superseded. The current inventory, contacts, drafts, endpoint register and sourcebook incorporate the corrections below. No outreach has been sent. A working implementation is being prepared at `/oregon-fire`; publication requires co-author review.

## Assessment

There is enough public data to build the first map. There is not enough evidence to claim comprehensive coverage or explain every burn’s location. The biggest gaps are ecological burning outside forestry programs, actual permit records, cross-system identifiers and site-specific explanations. A source is not complete merely because its public service was downloaded completely.

## Data interpretation and public page

FPA FOD is the historical occurrence baseline; Oregon Explorer is the historical perimeter baseline. Recent WFIGS supplements them. ODF occurrence and NWCC are enrichment/reconciliation sources. These are different representations, and their counts are not additive fire totals.

Keep treatment units, registrations/permits, individual ignitions, accomplishments and observations separate. Verified IDs can link records; proximity alone cannot merge them. Keep source dates and precision, repeated treatments, partial burns, reported treatment acreage, reported fire size and polygon area distinct. Unknown dates are explicitly included and labeled. Source-specific counts and freshness accompany results and export.

The page emphasizes prescribed burning, with selectable plans and wildfire context, year/location/agency/method/purpose/status filters, synchronized map/list, shareable selection and filters, record details, source coverage and private correction submissions. Default years: 2021–present. The Woodpecker burns supply the first explanatory story, without an invented map location. [OSU account](https://www.forestry.oregonstate.edu/news/fire-purpose).

Smoke measurements do not by themselves identify the responsible burn. MTBS excludes many small burns under its western size threshold. Spatial overlap with later wildfire does not establish treatment effectiveness. EPA AQS/AirData, AirNow, NOAA HMS, intrusion investigations, MTBS/RAVG/FTEM and treatment monitoring remain separate workstreams, alongside mechanical treatment, vegetation, land management, CWPP priorities, funding, incident impacts, weather and utility ignition records.

## Verification vocabulary

- **Discovered:** a lead or publication exists; data access/completeness is not established.
- **Queried:** endpoint/schema/response examined; retain date and query parameters.
- **Downloaded:** actual data retrieved and reconciled to the requested inventory; keep checksums, source timestamps, failures and a run ID.
- **Confirmed by steward:** the responsible program explicitly confirmed interpretation or coverage. No source has this status yet.

Runtime evidence lives in `fire.import_runs`, sanitized versioned `fire.records`, `fire.rejections`, and `fire.reference`. The exportable evidence snapshot in this directory records completed imports separately from source-level claims. Data downloads stay outside Git.
