Subject: Portland Civic Lab voters guide: Daniel Miller, Gresham Council, Position 6

Hi Daniel,

I run Portland Civic Lab, a nonpartisan, volunteer research project publishing a voters guide for every race on the November 3 ballot. Your page is at https://www.portlandciviclab.org/voters-guide/gresham-position-6/daniel-miller. It is built only from your published words (your filed statement, your site and public statements) and the public record, the same way for every candidate, with a source on every line. It carries no endorsements, rankings or scores. The race page also has a board for each live choice the City of Gresham faces, with every candidate’s statement or a gap: https://www.portlandciviclab.org/voters-guide/gresham-position-6#topics.

Right now the page shows 0 of 6 choices on record for you. A gap on a page is a research gap, not a position, and it says so, but readers do compare. The questions below are the gaps. A sentence or two on any of them is enough; we quote or paraphrase with a date and attribute it to your campaign, and we never fill a gap from a general goal.

The four issues every race page tracks
1. Rent and homes. How would you make housing more affordable?
2. Camps, crime and who responds. What balance of enforcement, prevention and care do you propose?
3. Your bills and taxes. Who pays, and what would receive priority?
4. Streets, buses and air. Where would you invest, and what would you restrict?

Choices we found no statement on
5. Public-safety fee. Keep the Police, Fire and Parks Fee at $15 a month, now that the council can change it by resolution?
6. Levy and fire district. Renew the $1.35 public-safety levy before it expires in 2029, and keep Gresham’s own fire department rather than join a district?
7. General-fund gap. Keep drawing reserves to cover a $10 million general-fund gap, or cut services or raise revenue?
8. Groundwater and rates. Stay on the new groundwater system and fix the taste and hardness complaints, with water rates rising 5% a year from 2028?
9. Camping and shelters. Keep the camping ban with housing-focused outreach, and allow pod shelters only under the new permit code?
10. Immigration enforcement. Go beyond the January 2026 rule-of-law resolution and declare an immigration-enforcement emergency, as residents asked?

If any line on your page is wrong, out of date or unfair, say so and I will correct it and note the change in the research log. Anything you send on the record we treat the same as we treat every campaign’s statements. Thanks for your time.

Edan Krolewicz
Portland Civic Lab
edan@portlandciviclab.org
