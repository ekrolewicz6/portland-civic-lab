# Research and verification notes

**Measure 26-267 · Research date: September 17, 2026**

## Method

The review started with the two requested campaign websites. Their substantive policy pages and linked evidence were examined, then compared with official election filings, the full proposed amendment, current budget records, the 2022 Budget Office memo, City Club's 2025 study, other jurisdictions' program records, and academic research. Follow-up searches deliberately sought both benefits and failures, and differences between charter mandates and specific funding formulas.

This is desk research, not a formal systematic review. There were no original interviews, public-records requests, contacts with campaigns, or transaction-level campaign-finance analysis. The user's meetings with the campaigns were not used as evidence because no notes or recordings were supplied.

Evidence priority varies by question:

- **Requirements:** actual proposed charter text, followed by official election records. Campaign explanations do not override the text.
- **Budget facts:** adopted and supplemental records, with forecast vintages identified. Proposed budgets and historical estimates are not substituted for current adopted figures.
- **Participation and selected projects:** primary program reports, attributed to their authors. Counts are not independently audited here.
- **Effects:** study design matters. Matched observational studies are stronger than testimonials for some questions but do not have the certainty of random assignment.
- **Policy judgments:** explicitly presented as analysis. A claim can be incorrect even when made by a campaign with an otherwise reasonable position.

## Important document distinctions

1. **Use initiative 1PDX2025 / Measure 26-267.** Earlier Portland PB petitions have different terms and dates. Search results surface them prominently.
2. **Use the official full amendment for detailed requirements.** The city election log identifies the one-page document used here. Its text was compared with the copy linked by the YES campaign.
3. **The final county filing was visually inspected.** It is a two-page scanned PDF with no useful extractable text. Page 1 is the signed August 28, 2026 referral; page 2 reproduces the August 13, 2025 ballot title. Both say operational July 2027 and give $16.4 million and $17 million estimates.
4. **The full text separates funding and first process.** Funding begins by FY2027–28; first process begins by July 2028. The report flags the difference from the summary without claiming an official resolution.
5. **A forecast is not an adopted allocation.** The current adopted-budget overview's $803.4 million discretionary-resource figure is not automatically the amendment's ongoing-expense base. The review did not obtain an official line-item reconciliation or a current measure-specific fiscal-impact report.
6. **The July supplemental budget matters.** The city's adopted-budget page explicitly says the original adopted book does not incorporate those subsequent changes.
7. **The 2022 Budget Office memo is historical.** It evaluates different percentage examples and different budgets. It supports an opportunity-cost concern, not a precise 2026 prediction of layoffs or station closures.
8. **City Club's report is a 2025 policy study.** It predates this exact July 2025 filing. Its recommendations are relevant but should not be labeled an official analysis of the final 2026 ballot text.
9. **PB funding, process cost, and project funding differ.** Do not add administrative costs to an all-in allocation without specifying that additional appropriation assumption.
10. **A resident's PB ballot can support multiple projects.** Do not confuse project votes with unique voters or divide participants by an inapplicable population to create a turnout rate.

## Outstanding questions worth putting to the campaigns and city

These questions are prepared for follow-up; they have **not** been sent.

### City Budget Office and City Attorney

- Publish the first-year calculation from FY2026–27 adopted line items: what exactly constitutes General Fund discretionary ongoing expenses? How do transfers, contingency, overhead, amendments, and one-time items affect the calculation?
- Explain whether the formula refers to the original adopted budget or a subsequently amended adopted budget.
- Provide a low, central, and high estimate for startup and recurring process costs, including bureau time, outreach, translation, voting administration, evaluation, and audit support.
- Identify legally eligible funding sources and the projects or programs they would otherwise support. Separate available dollars from already committed dollars.
- Clarify the July 2027 ballot-summary wording versus the July 2028 first-process deadline, and define “bi-annual.”
- Explain the implementation of binding decisions within Oregon Local Budget Law and the effect of the election-law distinction. Identify any unresolved issues without assuming the whole measure is unlawful.
- Explain carryforward rules and what happens to unused allocations before the first vote or between cycles.
- Explain how required audits would be scheduled and resourced while preserving the Auditor's independence.

### YES campaign

- What is the evidence for the chosen 2% floor rather than another amount? Which findings are direct evidence, and which are practitioner recommendations?
- Which implementation costs are included in the roughly $1 million estimate? Does it include staff time charged to other bureaus?
- Will you publish a draft rulebook for eligibility, residency verification, vote integrity, delegate selection, conflict management, and district allocation?
- Do you support limiting recurring commitments? If so, why is the limit absent from the charter text, and how would an ordinance address it?
- What alternative city expenditures would you be willing to displace to fund PB? How would residents see those tradeoffs before selecting projects?
- What independent measures would count as a disappointing result, and what changes would you support in response?
- How would outreach organizations or affiliated implementers be separated from project screening and contracting decisions?

### NO campaign

- Which service reductions are forecasts, which are cost-equivalence illustrations, and which are directly required by this measure? Please provide a current bureau-level model.
- How does your analysis account for PB projects that provide useful public services or use eligible restricted funds?
- Will you correct descriptions suggesting the amendment contains no audit requirement or no ability to change process rules?
- Does your ten-year calculation include administration within the allocation, or add it separately? What growth assumption does it use?
- How does your current account incorporate the July supplemental budget?
- What funded pilot would you support? Identify amount, department, eligibility, schedule, evaluation, and a date for deciding whether to expand.
- What primary-source inventory supports the categorical claim that no U.S. city uses the exact funding model?

### Auditor and independent evaluators

- Which goals can be measured before the first cycle, so later evaluations have a baseline?
- How would you compare participants with eligible residents and with participants in existing budget channels?
- How would you evaluate project delivery, benefits, displacement, conflicts, and costs beyond participant satisfaction?
- Can the evaluation report negative findings without relying on the program operator's permission?

## Checks before later publication or reuse

The report is complete as a dated desk analysis. Before presenting it as current at a later date, recheck the final voters' pamphlet, ballot status, any measure-specific fiscal estimate, budget updates, campaign statements, and new draft implementation rules. The main conclusions do not depend on knowing future project winners.

Do not imply that either campaign reviewed or endorsed this analysis. Do not describe selected projects as completed unless completion is independently established. Do not call the report a formal legal opinion, a systematic evidence review, or a verified donor investigation.

No conclusion is offered about campaign donors, pass-through contributions, or financial motives. The campaign footers identify Community Budgeting for All PAC, ID 23058, and Protect Our City Services, PAC ID 25285. A separate campaign-finance review would require dated official filings, transaction definitions, and checks against double counting. Endorsements alone do not establish a financial interest.

## Reproducible arithmetic

- Funding floor examples: $800,000,000 × 0.02 = $16,000,000; $750,000,000 × 0.02 = $15,000,000.
- At a $16.4 million allocation, $1 million in process cost is 6.10%; $2 million is 12.20%; $3 million is 18.29%.
- Corresponding project balances: $15.4 million, $14.4 million, $13.4 million.
- Flat ten-year allocation: $16.4 million × 10 = $164 million.
- With 3% nominal annual growth, first year $16.4 million: sum of $16.4 million × 1.03^t for t = 0 through 9 = approximately $188.01 million.

These are illustrations. They are not city forecasts, do not estimate inflation-adjusted costs, and do not assume that PB spending is entirely additional to existing city spending.
