Reviewed October 10, 2026. Michelle Jeffries sent the public links below after an October 9 conversation. This is an assessment of **her email and independently inspected public documentation**, not a record or paraphrase of the conversation. No October 9 call notes or transcript were available for this review. Do not attribute an oral technical statement to Michelle until it is checked against notes or confirmed with her. Her correspondence and any later notes remain outside the repository and deployment inputs.

The central distinction for the public guide is between **a treatment record, a fire perimeter, satellite-classified burned area, a severity assessment, and field monitoring**. They answer different questions. Layering or summing them without their definitions would mislead readers about what happened and whether it helped.

## Website decision

1. Improve the existing “After the fire” visual so it names **four distinct views**: perimeter, observed burned area, severity, and later observations. Link the USGS WFTT methods beside this visual. This fills a specific reader gap without adding a duplicate map layer or a new statistic.
2. Keep the current Oregon Explorer historical-perimeter and WFIGS recent-perimeter hierarchy. No Michelle-linked treatment or monitoring source becomes an additional public count until its identity, geometry, classification, and overlap pass reconciliation.
3. For the selected landscape investigation, test LTDL/TWIG/OWRI/AIM one by one against actual reader questions: what work occurred; what were the water or habitat goals; what was measured afterward? Their presence in one viewer does not make them a connected causal chain.
4. A future Oregon trend chart must choose its numerator (incident count, area inside perimeters, satellite-classified burned area, or severe-area extent), geography, threshold, years, and treatment of missing records before publication. Show these definitions next to the number.
