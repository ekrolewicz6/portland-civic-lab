# Inner Eastside zoning board: Portland: Neighbors Welcome questionnaire (added October 14, 2026)

Jesse Rawlins of Portland: Neighbors Welcome, asked on October 10 which housing decisions the next Council faces, named the Inner Eastside Zoning Project (the group's current campaign), social housing, affordable-housing funding and tenant protections (email, October 12). Asked whether the group's candidate questionnaire answers were public, he sent the folder link on October 14. The group endorses in these races. The answers are the candidates' own written words; the Lab read only the answers and adopted no rating.

## The board

- Topic id `inner-eastside`; question: "Rezone the inner east side to allow apartment buildings off the main streets (the Inner Eastside Zoning Project)?"
- Context facts: Resolution 37746 adopted as amended 12–0 on April 15, 2026 (portland.gov/council/documents/resolution/adopted/37746); the planners' July 27, 2026 status report schedules Council consideration in 2028; study area NE Fremont Street to SE Powell Boulevard (research/source-archive/2026-10-12.md, housing section).
- No `decisionId`: the resolution set a timeline and does not adopt zoning, so incumbents' votes are stated in the context rather than rendered as vote pills.

## Gaps

The seventeen other candidates (Corcoran, Frankenstein, Hallett, Kelly, Landgraver, McCormick, Novick, Parker, Pham, Sollitt, Ward; Arnold, Cronlund, Goldsmith, Leake, McDonald, Schulte) did not answer the questionnaire. Their campaign home pages, fetched October 14, contain no mention of the Inner Eastside project (five have no site). Subpages were not read. Their cells are gaps, not positions. Steve Novick, Tiffany Koyama Lane, Angelita Morillo, Olivia Clark, Mitch Green and Eric Zimmerman all voted for Resolution 37746; that vote is in the board's context, not inferred into a cell.

## Other questionnaire content not used

The questionnaire also asks about housing of all kinds, funding for households under 80% of area median income, social housing, evictions and tenant protections, displacement, homelessness interventions and transportation. Those answers could fill gaps on the existing housing lines and the camp-removal and Street Response boards; they were not read for that purpose on October 14.
