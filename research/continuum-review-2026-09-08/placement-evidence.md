# Placement, triage, and system-failure evidence

Reviewed September 8, 2026. Read the current `src/lib/homeless/continuum.ts`, `research/homelessness-continuum/document.md`, and source registry; then checked primary agency, auditor, statute, and university material. No site code changed.

The strongest defensible story is that housing, safety, clinical care, and continuity must work together. The page should show where the connection fails, distinguish available services from available access, and acknowledge improvements that already exist. A missing public measure is not proof that a service does not exist.

## 1. Replace “No diversion function exists” with a coverage and speed question

**Documented policy:** Multnomah County's Coordinated Access policies, version 1.1, updated November 2025, require housing problem solving at triage. It is available even outside the priority pool; assistance can include mediation and limited financial help. The policy also acknowledges limited capacity to contact households outside the priority pool. Pages 7–8.

**Correction:** “No diversion function exists” is materially misleading. “Housing problem solving exists; public reporting does not establish universal same-day access, available cash, or outcomes” is supportable from the sources reviewed.

**Proposal:** Put a “resolve housing crisis now” branch before and alongside assessment: landlord mediation, a safe family/friend option, arrears/deposit help, benefits, legal support. Publish requests, eligible requests, completed assistance, median time to payment, and subsequent housing status. Do not count a conversation as a resolved episode.

[Coordinated Access policies, November 2025](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/1.0_CA_Policies_FINAL_2025.pdf). [Current public Coordinated Access page](https://hsd.multco.us/coordinated-access/).

## 2. The housing queue already has rules; show whether the rules deliver

**Documented policy:** The same November 2025 policy specifies housing preferences/matching, regular contact, housing-provider contact within 15 days, and enrollment within 30 days. Denials and reasons are tracked and reviewed at least annually. Participants may decline any number of referrals. Missing contact can cause removal from the priority pool; mechanisms permit reengagement. Pages 11–18.

**Inference:** A proposed “single accountable lead / warm handoff” should build on CHAT, family navigators, and existing policies. The visible gap is whether assigned support, referrals, contact continuity, and move-ins occur promptly for every eligible household.

**Proposal:** An interactive matched-referral funnel should distinguish: assessed → prioritized → assigned navigator → matched → provider accepted → located/contacted → enrollment → keys/move-in. Side exits should name “not eligible for this funding,” “no suitable vacancy,” “care needs exceed this setting,” “cannot reconnect,” “person declined this offer,” and “still waiting.” Show actual cohort counts and stage durations; where unavailable, say “not publicly reported.”

[Coordinated Access policies, November 2025](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/1.0_CA_Policies_FINAL_2025.pdf).

## 3. Sheltering and housing are distinct outcomes—and shelter funding can miss the exit

**Agency analysis, not an independent audit:** The Adult Shelter Review covers 31 housing-focused, 24/7 adult programs in FY2025: 6,731 unique people, 88% average occupancy, 841 people exiting to permanent housing, and 0.39 permanent-housing exits per bed/unit. Providers identify insufficient affordable housing, subsidies, flexible assistance and post-placement support. This is not a causal comparison.

**Critical reconciliation:** Its methodology gives approximately 2,800/5,213 exits with unreported destinations (54%); another section gives 5,856 exits and 51% unreported. Reconcile before a 100-exit graphic. Do not back-calculate 834 housing exits from 16% × 5,213 when the executive summary says 841.

**Proposal:** Present “a bed tonight” and “a lasting home” as separate achievements. Show case-management staffing, available rental assistance, and suitable housing offers next to bed inventory. The policy question is the resource mix that maximizes safety and durable housing, not a blanket closure rule based on one year's unadjusted shelter ranking.

[Adult Shelter Review, final December 17, 2025; published January 2026](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf), executive summary, methodology, housing-outcome and barrier sections.

## 4. The page should diagnose unequal access, not only average performance

**Agency finding:** In that review, Hispanic/Latino residents were 17% of the comparison by-name-list population but 11% served; Black residents were 23% versus 17%. The analysis excludes a closed culturally specific motel program and has different missing-demographic rates between comparison groups. These are access signals, not a causal discrimination estimate.

**Proposal:** Every funnel needs an optional equity view by race/ethnicity, age, disability/accessibility needs, household type, and geography. Ask which referral sources, admission rules, physical accessibility, language access, and transport conditions explain unequal passage through stages. Publish small-cell protections and avoid ranking tiny programs with unstable percentages.

[Adult Shelter Review](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf), printed pages 87–90.

## 5. The strongest new local lead is what people themselves say

**Primary study:** PSU's Pathways survey report, released April 9, 2026, finds **98% of 350 respondents asked** wanted permanent, stable housing. The overall analytic sample was 541 adults, surveyed March 11–December 31, 2025. The 350 were an opt-in subset who were unhoused or lacked long-term housing stability—not all 541. Recruitment used selected service/housing sites and outreach, with first-come participation; English/Spanish only. The sample is not a probability sample of every person experiencing homelessness and overrepresents some demographic groups. Separate housing-dealbreaker questions had 440 respondents: 49.8% selected restrictions on guests/roommates/partners, 44.5% temporary stays, and 36.8% no pets. Reported housing-loss reasons included reduced/lost income (44.3%, n=440; multiple selections allowed). Pages 20–28, 44, 49, 135.

**Quote option:** “Safe, warm, electricity, stable place to cook and clean myself and have my kitties.” Anonymous study participant, p45. Do not invent a name or character backstory.

**Proposal:** Open with the contrast between a widely desired outcome and a system unable to offer enough suitable, affordable paths to it. Replace “service-refusing” as a permanent cohort with “offer declined / barriers to fit.” A wish for permanent housing is not proof that any particular shelter offer is acceptable or accessible.

[Pathways survey report, April 9, 2026](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Pathways-Survey-Findings-Published-4.9.2026.pdf).

## 6. There are already some arrival counts—extend the chain instead of saying none exists

**Administrative report:** Portland's July 20–26, 2026 Street Services report, published July 28, records 20 people expressing interest in a referral and prompting an availability call, 11 accepting referrals, and 8 using a bed for at least one night. It also records 423 active campsites visited. Campsites and people are different units; the 20 is not a count of everyone offered shelter, despite the report's shorter summary label.

**Correction:** Claims of “no arrival tracking” are overbroad. The public gap is a complete, linked, longitudinal chain across programs: who received a suitable offer, who arrived, stayed, moved to housing, and remained housed.

**Proposal:** Display this as a small real example with its narrow scope, then clearly label the missing 7-day, 30-day, housing, and retention links. Never divide 8 people by 423 camps to make a conversion rate. Request person-deduplicated cohorts and definitions before computing longer-term conversion.

[Street Services report, July 20–26, 2026](https://www.portland.gov/homelessness-impact-reduction/news/2026/7/28/weekly-street-services-report-july-20-26-2026).

## 7. Housing data problems are real, but a correction already passed an audit

**Independent audit:** The County's 2024 housing-data audit found JOHS had corrected placement reporting to use housing move-in dates rather than project-start dates. Reporting reflected provider data accurately most of the time. It also found HMIS usability problems and duplicate entry into separate systems that weakened data quality.

**Correction:** Avoid implying current housing placements are still merely program enrollments or that the auditor found all housing reporting unreliable. The stronger criticism is specific: corrected placement counts do not automatically establish address completeness, representative follow-up, care continuity, or lasting housing stability.

**Proposal:** Give every metric a definition, observation period, denominator, known/unknown split, and latest independent verification. Link “what changed since the audit.” Show the planned/new HMIS as an implementation whose quality must be measured, not as an automatic cure.

[County housing-data audit, 2024](https://multco.us/info/johs-reporting-supportive-housing-and-rapid-rehousing-was-accurate-reflection-provider-data).

## 8. Retention needs both outcomes and visibility

**Administrative evidence:** The County's May 14, 2025 budget follow-up shows a rapid-rehousing retention result of 88% for its specified FY25Q2 measure, while noting 53% of a 2,436-person placement cohort had an unknown housing situation at follow-up because of missing data/non-contact. These are differently described cohorts in the presentation; do not mechanically turn 88% into a whole-cohort success rate.

The preliminary May 2026 HRAP report includes linked Medicaid/housing evidence: over two years, observed returns from permanent housing were 4.2% for people identified with psychosis, stimulant-use disorder or opioid-use disorder versus 1.2% for those without those claims indicators. This is not an acuity-screening instrument, causal effect, or proof that all other participants remained housed.

**Proposal:** A retention visual should show “confirmed housed,” “observed return to homelessness,” “other known outcome,” and “not known,” with denominator/date visible. Show services that continue after move-in: tenancy help, food, medication/clinical continuity, ADL support, crisis response, eviction prevention. Measure the unmet need and completed connection, not only referral volume.

[May 14, 2025 budget follow-up, slide 11](https://multco.us/file/fy_2026_homeless_services_department_budget_worksession_follow-up/download). [Preliminary HRAP report, May 2026, p12](https://multco.us/file/hrap_quarterly_report_may_2026/download).

## 9. A bed can exist and still be the wrong destination at this moment

**Current published access rules:** County sobering serves up to 13 adults at a time, 24/7, voluntarily and by referral from listed partners. Medical emergencies and mental-health crises are excluded; unsafe presentations are transported to other care. The maximum stay is 24 hours. Department of Community Justice is a listed referring partner, so the page's universal assertion that jail-release referrals cannot occur needs verification rather than inference. Page last reviewed July 13, 2026.

The permanent county center is a future project, with the current project page stating up to 50 people and planned fall 2027 opening. Its planned capacity must not appear as available now, and reporting “47” versus “up to 50” should cite the relevant design date.

**Proposal:** Clinical/sobering resource cards need five separate attributes: licensed/design capacity; staffed capacity; occupied/reserved; accepting referrals now; eligible/appropriate for this person. Add hours, referral channel, transport, medication/accessibility needs, and next destination. This is the clearest way to explain why an apparently empty bed can fail to solve an immediate need.

[Sobering services, reviewed July 13, 2026](https://multco.us/info/sobering-services). [Future center project page](https://multco.us/info/future-sobering-and-crisis-stabilization-center).

## 10. Medical respite has an eligibility boundary; show the higher-care gap honestly

**Provider documentation:** CCC Recuperative Care at Evergreen Crossing accepts referrals from hospitals/outpatient providers for conditions expected to resolve/stabilize in two to six weeks. Patients must independently manage daily activities and medications and move independently, with aids permitted. The public contact hours are weekdays 8–5; those are not necessarily the hours residents receive care. Its cumulative “over 60% stable housing” statement does not supply a recent cohort or standardized permanent-housing definition.

**Inference:** Medical respite cannot stand in for all nursing, personal-care, or long-term-care needs. The current provider page does not publish real-time vacancies; “84” should be date-stamped to a separately verified capacity source.

**Proposal:** Add “needs help with daily activities” as a support-matching dimension handled by qualified assessors. Connect to long-term services and supports, accessible housing and home-care options, respite, or appropriate facility care. Do not route every medically complex person from an emergency department to a standard shelter or presume PSH alone meets all care needs.

[CCC Recuperative Care current program and eligibility](https://centralcityconcern.org/health-care-location/recuperative-care/).

## 11. Build on existing health–housing coordination and bridge services

**Current programs:** HSD Cross Sector Case Conferencing already joins homeless-service providers, health plans, health systems, behavioral health, Aging/Disability/Veterans Services, and IDD. It meets twice monthly, prioritizes people 55+, and expressly does not provide housing resources. It offers a concrete starting point for care coordination, with consent. A spring 2026 ADVSD Navigation Team pilot expands help accessing behavioral-health long-term supports; June 2026 guidance is linked publicly. Legacy reports its Adult Bridge Clinic, launched March 2025, had served over 260 people by May 14, 2026, providing post-hospital medication management/counseling.

**Proposal:** Draw housing and health care as parallel paths with a common accountable plan. Make case conferencing faster/more available for urgent transitions; create an escalation path for unresolved care needs; track completed appointments, medication continuity, ADL assistance, housing transfer/retention and client-reported fit. Mark the clinic's result as people served, not proven avoided admissions.

**Other claims needing revision:** The current page says hospital staff have no housing role/no HMIS access and hospitals have no in-reach. Those are sweeping institutional assertions not established by the reviewed sources. Describe the verified absence of any universal county service, coverage, or outcome reporting precisely; do not erase hospital social work, referral and discharge coordination, or existing community partnerships.

[Cross Sector Case Conferencing](https://hsd.multco.us/cross-sector-case-conferencing/). [Healthcare resources and spring 2026 Navigation Team](https://hsd.multco.us/healthcare-resources/). [Legacy expansion update, May 14, 2026](https://www.legacyhealth.org/about/news-and-media/story-center/2026/05/unity-center-and-legacy-health-expand).

## 12. Replace the public clinical/legal algorithm with safe systems routing

**Verified legal correction:** ORS 426.131 includes inability to provide for basic personal needs and specified chronic-mental-disorder criteria, as well as danger to self/others. Some statutory harm standards do not require imminent harm. The 2025 legislation took effect January 1, 2026. Therefore “below the danger standard nobody can hold anyone” is too simplistic. ORS 430.399 also allows jail custody under specified lack-of-appropriate-facility/rejection circumstances, contradicting the categorical sentence “Never a cell ... which is Oregon law.”

**Proposal:** The civic page can teach routing without becoming a field medical/commitment protocol: immediate safety/urgent professional assessment; household and confidentiality needs; housing options/affordability; functional support needs; suitable available offer; held/confirmed handoff; continuing support. Housing work continues while clinical care is arranged. Keep specific diagnosis, intoxication, withdrawal, medication timing and involuntary-authority decisions with qualified professionals and current protocols. If retained, the operational material needs named clinical/legal review and a revision date.

[Current ORS chapter 426](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors426.html). [OHA rules explaining January 1, 2026 effective date](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/HSD/HSDRules/TEMP_309-033-Civil_01012026.pdf). [ORS 430.399](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors430.html).

## Most useful original explanatory lines (editorial proposals, not quotations)

- “An available bed is not necessarily an accessible placement.”
- “A referral is a promise. Arrival is the first result. A lasting home is the outcome.”
- “The system needs to answer two questions at once: where can this person live, and what support makes that placement work?”
- “Unknown is a measurement failure—not evidence that someone succeeded or failed.”
- “Count what happens between the doors.”

## Visual and data specification worth building

1. **Two-minute system story:** A person or household reaches a first door; the viewer sees the housing path and service path in parallel, with real capacity/eligibility obstacles. No universal ladder where every person must pass through shelter, detox, treatment, and transitional housing.
2. **Failure overlay:** Supply, access, fit, handoff, retention, and data visibility. Each obstacle has observed evidence, owner, corrective action, and verification measure.
3. **Offer-to-outcome funnel:** One cohort, one period, one person-deduplication rule; retain pending, unknown and competing destinations. Use non-scaled arrows for conceptual routes without measured flow.
4. **Door matrix:** Open now / future capacity / not verified; who can enter; who refers; hours; transport; accessibility; clinical/support boundary. Never imply live availability from a directory.
5. **Durable-exit dashboard:** Confirmed move-in, 3/6/12-month outcomes, missing follow-up, observed returns, and unresolved service needs. Evaluate programs within comparable populations rather than rewarding easy-to-house selection.
6. **Action register:** Expand/repair existing problem-solving and navigation, flexible funds, landlord/subsidy access, individualized retention, cross-sector care support, and continuity at institutional transitions. Assign an owner and measurable next milestone to each proposal.
