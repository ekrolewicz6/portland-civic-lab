# What the first rebuild compressed too far

Reviewed September 8, 2026 against the original continuum data/components, the first rebuilt route, and the research evidence notes. This audit supports the next diagnostic-board revision; it does not justify restoring the original unsupported claims.

## Why the user’s concern is valid

The rebuilt page improves dates, denominators, cost labels, source honesty, and the relationship between housing and clinical care. However, it replaces a large amount of specific local diagnosis with generic explanations of how any homelessness system could fail.

The new SystemMap asks readers to select six types of barrier, but it has only three coarse locations. Selecting a different barrier often moves the same marker and changes the text, without revealing which actual part of Portland’s system is constrained or how. In particular, the “unmet support” evidence names a coordination program’s existence; that does not itself establish unmet support. The ReferenceAtlas retains definitions but removes most concrete local findings. Actual budget reductions are hidden in a cost tab. Execution and audit findings are almost absent. The page therefore explains the concept of a handoff more clearly than it diagnoses this system.

## Coverage of the original request

| User’s request | First rebuild | What should return |
|---|---|---|
| Visually understand where the system fails | Broad failure categories and a conceptual path | Named functions crossed with specific failure types, with visible evidence rather than only icons |
| Better facts and information | More defensible but much less detailed | Actual reported access restrictions, staffing examples, resource reductions, treatment transitions and audit findings |
| Actual current costs | Properly distinguished FY25 costs, 2026 rent benchmarks and FY27 budgets | Link funding decisions to the affected functions; show missing actual service costs as an investigation target |
| Better triage and placement | Three illustrative journeys explain fit | Which settings can meet which care/household needs, which access rules apply, and what remains unavailable or unmeasured |
| Where failures are and why | Often a general mechanism or unanswered question | State the documented failure, the plausible mechanism and the unanswered causal question separately |
| Missed opportunities | Useful but generic action agenda | Existing resources that lack coverage, access or attached housing funds; plus records that reveal how to repair them |

## A useful diagnostic board

Keep the short conceptual map, but make the diagnostic board the main evidence display. Use the familiar service functions as rows, grouped into prevention/access, care, temporary safety, housing, and retention. Columns should represent **capacity, workforce, funding, access, handoffs, execution, and outcomes**.

Each cell needs a short substantive label, not a colored dot alone. Examples: “Staffing limited operations,” “Referral only,” “Housing aid uneven,” “Monitoring unresolved,” or “No linked follow-up.” A selected cell opens its source, date, geography, what the finding does and does not show, responsible office, next decision, and the records needed to test it. Default to the strongest shelter/housing-resource finding so the user immediately sees a diagnosis.

Use three evidence states:

- **Documented constraint:** a dated provider rule, reported restriction, staffing reduction or audit finding. A rule can restrict access without being inappropriate; the unmet-need consequence may remain unmeasured.
- **Documented data gap:** the source explicitly reports missing outcomes, incomplete monitoring records or absent reporting. Otherwise say “not established in this review,” which is weaker.
- **Hypothesis to test:** a plausible reason for delay that needs records. A hypothesis should not become a red failure cell simply because it sounds likely.

Keep an unassessed cell distinct from all three. Do not total colored cells into a system grade, imply all sources describe the same date, or turn the matrix into a mandatory sequence through treatment and shelter.

## Valuable facts and distinctions to restore

### Workforce and housing-resource constraints

The County shelter review contains more than a missing-destinations statistic. It reports a specific operational capacity loss linked to staffing at Kenton Women’s Village early in FY25 (printed p25). A provider reported eliminating navigation specialist positions for FY26 and shifting housing work onto case managers (p80). Its discussion says housing-focused positions and services were cut or reduced for FY26 (p94). It also describes shortages of affordable units, subsidies and flexible move-in funds (pp84–85). These support concrete workforce/funding cells, with their historical periods visible. They do not establish that all navigation capacity disappeared.

[Adult Shelter Review FY25](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf).

### Care and the next placement

Restore the historical Hooper transition example: CCC reported 1,554 recommendations for residential treatment in 2022 and 264 placements at discharge, approximately 17%. Its staff also estimated some referred patients required a higher level of care. This identifies both a next-placement problem and a care-fit problem. It is not current performance; treatment capacity has changed since. Do not generalize the result into a requirement that everyone complete residential treatment before housing.

[CCC, November 12, 2024, reporting 2022 experience](https://centralcityconcern.org/blog/meeting-our-regions-need-for-more-treatment-beds/).

OHA’s June 2024 facility study can support a **historical capacity-versus-modeled-need** panel. Its Portland/North Coast geography is larger than the three metro counties. It explicitly says facilities, staffing and community/outpatient services all affect usable capacity. Never label its licensed inventory as current available beds.

[OHA Residential+ Facility Study](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/HSD/AMH/DataReports/Behavioral-Health-Residential-Facility-Study-June-2024.pdf).

### Published access is worth showing

The County’s current sobering page documents 13 places, voluntary referral-based access and care limits. Those are real access attributes. Its referral list includes Community Justice, so a universal “no jail-release path” claim remains unsupported.

[County sobering services, page reviewed July 13, 2026](https://multco.us/info/sobering-services).

Hooper’s published morning intake window should return with its full context: the page also describes standby/possible afternoon admission and an outpatient pathway. It is misleading to make a one-hour bar imply no other access. Provider criteria and intake channels can be visualized without giving medical directions.

[Hooper program information, checked September 8, 2026](https://centralcityconcern.org/recovery-location/hooper-detoxification-stabilization-center/).

Respite’s independence criteria belong in the main care-fit display. Someone who needs help with daily activities needs a suitable alternative; a standard respite place does not answer every hospital discharge need. Current city site changes also need effective dates: announced future inventory is not capacity available today. These findings and sources are in `placement-evidence.md` and `costs-evidence.md`.

### Execution and accountability

The April 21, 2026 County Auditor memorandum is a concrete execution anchor. The Auditor says recommended higher-frequency monitoring of Sunstone Way had not occurred to the recommended degree, and again raises the conflict between advocating for providers and overseeing performance. The memorandum connects unresolved recommendations with service-disruption risk. Attribute these findings to the Auditor; do not claim this establishes the sole cause of a closure or homelessness outcomes.

[April 21, 2026 Auditor memorandum](https://multco.us/file/memorandum_to_board_of_county_commissioners:_request_for_advocacy_to_implement_auditor_recommendations/download).

The February 2025 follow-up gives a historical benchmark of one recommendation implemented, five in process and two not implemented. Also show the completed improvement: three contract specialists had been hired. That is a better diagnostic than saying there is no oversight. The age of the status evaluation must remain visible.

[February 2025 recommendation follow-up](https://multco.us/info/recommendation-status-evaluation-joint-office-homeless-services-has-implemented-one-2023-audit).

The housing-data audit likewise found corrected move-in-date reporting and generally faithful aggregation, alongside burdensome duplicate data entry. This supports a specific execution/workforce problem without erasing the improvement.

[County housing-data audit](https://multco.us/info/johs-reporting-supportive-housing-and-rapid-rehousing-was-accurate-reflection-provider-data).

## Turn unanswered questions into investigations

The new `InvestigationRequests` component supplies seven selectable, copyable requests: capacity, staffing, outreach, handoffs, housing/funding, execution and lasting outcomes. Each specifies the question, likely holder and exact records. Public aggregates and redacted documents are requested; no individual client, staff, medical or location records. Clipboard copying does not send a request.

Link every uncertain diagnostic to this section. The gap should lead to a concrete inquiry, not the generic conclusion that someone should collect more data. Published access rules establish restrictions; records of turn-aways establish their observed consequences. Filled positions and caseloads distinguish workforce capacity from budget authority. Linked referrals distinguish supply failures from transport and contact failures. Monitoring records distinguish promised reforms from completed work.

The next version should restore this depth in the visible story while preserving the first rebuild’s corrections. A clean design can carry a substantive diagnosis; brevity should come from hierarchy and visual structure rather than deleting the facts the user came to understand.
