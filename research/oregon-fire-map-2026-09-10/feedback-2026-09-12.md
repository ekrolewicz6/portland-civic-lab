# September 12 feedback: forest condition and fire effects

Current public co-authors: Edan Krolewicz and Dominic Kuklawood. Public credits and current outreach drafts were updated at the project owner's request. Earlier archived research is a historical snapshot, not the current author list. No outreach has been sent.

## Proposed next research questions

Readers would benefit from a view of forest extent, recent fire scars, and documented ecological effects, including effects of wildfire. These are research questions; no statewide “percent good burned” or target of burning 100% of Oregon forest has been adopted.

1. Define the denominator: current forest land, tree canopy, or a specific ecosystem with a documented restoration objective. Choose an explicit reference year and geography. Investigate FIA forest-area estimates and LANDFIRE vegetation layers; do not substitute forest cover for ecological condition.
2. Add a recent-fire view with severity and time since fire. Keep unassessed areas explicit. Existing perimeters identify event extent, not uniform effects within them.
3. Seek locally reviewed outcome evidence. Separate reported management intent, observed vegetation change, and documented ecological benefit. Include wildfire where evidence supports it; do not automatically equate prescribed fire or low severity with ecological success.
4. Design progress measures only after defining the objective, eligible area, time window, evidence standards, uncertainty, and repeat-treatment accounting. Spatially union relevant areas before calculating a percentage; do not add overlapping treatment acreage or count repeated ignitions as new land.
5. Start with a reviewed pilot landscape and separate indicators for forest extent, mapped fire exposure, severity, and documented outcomes. Statewide extrapolation remains a later decision.

## Source leads checked September 12

- [MTBS FAQ](https://www.mtbs.gov/faqs): mapping describes severity within large-fire perimeters and has a 1,000-acre western size threshold. Its severity measures emphasize remotely detectable vegetation change; the FAQ specifically cautions against interpreting low severity after reburning as ecological integrity. Smaller burns and longer-term outcomes need other evidence.
- [LANDFIRE Existing Vegetation Type](https://landfire.gov/vegetation/evt): vegetation context lead; no raster was downloaded or analyzed in this follow-up.
- FIA forest inventory remains an acquisition lead. The attempted Forest Service program URL could not be retrieved in this check; no current Oregon area estimate is asserted.
