In 2025, City of Portland (City) engaged Hunden Partners (Hunden) to 
complete a market feasibility analysis to determine whether the regional 
cultural and entertainment ecosystem can support two Broadway-capable 
performing arts venues located in downtown Portland, Oregon (Project): one 
at the current Keller Auditorium (Keller) site and another at a proposed site 
at Portland State University (PSU). The result of that study determined there 
was insufficient market support for two Broadway-capable venues in 
Portland.
As the City now considers a path forward, Hunden was engaged to develop 
projected stabilized demand, revenue, and economic impact projections for 
both scenarios, both assumed to be publicly-managed facilities.
§ Renovated Keller with an assumed 3,000-seat capacity
§ PSU venue with the 3,200-seat capacity recommended in Hunden’s 
prior study
The purpose of this analysis is to estimate how a renovated Keller would 
perform compared to a new PSU venue. 
In order to be more directly comparative, this analysis does not consider the 
performance or impacts of the additional components of the proposed PSU 
performing arts and mixed-use campus.
This initial analysis also does not consider the short-term impacts of closing 
the Keller, costs to build either venue, or ongoing expenses to operate these 
facilities. Hunden recommends this be a next step to look at the full picture 
of the impact of moving forward with either scenario.

Hunden also evaluated the impacts new venues had on Broadway performance. Salt 
Lake City and Orlando both developed new Broadway-capable venues in the 2010s 
that replaced older venues being utilized for Broadway shows.
The impact was significant in both cases. Broadway subscriptions increased 
significantly with the opening of the new venues and have consistently maintained 
those elevated levels.
Both venues have increased capacities compared to the older venues, and despite 
this increase witnessed a significant bump in average occupancy of Broadway 
shows, going from 75 to 83 percent occupancy on average to more than 93 
percent. 
Average ticket prices also increased for Broadway events, increasing 11 to 38 
percent on average. 
Overall attendance also increased significantly, due not only to an increase in 
occupancy, but also due to an increase of Broadway content that was hosted at 
these venues. These, in turn, drove a significant increase in ticket sales compared to 
the prior venues. 
Hunden also looked into renovated venues; however, these projects were complete 
restorations of 100+ year old historic venues, and not comparable to the situation in 
Portland.

Two new concert and live entertainment (commercial) venues are proposed in 
Portland. As these venues have the potential of pulling commercial content from 
the Keller, an evaluation of commercial content at the Keller was completed.
Commercial events at the Keller in FY25 totaled 25 performances. Of these 
events, 56 percent were concerts, which could be accommodated at the new 
commercial venues. However, when evaluating types of concerts, 93 percent of 
these acts typically utilized fixed-seat theaters and are unlikely to move to the 
new flat floor venues.
Based on the types of historical acts hosted at the Keller, Hunden does not 
expect the new commercial venues to have a material negative impact on the 
Keller or PSU venueto attract this level of commercial demand. 
As the City considers who may manage either of these venues in the future, 
while dependent on the actual management team in place, private operators 
typically bring in additional commercial events through their network of touring 
shows and promoters. The level of Broadway and other content types are 
expected to be similar regardless of public or private management.

Hunden developed stabilized demand projections for both the 
proposed PSU venue at the recommended 3,200-seat capacity 
and the renovated Keller assumed to achieve a 3,000-seat 
capacity.
Aside from PSU events, it is assumed both venues would be able 
to generate the same number of events and at the same average 
occupancies. Broadway events are projected to include 11 single 
weekend shows, and one blockbuster running three weekends, for 
a total of 112 performances. Other event types are assumed to 
generate a similar level of performances as historical levels.
Due to new and expanded lobby spaces at both venues, it is 
assumed these facilities will also generate a significant number of 
banquets and receptions annually.
Based on the performance of other new Broadway-capable 
venues, it is assumed that in both scenarios, Broadway occupancy 
would average 95 percent. In the case of PSU, this estimate is 
above 3,000 seats, and certain events, like blockbusters, are 
assumed to carry occupancies nearing 100 percent. Other event 
types are assumed to have a slight lift in historical occupancy, 
ranging from 65 to 70 percent
Total tickets sold is estimated at nearly 500,000 for the PSU and 
460,000 for the Keller. Based on an estimated attendance ratio of 
92 percent of tickets sold, total actual attendance is estimated to 
exceed460,000 for PSU, and 430,000 for the Keller, an increase 
of 8 percent for PSU.

Based on the demand projections, Hunden developed 
stabilized operating revenue projections for both venues, in 
today’s dollars. Due to the venues being new or renovatedand 
based on the price lift at other new venues, a 15- to 20-
percent increase in average ticket price, and a 10- to 15-
percent increase in facility rental was assumed.
Due to expectations of expanded offerings, points of sale, and 
increased circulation, food and beverage and merchandise per 
caps were increased from historical levels.
Other revenue types not based on a percentage of ticket sales 
or fixed fees are assumed to have a 10-percent increase from 
historical levels due to the facilities being new or renovated. 
In total, the PSU venue is projected to generate stabilized 
annual operating revenues of approximately $15.3 million, 
compared to $12.3 million for the Keller, an increase of 
approximately 12 percent for PSU. 
The PSU project is expected to have 740 on-site parking spaces. 
Hunden assumed that parking revenues charged for events would 
be retained by PSU and are not included in these revenue 
projections. If these revenues do accrue to the venue, they would 
be significant (estimated at ~$1.4 million annually).

Introduction to Impacts
Hunden used the IMPLAN input-output multiplier model, which determines the level of additional activity in the economy due to additional inputs. For 
example, for every dollar of direct spending, the IMPLAN model provides multipliers for the indirect and induced spending that will result. 
On- and off-site spending from visitors to either venue is considered to be the Direct Impact. 
From the direct spending figures, further impact analysis was completed. 
§ Indirect Impacts are the supply of goods and services resulting from direct spending. For example, a visitor’s direct expenditure on a hotel room causes 
the hotel to purchase linens and other items from suppliers. These purchases are considered an indirect economic impact.
§ Induced Impacts embody the change in spending due to the personal expenditures by employees whose incomes are affected by direct and indirect 
spending. For example, a waitress at a restaurant may have more personal income as a result of an attendee’s visit. The amount of the increased income 
that the employee spends is considered an induced impact. 
§ Employment Impacts include the incremental employment due to spending. Direct, indirect and induced spending support new and ongoing businesses, 
and ultimately result in ongoing employment. Hunden will show the number of ongoing jobs supported by each venue and provide the estimated income 
generated. 
§ Fiscal Impacts represent the incremental tax revenue collected due to direct spending. The fiscal impact represents the government’s share of total 
economic benefit.

§ Hunden projects that both the PSU venue and the renovated Keller would generate increased visitation, facility revenues, and economic impacts compared to the 
current Keller. While the number of performances projected in either scenario is similar, the larger new PSU venue is expected to achieve a lift of 8 percent in 
attendance, 12 percent in operating revenues, and 16 percent in economic impacts compared to a renovated Keller.
§ This analysis only considers the performance and impacts of the larger Broadway-capable venue at PSU. The additional components of the PSU performing arts 
campus, including a 1,200-seat venue, hotel, conference, office and educational space, and restaurant are expected to generate significant additional impacts to 
Portland if developed.
§ This initial analysis does not consider the short-term impacts of closing the Keller. A closure of the Keller is estimated to cause the loss of direct spending by more 
than $50 million per year and negatively impact other P’5 performing arts organizations that rely on the Keller to be sustainable.
§ A multi-year closure of Keller also risks eroding Portland’s Broadway audience and forcing skilled labor to relocate from Portland to other citiesfor employment. 
Unlike the continuity that the PSU scenario allows for, a closure of the Keller will also likely result in a ramp-up period after opening to achieving stabilization, 
compared to an expected immediate lift in performance moving straight from the Keller to the new venue at PSU.
§ A temporary/semi-permanent venue could also be considered to maintain continuity while the Keller is renovated. The site, performance, cost, and impact of 
developing such a venue, as well as the expected number of years such a venue would be in operation and in demand would need to be evaluated to determine the 
value of such a pursuit.
§ It is anticipated that the new PSU venue will cost more to develop than renovating the Keller. A more definitive physical program of both venues and associated costs 
to develop either venue would need to be determined to better understand public funding needs and additional advantages or disadvantages of moving forward with 
either scenario.
§ The proximity of the proposed PSU venue to the Keller (0.5 miles) is not expected to have a significant negative impact on downtown activity. Rather, the 
development of the PSU campus is expected to increase activation of the downtown area.
Implications
Hunden Recommendation: Develop a 3,200-seat capacity Broadway capable venue at PSU.
