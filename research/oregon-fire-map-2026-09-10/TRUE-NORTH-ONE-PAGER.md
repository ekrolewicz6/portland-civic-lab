# Fire in Oregon: from plan to work

Portland Civic Lab · Edan Krolewicz and Dominic Kuklawood · September 30, 2026

Oregonians can see fire perimeters and some treatment records. It is harder to answer a practical question: **what would it take to carry out and maintain the work identified for a particular landscape, and how would we tell if it helped?**

We are building a public visual guide and a deeply documented investigation of one Oregon landscape. The guide will explain how different fires affect different places, why managers burn or thin, what communities can do, and how to interpret results. The investigation will follow a local program from objectives and decisions through funding, people, contracts, completed work, maintenance and monitoring.

The first landscape will be chosen from Ashland/Rogue Valley, Central Oregon/Deschutes, and Wallowa/northeastern Oregon based on the availability of actual financial, activity and monitoring records and an accessible custodian. We will not choose a place because its story is convenient.

Our first deliverable will include an illustrated landscape page, a transparent activity and spending account, and a short brief about what remains to be done. A reader will be able to see what is recorded, what is estimated, and what remains unknown. The work will be useful to educators and residents while giving practitioners and funders a way to correct or build on the evidence.

**The most helpful first contribution:** one completed project with its existing boundary, decision or project summary, activity dates, attributable expenditure or contract record, and monitoring or maintenance notes. Partial records are useful. A brief written clarification can often answer more than a long meeting.
