# Climate Protection Program case status (checked September 28, 2026)

Prompted by suggested copy from Juan Muñoz, Oregon Communications Director at Climate Solutions (email, September 28, 2026). Climate Solutions is one of the six intervenors defending the program, so the copy was checked against independent sources before use.

## Suggested copy (verbatim)

> The Climate Protection Program helps Oregon meet its greenhouse gas reduction goals. The program establishes a declining cap, or limit, on greenhouse gas emissions from fossil fuels used throughout Oregon. The program is designed to reduce these emissions 50% by 2035 and 90% by 2050. Nearly 30 business, utility, and labor groups asked the Court of Appeals on April 16, 2026 to strike it down. A coalition of environmental justice, climate, and business organizations moved to legally defend the program on June 16, 2026.

## What was used and what was not

- Used: the plain description of a declining cap on emissions from fossil fuels used in Oregon; the 2035 and 2050 targets (already on the board, from DEQ); the April 16, 2026 challenge (already on the board, from OPB); the existence of the defense side.
- Not used: "helps Oregon meet its greenhouse gas reduction goals" (asserts effectiveness, which is what the case and the candidates dispute); the June 16 date.
- Added from independent reporting: the court's rulings on intervention and expedition, which are the current status.

## Sources

- Motion date: Oregon Environmental Council's post of the coalition release is dated June 12, 2026 and says the groups filed "this morning": https://oeconline.org/cpp-interveners-release/ . Climate Solutions posted the same release (byline Ally Harris) on June 15, 2026: https://www.climatesolutions.org/article/2026-06/oregon-groups-move-intervene-lawsuit-defend-climate-protection-program-against-oil . The suggested copy said June 16. The board gives no motion date.
- Intervenors: Beyond Toxics, Climate Solutions, Oregon Business for Climate, Oregon Environmental Council, Rogue Climate and Verde, represented by Crag Law Center (both releases).
- Court rulings: Capital Press, "Court agrees to expedite lawsuit over Oregon's climate rules," Mateusz Perkowski, July 29, 2026: the Court of Appeals granted the petitioners' motion to expedite (over the Environmental Quality Commission's opposition) with opening brief by mid-September, state response by mid-November and reply by mid-December, and found it "appropriate to permit intervention" by the six groups. Petitioners include the Oregon Farm Bureau and individual farms; OEC describes the suit as "led by Oregon Business & Industry." https://capitalpress.com/2026/07/29/court-agrees-to-expedite-lawsuit-over-oregons-climate-rules/
- First version voided on procedure: the intervenors' filing, as quoted by Capital Press, says the original program "was invalidated due to a procedural issue" without a ruling on "the substantive issues" (Oregon Court of Appeals, December 2023).
- April 16, 2026 challenge by "nearly 30 business, utility and labor groups": OPB, https://www.opb.org/article/2026/04/16/oregon-climate-protection-program-lawsuit/ (the board's existing source).
