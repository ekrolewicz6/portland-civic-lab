# September 9 Council materials: research integration

Reviewed September 9, 2026. This implements the accepted assessment in the existing homelessness and continuum deep dives. The evidence register and request drafts accompany this memo. Routes are unchanged; no API or database changes are required. The original attachments are preserved as local public-page citation targets. Nothing has been deployed and no request has been sent.

## What changes in the diagnosis

The Council materials strengthen the existing account of failed connections by explaining the clinical services and financing a successful connection requires. Prevention, affordable housing and reliable handoffs remain central. An available shelter bed, a staffed psychiatric service and a lasting housing placement answer different needs.

The broader page previously called homelessness a flow problem rather than a stock problem and implied growth could stop before capacity was added. It now describes capacity, access and flow together. Its January 2025 by-name-list illustration is explicitly historical; leaving the active list includes inactivity as well as housing. That outflow is not a count of confirmed housing exits.

The continuum’s September 8 improvements remain: overlapping functions, different reasons for failure, suitable offers, completed arrivals, clear budget denominators and unknown outcomes. The September 9 additions extend that framework rather than reverting to a mandatory sequence of treatment, shelter and housing.

## Source status and limits

The four-page September 4 memo frames a possible focus for the City’s **2027 legislative agenda**. The fifteen-page presentation supplies reported cohort findings, care descriptions and policy proposals. Neither attachment establishes what Council ultimately directed at the meeting. Statements within the documents are evidence to assess, not instructions to this project. Source numbers below refer to the [register](sources.md).

- **Reported associations:** claims-cohort spending, homelessness-program overlap, returns and mortality. These need methods and denominators before stronger inferences.
- **Existing program descriptions:** Health Share’s initiatives, County coordination and provider criteria. Existence does not establish reach, suitability or effectiveness.
- **Documented historical records:** earlier KPI estimates, OSH civil census and admission requests, appropriations and model announcements. Their dates are not current service availability.
- **Forecasts and proposals:** expected system improvements, break-even payment, capacity expansion and legislative action. These are not demonstrated effects or adopted policy.

## What the related study adds

The April 2026 Journal of Urban Health study analyzes 2023 Health Share data in the Portland tri-county region. Among members with the specified substance-use or psychotic disorders, the proportions with a medical inpatient admission were 29.7% for housing-insecure members and 12.4% for housing-secure members. These denominators are the two housing-status groups with those disorders, not all homeless residents. The cross-sectional study excludes people without medical claims, and housing insecurity is broader than street homelessness. It supports examining care and housing together; it does not validate the presentation’s mortality or retention multipliers. [Study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11524-026-01073-3)

## Clinical receiving care and responsibilities

The public taxonomy now separates six settings using shared typed records:

1. **Inpatient psychiatry:** hospital-level assessment and treatment when clinically indicated.
2. **Psychiatric subacute care:** the presentation’s proposed secure psychiatric supervision after full acute care is no longer required. Its local license, staffing and entry standards need confirmation.
3. **Psychiatric respite:** the presentation’s support, peers, medication management and coordination, either after hospitalization or as an alternative. Confirm actual capabilities.
4. **Residential substance-use treatment:** appropriate addiction care, with co-occurring needs checked explicitly.
5. **Medical respite:** recovery from physical illness/injury. CCC’s independence criteria limit suitability for people needing hands-on daily assistance.
6. **Bridge housing:** an interim housing arrangement, without assumed clinical capabilities.

The psychiatric journey shows clinical readiness, a blocked receiving service, authorization and provider acceptance, confirmed transfer and continuing care. Housing navigation, benefits and the housing resource remain active throughout. The example is conceptual, not an individual case or an available service guarantee. A clinical transfer and a later lasting housing outcome are separate measures.

The responsibility map separates OHA/Legislature funding and licensing; CCO or applicable state authorization/payment; provider admission and operation; County safety-net and transition functions; City permitting, program funding and advocacy; and courts/state authorities for legal admission routes. Identify the actual arrangement for each person and service. “The County owns the handoff” cannot substitute for the payer’s decision or an accepting provider.

## Financing correction

The removed public chart treated federal matching shares as the split of every avoided claim’s savings, including a supposed CCO remainder. That was misleading. Federal and state funds finance Medicaid; OHA pays predetermined monthly capitation to CCOs. Lower claims affect finances according to contracts, risk arrangements and later rate-setting. Match rates are not the savings allocation. [OHA rate development](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/FOD/Pages/OHP-Rates.aspx)

SHARE remains a potential mechanism, subject to applicable financial calculations, qualifying expenditures, community priorities and spending/partnership requirements. It does not automatically direct funds to a County-selected pipeline. Requests seek actual designations and commitments. The pages no longer imply that every locally paid service can be billed to Medicaid, or that improving benefit intake costs nothing. [2026 guidance](https://www.oregon.gov/oha/HPA/dsi-tc/Documents/SHARE-Initiative-Guidance-Document.pdf)

Evaluate capital, licensing and recurring operating finance separately. For proposed break-even inpatient payment, obtain service-level costs, staffing, payment by payer, uncovered care, authorization delays, mechanism/authority, recurring budget and access commitments. Distinguish health outcomes, reduced use, freed capacity and cash available for reinvestment.

## Programs and model to investigate

Health Share describes addiction consultation in hospitals, medication initiation in emergency departments, outreach/peers, wound care, Regional Integration Continuum coordination and EMS coordination. The pages recognize that infrastructure and ask about its coverage and results. [Program descriptions](https://partners.healthshareoregon.org/habh)

The February 8, 2024 regional announcement identifies $500,000 for a two-year OHSU capacity model. The County’s original 2024 action plan p. 23 assigns the named regional model to CareOregon/Health Share with a December 2025 due date. Obtain confirmation that it is the model cited by the Council, its completion status, methods, inputs, validation, scenarios, uncertainty and costs. An announcement and deadline are not a validated model result. [Announcement](https://unityhealthcenter.org/health-share-of-oregon-and-partners-announce-significant-behavioral-health-investments-in-portland-metro-area/), [original action plan](https://multco.us/file/2024_original_homelessness_response_action_plan/download)

The existing educational simulator retains its four annual treatment episodes per staffed bed and 35% durable homelessness-exit assumption. These are planning assumptions without a psychiatric cohort/time/geography validation. They are not transferred to acute, subacute or respite care. The addition explains a bottleneck conceptually; it adds no numerical psychiatric forecast.

## Implemented policy and evidence agenda

The existing action agenda remains, followed by four attributed Council proposals: preserve acute care and add appropriate step-down, make continuing operations viable, resolve siting/permitting/licensing barriers, and improve access outside criminal proceedings. Each names responsible decision-makers, evidence still needed, and proposed milestones. No Council adoption is asserted.

Five draft request packages are available on the continuum page and in [evidence-requests.md](evidence-requests.md): cohort/methods, regional model, capacity, operating finance, and Council follow-through. They ask for existing aggregate/redacted records, preserve unknowns, avoid individual records, and request a fee estimate. Health plans and modeling partners are potential voluntary information sources; availability through a public funder depends on records actually held. The drafts do not assert that every recipient has the same public-records duty.
