SUBJECT:  Performing Arts Center 
Summary – Transportation Study Findings 
This memorandum marks the conclusion of our initial study of the three potential performing arts 
scenarios, including the renovation of the Keller site, the construction of a new PSU site, and 
combined uses at both locations.  
In summary, this study did not identify any fatal flaws for either site but did identify various 
opportunities and challenges with potential considerations for future site refinement to 
enable site function.  
SUMMARY OF FINDINGS  
1. Both the renovation of the Keller site and the construction of a new PSU site share two
common benefits:
a. the traffic activity generally occurs outside of key congestion periods; and
b. the central location within the region means that patron travel will be spread more evenly
among regional travel routes.
Key Takeaway—This decreases the opportunity for significant negative traffic impacts (such as 
congestion, bottlenecks, closures, etc.). 
2. We collected information to help us learn how a new performing arts center might affect
traffic. This includes how many cars are already on nearby roads, how many more car trips
the center would cause, what time those trips would happen, and which roads people would
use.
Key Takeaway— This new data can be used to support detailed traffic analysis for either site.
3. The Keller site is already in operation and is not expected to add more traffic to the system.
However, the PSU site would bring new trip activity, either by replacing the Keller site or adding
to it. Portland City Code 17.88.050 requires traffic studies for development that exceeds 100
vehicle trips during an hour in the peak direction. This threshold would be triggered if a new

Key takeaway—Additional coordination with PBOT and ODOT staff would be needed to

RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS  
 
1. Lane Reduction Pilot Project at Existing Keller Site—Coordinate with PBOT staff to implement 
a pilot project that temporarily reduces the number of lanes on SW 3rd Avenue to one travel

duration and implementation of this pilot should be determined by traffic engineers at PBOT. 
2. Incorporate Traffic Impact Reduction Treatments at PSU Site as Project Design Advances—
As site design advances, consider incorporating treatments that would reduce the potential 
for vehicle queuing on the SW 4th Avenue off-ramp. Additional coordination with PBOT and 
ODOT staff would be needed to determine the specific requirements of a traffic study that may 
be needed for the PSU site to address the requirements of Portland City Code 17.88.050. 
 
Thank you for this opportunity to advance the understanding of the potential transportation 
impacts and needs for a Performing Arts Center.

SUBJECT:  Performing Arts Center 
Transportation Study 
 
 
This document summarizes a high-level transportation study that was conducted to identify key 
issues and needs for further review and evaluation of three potential Performing Arts Center 
scenarios in downtown Portland. This high-level analysis is based on readily available data to better 
understand the transportation system and its current conditions, opportunities, and constraints for 
the three scenarios.  
This analysis is not intended to represent a full transportation study for specific uses at any 
location, and each potential scenario will require more study if it is recommended for further 
consideration by stakeholders. This study did not identify any fatal flaws for either site but 
did identify various opportunities and challenges with potential considerations for future 
site refinement to enable site function. The information summarized in this document may be 
leveraged to support the future additional transportation study related to site development. 
The following sections summarize the key analysis findings. Additional technical data is included in 
the appendix. 
GENERAL INFORMATION  
• Scope of Study: Three potential scenarios were considered: 
• Scenario 1: Renovate the existing Keller Auditorium 
• Scenario 2: Construct a new venue at Portland State University (PSU) 
• Scenario 3: Renovate the existing Keller Auditorium (Scenario 1) + Construct a new 
venue at PSU (Scenario 2) 
• Data Collected: Data was collected to inform and better understand the impacts and needs 
of a venue, regardless of specific location. This data is applicable for each scenario, with more 
information provided in the appendix. 
• Patron trip generation – Patrons were surveyed upon arriving at a Keller event to 
determine the type of travel mode and size of their group. This information is important

to estimate the amount of vehicular traffic that could be added to the transportation 
system for a new venue. The survey of approximately 320 patrons indicated that there 
is approximately one vehicle added to the system for every 2.6 patrons. This and the 
related survey data are needed to estimate the trip generation for the site since national 
data from other sources is limited. 
• Patron trip distribution – Sales zip codes for an event (SIX - The Musical) were used to 
estimate where patrons were traveling from. This information is important to understand 
the potential impact and added traffic on regional roads. This was a non-member event 
and member event distribution may vary. The information for Six indicated: 
• 22 percent of patrons travel to/from the north of downtown Portland (e.g., N/NE 
Portland, Vancouver) 
• 36 percent to/from the south of downtown Portland (e.g., SW Portland, Tigard, Lake 
Oswego) 
• 21 percent to/from the east of downtown Portland (e.g., E/SE Portland, Happy Valley) 
• 21 percent to/from the west of downtown Portland (e.g., NW Portland, Beaverton, 
Hillsboro) 
• Parking Arrival and Duration data – A mix of parking summary and raw data was 
obtained for several private and public garages in the area. Function of the ticketing 
systems limits some of the data that is available. However, based on a review of the 200 
Market event parking data, it appears that approximately 20 percent of parking patrons 
arrive over an hour before the show time, while the remaining 80 percent arrive within 
the hour of the show. This data is important to understand the portion of event traffic 
that is added to the roadway system before a show. 
• Traffic volumes – Traffic volume data was collected along streets in the vicinity of the 
Keller Auditorium and proposed PSU venues to better understand current travel 
patterns. The data included turn movement volumes at intersections and average daily 
traffic volumes along street segments. 
• General Use Opportunities: The following benefits are applicable to both locations studied: 
• Location – Being centrally located within the region in downtown Portland allows traffic 
to arrive and disperse from a variety of directions and facilities. 
• Use Type – The typical programming for events and facility use (e.g., weekday 
evenings) is not during peak traffic system congestion periods.  
• Future Needs: This high-level transportation study identifies key issues and needs for 
further review and evaluation.  
• This is not a full traffic study for any potential scenario. Each potential scenario will 
require more study if it is recommended for further consideration by stakeholders. 
SCENARIO 1: RENOVATE THE EXISTING KELLER  AUDITORIUM  
• The existing Keller Auditorium remains in its current location, bounded by SW 3rd Avenue, 
SW Market Street, SW 2nd Avenue, and SW Clay Street. 
• The venue is renovated, although the capacity remains at 3,000 seats.

CURRENT CONDITIONS  
• The existing trips are already served by the transportation system, and no changes are 
expected in that estimated demand. 
• During a sold-out event, the current venue generates an estimated: 
• 923 pre-event peak and 981 post-event peak motor vehicle trips 
• 144 pre-event peak and 153 post-event peak transit, walking, or biking trips 
• The transportation system generally functions adequately with the current arrival and 
departure patterns for patrons1. 
• Existing vehicular parking is abundant in the area, with over 3,000 spaces located within a 
one-block walk and an additional 2,000 spaces within five blocks. 
KEY ISSU ES 
• Peak event flows for patrons creates congestion along frontages adjacent to the venue 
entrances along SW 3rd Avenue and SW Clay Street. 
• Pedestrians walking from nearby parking garages are frequently using crosswalks on SW 
Market Street and SW Clay Street at SW 3rd Avenue, and conflicting with vehicular traffic 
along these streets. With adjacent parking garages located kitty-corner from the Keller 
venue, pedestrians typically cross two legs of the intersection to arrive at the front door. 
POTENTIAL OPPORTUNITIES  
• Changing the configuration of SW 3rd Avenue between SW Clay Street and SW Market Street 
could be considered to accommodate peak event pedestrian flows.  
• SW 3rd Avenue currently has two travel lanes and a lane for loading along both the east 
and west block faces. Potential alternative configurations for SW 3rd Avenue along this 
segment include: 
• Option 1: Close SW 3rd Avenue to vehicular traffic permanently 
• Option 2: Close SW 3rd Avenue to vehicular traffic only during events 
• Option 3: Narrow SW 3rd Avenue to one travel lane, but always keep one-lane open 
for vehicular traffic 
• This block of SW 3rd Avenue is the last block before the street ends at SW Market Street 
OVERALL FINDINGS  
• Option 3 (narrow to one travel lane) is recommended for further consideration. It is expected 
to have a minimal impact on the surrounding transportation system throughout a typical day, 
and current arrival and departure patterns for patrons during events.

turn onto Market Street. If congestion and vehicles queued on SW Market Street limit

SCENARIO 2: CONSTRUCT A NEW VENUE AT PSU  
• Construct a new Performing Arts Center along SW Lincoln Street just east of SW 4th Avenue. 
• Two venues are proposed with a total capacity of 4,200 seats: 
• 3,000 seat performing arts center facility  
• 1,200 seat event venue 
CURRENT CONDITIONS  
• Since the use does not currently exist, these venues would add additional traffic 
• During sold-out events, the proposed venues are expected to generate an estimated: 
• 1,474 pre-event peak and 1,566 post-event peak motor vehicle trips 
• 202 pre-event peak and 214 post-event peak transit, walking, or biking trips 
• Existing traffic volume data at the SW 4th Avenue/SW Lincoln Street intersection during the 
anticipated pre-event peak hour (i.e., 6:30-7:30 p.m.) indicated that utilization is minimal.

• This included 348 vehicles on the northbound I-405 exit ramp and SW 4th Avenue

• Existing observations indicate that the SW 4th Avenue/SW Lincoln Street intersection operates

KEY ISSUES

Traffic spilling back onto I-405 from the SW 4th Avenue off ramp is likely the most critical 
traffic issue to be addressed and will require additional study and coordination with ODOT.

would potentially add 374 trips to the I-405 ramp during the pre-arrival peak hour. 
Additional traffic analysis would be required to determine the potential impacts to the 
ramp and potential for vehicle queues to reach I-405 mainline. 
• The remainder of these trips from the south (i.e., 25% or 125 pre-arrival peak hour trips) 
would likely use SW Naito Parkway or S Harbor Drive to reach the proposed PSU venues 
and nearby parking. 
• There is potential for conflicts between pedestrians and vehicular traffic at the pedestrian 
crossing on SW Lincoln Street adjacent to the Lincoln Street/SW 3rd Avenue MAX stop. 
POTENTIAL OPPORTUNITIES  
• Various opportunities could be considered to improve vehicle flow to the site and manage the 
potential for traffic queues on the I-405 off-ramp.  
• SW Lincoln Street Site Access 
• Locate the site access as far as possible to the east along the site frontage to 
maximize queue storage potential 
• Add an eastbound right turn lane to enter the site and avoid blocking through 
traffic on SW Lincoln Street 
• Provide an additional curb loading lane on SW Lincoln Street for drop off

• Consider alternative signal timing and/or turn restrictions at the intersection, 
including the potential restriction of westbound left turn movements during and 
before events 
• Maximize vehicle queue storage and maintain circulation on site to reduce 
opportunity for vehicles entering the site to spill back to SW Lincoln Street 
• Consider treatments to manage the conflicts between pedestrians crossing SW 
Lincoln Street and vehicles entering the site 
• SW 4th Avenue at Lincoln Street 
• Consider opportunities to modify the intersection to improve flow for the right

• Since the use does not currently exist, these venues would add additional traffic. However, 
traffic would be arriving from multiple directions and locations and could likely be managed. 
• The SW 4th Avenue off-ramp from I-405 will require additional study and coordination with 
ODOT to address concerns about potential vehicle queue spillback onto the I-405 mainline. 
• Opportunities to manage the potential for vehicle queues on the I-405 ramp were identified 
and could be considered through the site refinement process. The feasibility and effectiveness 
of each option will likely depend on other site conditions and constraints.
