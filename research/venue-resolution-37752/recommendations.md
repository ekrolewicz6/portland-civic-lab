# Venue portfolio: Resolution 37752 review and recommended revisions

Reviewed September 9, 2026. Scope: the adopted resolution, all 15 linked PDFs (442 pages), and the available captions for the September 9 Council video. This is a source review and editorial recommendation, not an engineering opinion or a construction-cost audit. No website code was changed.

## Main conclusion

The deep dive should shift from a binary “PSU versus Keller” decision to the obligations created by a possible transition: a new Broadway hall at PSU, continued operation and maintenance of Keller until replacement capacity is ready, possible conversion of Keller, and continued support for the other Portland’5 theaters. Keeping a single major Broadway operation is a coherent objective. It does not remove the cost of retaining or repurposing Keller, or automatically preserve the subsidy that Broadway provides to other venues.

The strongest affirmative argument for PSU is continuity: retaining touring shows, employment, and revenue while providing a modern accessible venue. The strongest unresolved issue is whether the full portfolio can afford the capital and operating obligations under an enforceable agreement. The adopted resolution advances that investigation; it does not answer it.

## 1. Correct the live status and explain the approval gates

[Resolution 37752](https://www.portland.gov/council/documents/resolution/adopted/37752) was adopted 8–4 on September 9, 2026. It advances planning and predevelopment for a potential City-owned, approximately 3,000-seat Broadway-capable hall at PSU, targeting 2030, alongside feasibility planning for a 1,200–1,800-seat Keller.

The December 1, 2026 milestone is a Project Commitment Agreement. It is not an adopted $44 million appropriation or construction authorization. The amended resolution requires a detailed financial plan and further Council approval before financial obligations; it permits work within existing staff capacity and previously approved resources. A goal date is not a guaranteed opening date.

Explain the amendments rather than reproducing the original proposal:

- Avalos 1 made the direction conditional planning rather than an unconditional construction commitment.
- Avalos 2a removed the return-on-invested-capital requirement and added the fiscal constraints and further approval requirement.
- Avalos 3 made the agreement propose a funding strategy rather than commit its implementation.
- Clark-Zimmerman 2 added operating and maintenance responsibilities, ongoing Keller investment, and tandem planning. Its funding language is qualified by feasibility. The stronger paired-fundraising language in withdrawn Clark-Zimmerman 1 is not an adopted guarantee of matching Keller dollars.

The July 28 presentation predates these changes. Redline PDFs can extract deleted and added words together; use the adopted webpage to establish the final instruction.

The technical steering committee was unanimous, but the ex-officio group was not: Exhibit E identifies ten signatories, one abstention, and four opponents. Exhibit D leaves the future Keller building and/or site more open than the smaller-theater recommendation in Exhibit E. Avoid merging these into one unanimous endorsement of a fully specified two-building program.

**Suggested page text:** “Council authorized the next stage of planning on September 9, 2026. Before Portland takes on financial obligations, it still needs a complete financial plan and Council approval. The December agreement must explain how the proposed hall, Keller transition, and operating responsibilities would work.”

## 2. Add a funding ledger by component

The $137.5 million state commitment is $85 million for PSU theater/academic space and $52.5 million for parking. It is not a $137.5 million grant toward the City’s Broadway hall. The committee staff summary, page 2, explicitly says no state funding has been identified for that hall.

Exhibit D cites $157.6 million committed to the campus project in aggregate. Do not subtract that aggregate from a Broadway-hall estimate. Council discussion also refers to philanthropy and TIF resources; staff cautions that the TIF amount is not guaranteed. Record recipient, permitted uses, conditions, match requirements, availability date, and whether each amount is cash, bonds, a pledge, or a proposal.

The $44 million discussed at Council is a fundraising target described as approximately 10% of the hall estimate. It is neither the whole project cost nor an authorized City expenditure or demonstrated maximum City exposure.

Reconcile the $358 million and roughly $447–449 million PSU figures, and the $236 million and $290 million Keller figures, before showing a comparison. The resolution attributes its $447 million/$290 million figures to May 2024 estimates. Do not call one an updated, current, all-in price without reconciling scope, base year, escalation, contingency, fit-out, shared infrastructure, financing, and excluded work. Neither option has an established “known ceiling.”

Display alternative *complete programs*: PSU hall + interim Keller work + Keller conversion + shared infrastructure and reserves, versus a renovated Broadway Keller + any temporary facilities and interruption costs. Do not add mutually exclusive full Broadway projects, but do not omit real retained-building obligations.

## 3. Show what the financial projection does and does not establish

Exhibit C, dated April 16, 2026, explicitly excludes construction cost, operating expenses, closure losses, and ancillary PSU campus components (page 2). It assumes publicly managed facilities. It estimates receipts and economic activity; it is not a net operating budget, debt-service plan, or affordability finding.

| Exhibit C measure | PSU | Keller | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Capacity modeled | 3,200 | 3,000 | Different from the adopted approximately 3,000-seat PSU description |
| Attendance | 462,820 | 430,160 | PSU advantage 32,660, approximately 7.6% |
| Gross ticket sales | $48,934,400 | $41,742,000 | Not City revenue |
| Operating receipts in page 11 table | $13,854,122 | $12,386,822 | Difference $1,467,300, approximately 11.8%; expenses not deducted |

The model includes 112 Broadway performances at each venue and equal occupancy assumptions. More seats and assumed price/rent increases help generate PSU’s advantage. It assumes ticket-price increases of 15–20% and rental increases of 10–15%. Show sensitivity to a 3,000-seat PSU configuration, lower demand, price resistance, and nonprofit rental subsidies.

PSU’s page 11 receipts include approximately $2.956 million in labor/service reimbursements, which carry corresponding costs. The table excludes approximately $1.4 million in parking revenue retained by PSU. Neither receipts nor reimbursed labor should be presented as disposable surplus.

Two discrepancies need a visible source note or correction request:

1. Page 11’s narrative says approximately $15.3 million versus $12.3 million and calls that a 12% difference. Its table says $13.854 million versus $12.387 million, which does produce approximately 12%. This discrepancy was visually checked. Parking could be relevant, but the document does not establish the reconciliation.
2. Page 14 shows lodging-tax totals around $1.8 million/$1.7 million. Applying its stated combined 11.5% rate to the page 12 lodging-spending amounts ($2,942,912/$2,754,358) yields about $338,435/$316,751. The taxable base does not reconcile on the face of the document. Request the workbook and tax-base explanation; these arithmetic checks are not replacement official estimates.

Economic spending of $95.5 million versus $82.5 million is not a fiscal return to the City. The 367 versus 320 jobs supported are not necessarily net new permanent jobs. Do not mix Exhibit B’s earlier forecasts or the site’s different FY25 figures with Exhibit C without reconciling dates and definitions.

**Add a four-part display:** customer spending → venue receipts → operating surplus/deficit → public contribution after capital and financing. Unknown stages should remain explicitly unknown.

## 4. Make the Portland’5 subsidy a contract question

Exhibit B, page 60, shows allocated FY2023–24 results of approximately +$1.2 million for Keller, −$2.6 million for Schnitzer, and −$2.4 million for Hatfield: approximately −$3.8 million combined. These are allocated venue accounts, not independently audited standalone businesses; they exclude capital, government contributions, and miscellaneous items.

This does not establish the future subsidy requirement, but it demonstrates why moving Broadway receipts matters. Exhibit D explicitly recommends preserving a revenue stream to support Portland’5 and the arts community.

Council staff explain in the video that the PSU hall is outside the current Portland’5 management procurement. A future combined operator is not ruled out, but neither the operator nor the revenue-sharing arrangement has been settled. City ownership alone does not join the revenue streams.

Require the agreement to specify: booking and rental authority; promoter versus venue receipts; operating costs; renewal reserves; the transfer to other theaters; who absorbs a shortfall; access to audited accounts; enforcement; and obligations after an operator change or default. Community access and subsidized rents also need funded, measurable terms.

**Suggested visual:** ticket income splits between promoter and venue; venue receipts pay expenses and reserves; only the remaining contracted transfer supports other theaters. Label each agreement that controls the split.

## 5. Study the smaller Keller as part of the whole market

The finding against two competing Broadway-scale halls does not mean all possible two-venue programs are infeasible. Conversely, it does not prove demand for a smaller Keller.

Compare the proposed 1,200–1,800-seat Keller with PSU’s secondary theater (600–1,200 in the embedded letter of intent; 1,200 in study scenarios), Newmark’s 880 seats, Schnitzer, and relevant private venues. Include stage geometry, fixed seating versus flat floor, available dates, production capability, rental prices, and required subsidy. A broad “mid-size gap” is insufficient; seek realistic booking commitments and a funded operating plan for the actual configuration.

Exhibit B’s organizational survey has only 11 respondents, mostly nonprofits. Ten report difficulty finding suitable space, but seven would not add days even with a new 3,000-seat hall. Those are useful program signals, not a comprehensive market census or proof that a particular conversion pays for itself.

Its 5,160 audience respondents skew older: 71.5% are 50 or older. They represent current audiences rather than Portland voters or all potential attendees. Page 153’s roughly 30% ticket-price result is a share of 9,041 selections in a multiple-response question. The 2,735 respondents selecting cost equal approximately 53% of respondents. Avoid confusing selection shares with people. Pair higher ticket/rental assumptions with an explicit affordability strategy.

## 6. Price the transition and define the safety work

The strongest PSU case is the ability to continue using Keller during construction. Exhibit B discusses a 19–28-month full-renovation closure and a broader potential touring interruption of three to five years. These are different measures; the longer period is a stakeholder scenario, not a measured physical construction duration.

Do not describe temporary Broadway accommodation as impossible. Exhibit D considers it unproven, while Exhibits B and C leave questions to investigate. An alternative requires producer acceptance, a site, permitting, equipment, setup/removal costs, schedule, and operating economics. It should not be credited with savings without that work, or dismissed without a costed test.

The $8.5–17.5 million ten-year Keller maintenance range is not a seismic retrofit budget. Add a funded annual bridge plan covering engineering review, life safety, accessibility, necessary repairs, and an emergency closure contingency. The attachments do not independently establish that Keller is safe until 2030. Underlying structural reports and facilities assessments should be obtained before making that assertion.

Separate construction employment, ongoing theatrical employment, and campus labor provisions. Written building-trades testimony describes an agreement covering PSU-owned portions; it does not prove universal coverage of City and shared components. Request the executed agreements and their scopes.

## 7. Add the shared-property and delivery risks hidden in the testimony packet

The testimony packet contains a signed, explicitly nonbinding letter of intent on PDF pages 120–125. It is a useful early negotiating framework, not an executed commitment to fund construction.

It contemplates City ownership of the main hall; PSU ownership of a secondary theater and other components; condominium-style shared ownership and pro-rata common construction/operating expenses; PSU procurement leadership with City consultation; and City equal approval authority over design documents affecting its own and shared systems. It does not establish condominium voting percentages or prove that PSU has complete control.

It also describes restricted City capital accounts held by PSU, proposed limits on withdrawal unless the project is abandoned, and future matching/timing expectations. Treat these as proposed historical terms requiring current negotiation. Old estimates and milestones should not be portrayed as current executed obligations.

The opening date says November 15, 2025, while signatures show November 14–15, 2024; Council discussion also refers to 2024. The signature page was visually checked. Ask for a certified, version-controlled copy rather than silently resolving the discrepancy.

Before a binding agreement, demand clarity on land title, common-area voting and vetoes, shared costs, overruns, capital-account refunds, donor shortfalls, withdrawal and abandonment, design approval, environmental/geotechnical responsibility, construction oversight, labor requirements, and limits on predevelopment exposure. Show both proposed City safeguards and unresolved terms.

## 8. Treat transportation as conditional feasibility

Exhibit A is a high-level transportation screening, not final approval. Its June 2025 scenario models simultaneous 3,000-seat Keller, 3,000-seat PSU main hall, and 1,200-seat secondary hall activity. That differs from the current smaller-Keller concept.

The travel survey comes from one performance of SIX on May 2, 2025: 320 groups comprising 787 people, approximately 30% of attendance. The appendix clarifies groups versus patrons, which the narrative does not consistently distinguish. Approximately 88% arrived by private vehicle, 6% by drop-off, 4% by transit, and 2% walking. One show is not a definitive future mode split.

The proposed 740-space PSU garage has a single SW Lincoln access across MAX tracks. Queuing toward the I-405 Fourth Avenue off-ramp is a material issue for further analysis. “No fatal flaw” does not settle access design, mitigation, agency approval, or cost.

Require updated concurrent-event assumptions, freight and accessible drop-off plans, parking prices and revenue rights, PBOT/ODOT coordination, and measurable mode-shift goals. Distinguish a SW Third Avenue lane reduction from permanent full street closure at Keller.

## Testimony: use it to identify tests, not vote totals

The 162-page packet mixes individual submissions, organizational letters, duplicates, embedded studies/agreements, press articles, and sign-up sheets. Page count is not public support or opposition. Do not republish personal contact information in the deep dive.

Useful themes include IATSE and Broadway continuity; Profile Theatre’s training/community-space case; Historic Landmarks and heritage concerns about retention and embodied carbon; former Portland’5 operations experience about lost commercial cross-subsidy; and construction-labor requests for qualifications, payroll, apprenticeship hours, safety, and enforcement. These support concrete diligence questions. They do not establish unanimous labor support, definitive engineering conclusions, or a life-cycle carbon comparison.
