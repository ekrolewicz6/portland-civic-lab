## Paste block: the ladder

> Our race page shows each candidate's positions as a three-rung ladder: **what** you promise, **how** you would deliver it, and **measured by** what result. "How" means the mechanism you name yourself: a funding source, a rule change, staffing, sequencing, or a partner. "Measured by" means the number, deadline or visible result you say would show it worked. We fill a rung only from your own published words, and we never fill one from a general goal. Where your site or pamphlet statement does not name a mechanism or a result, the page shows a gap, and once we have asked you it says "asked" instead. The questions below are the gaps we found for you. A short, specific answer to each is enough; we will quote or paraphrase it with a date and attribute it to your campaign.

**Replied September 19, 2026** to the individual questions in the first round (three policies with costs; housing, safety, homelessness, spending, climate and transportation; committee work with attached documents). His positions, ladder rungs and two topic stances (new taxes: none until the audit is done; police staffing: more officers plus cadets) now come from that reply; see the research log entry `anderson-2026-09-19`.

Contact: reply to his September 19 email (no campaign site or public email in the pamphlet).

Issue gaps still open after his reply:

- **Housing.** What number of homes, or what timeline, would show that reusing city assets and making the city more investable worked?
- **Climate.** What result would show the climate-and-business approach worked: emissions, ridership, or something else?

Extra topics without an explicit statement:

- **Moda Center.** Would you vote for public money in a final Moda Center deal? Council approved non-binding negotiating terms in August 2026; any public money needs further votes.
- **Clearing camps.** Would you keep camp-removal funding at current levels? A 2025 amendment to move about $4.3 million from removals to services failed.
- **Data centers.** Would you vote for a moratorium or ban on new AI data centers in the city? Several candidates propose one.
- **Street Response.** Would you fund Portland Street Response to a full 24/7 citywide role? Its scope and funding have been contested at Council.
- **Street repair fee.** Would you keep the monthly street repair fee Council created in 2026?
- **Water rates.** Would you vote for further water-rate increases to pay for the Bull Run filtration plant? Rates rose about $5 a month in 2026.
