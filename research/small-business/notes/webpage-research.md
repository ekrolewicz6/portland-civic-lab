## New calculations

`build-web.py` reads the pinned revised 2022 SUSB metro file, selects the 387 rows labeled Metro Area (excluding Micro Area), and calculates a job-weighted metropolitan benchmark: **47.457%** of employer jobs at enterprises with fewer than 500 employees; **16.504%** at enterprises with fewer than 20. This is metropolitan America, not a complete United States total. Jobs can be summed across geographies; firm counts cannot (multi-location enterprises recur).

Peer industry standardization fixes each metro's weights to Portland's employment distribution across 19 broad NAICS sectors. Within-sector under-threshold shares are multiplied by those weights and summed. NAICS99 is omitted; coverage is 1,084,470 / 1,084,535 = 99.994% of Portland jobs. All comparison cells are published numerical values with G/H/J noise flags, not suppression imputations. This controls broad industry composition only: detailed subsectors, enterprise age, headquarters patterns, population, labor force and policy selection remain different. It is descriptive, not a causal estimate or a robust global ranking. For fewer than500 workers the standardized peer range is 43.3–50.2%; Portland remains highest among these eight comparators, but small margins should not be overinterpreted.
