# September 12 feedback: forest condition and fire effects

Current public co-authors: Edan Krolewicz and Dominic Kuklawood. Public credits and current outreach drafts were updated at the project owner's request. Earlier archived research is a historical snapshot, not the current author list. No outreach has been sent.

## Proposed next research questions

Readers would benefit from a view of forest extent, recent fire scars, and documented ecological effects, including effects of wildfire. These are research questions; no statewide “percent good burned” or target of burning 100% of Oregon forest has been adopted.

1. Define the denominator: current forest land, tree canopy, or a specific ecosystem with a documented restoration objective. Choose an explicit reference year and geography. Investigate FIA forest-area estimates and LANDFIRE vegetation layers; do not substitute forest cover for ecological condition.
2. Add a recent-fire view with severity and time since fire. Keep unassessed areas explicit. Existing perimeters identify event extent, not uniform effects within them.
3. Seek locally reviewed outcome evidence. Separate reported management intent, observed vegetation change, and documented ecological benefit. Include wildfire where evidence supports it; do not automatically equate prescribed fire or low severity with ecological success.
4. Design progress measures only after defining the objective, eligible area, time window, evidence standards, uncertainty, and repeat-treatment accounting. Spatially union relevant areas before calculating a percentage; do not add overlapping treatment acreage or count repeated ignitions as new land.
5. Start with a reviewed pilot landscape and separate indicators for forest extent, mapped fire exposure, severity, and documented outcomes. Statewide extrapolation remains a later decision.

## Proposed next page iteration — clarified September 12

This is a proposed implementation, not a feature already published.

- **Recent fire scars:** a separately selectable overlay alongside prescribed burns, with 1-, 5-, and 10-year windows and individual-year selection. Existing Oregon Explorer perimeter history runs through 2025. Acquire newer perimeter geometry separately; the current WFIGS occurrence-point import does not supply a scar boundary. Render perimeter footprints at statewide scale instead of relying only on record clusters, with clear source/year coverage.
- **What happened inside the perimeter:** add MTBS severity data with a legend for source-defined severity classes and unmapped/unassessed areas. Distinguish a mapped perimeter from a remotely assessed burned area. Show imagery/assessment dates and assessment type. Preserve small-fire coverage gaps; no severity class is automatically labeled “good.”
- **Forest context:** add a dated vegetation/forest layer and a separate forest-area indicator. Do not subtract burned acreage from forest acreage and label the result “forests left.” Investigate forest land-use change separately from tree-canopy loss or fire exposure.
- **Progress panel:** begin with clearly labeled descriptive metrics: forest land area, unique area with documented prescribed burning during the selected period, and unique forest area intersecting assessed recent wildfire. Treatment boundaries cannot substitute for actual burned footprints. Keep overlap and repeat fires explicit, and report unassessed coverage. Ecological success remains a separate evidence-based measure.
- **Potential goal bar:** for a pilot landscape, evaluate “share of a defined priority area meeting agreed restoration objectives.” A target, assessment date/window, local ecological interpretation, and evidence of outcomes are prerequisites. Wildfire can contribute if supported by the agreed criteria. Do not invent an Oregon-wide percentage or assume all forest should burn on one schedule.
