# Sizing the 2027-29 state budget gap (checked September 25, 2026)

Prompted by John Tapogna (Oregon Business Council), who asked how big the "2027–29 budget gap" on the governor page is in percentage terms.

## What the sources say

- **Medicaid gap from H.R. 1: $421 million General Fund, 2027-29.** Governor's Advisory Group on Medicaid Sustainability, final report to Governor Kotek (2026), Figure 1 and p. 9: "for the 2027-29 budget, there is an estimated net $421 million general fund gap created by H.R.1. This is the estimated amount of funds needed to continue the current OHP program." It is $833 million in lost General Fund (provider tax, state directed payments, match rates) minus $412 million in projected General Fund savings from lower enrollment. The report projects the gap growing in 2029-31 (Willamette Week, July 29, 2026, reports $868 million). https://www.oregon.gov/oha/OHPB/MtgDocs/4.%20Advisory%20Group%20on%20Medicaid%20Sustainability%20Report%20to%20Governor%20Kotek%20Final.pdf ; https://www.wweek.com/news/state/2026/07/29/medicaid-shortfall-follows-decade-long-growth-in-state-spending/
- **2027-29 General Fund revenue: $42.66 billion gross, $42.36 billion net.** Office of Economic Analysis, September 2026 forecast, Table R.2. https://www.oregon.gov/das/oea/Documents/revenue0926.pdf
- **2025-27 ending balance $400 million; reserves $3,463 million = 9.7% of General Fund revenues; non-corporate revenue $526 million under the kicker threshold.** Same forecast, Table R.1 and the budgetary reserves section (reserves are the Rainy Day Fund at $2,192 million plus the Education Stability Fund).
- **2025-27 adopted budget: $37.3 billion General Fund, $1.8 billion Lottery, $138.9 billion total funds.** LFO Budget Information Brief 2025-2. https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/lfo/Documents/2025-2%20LAB%20Summary%202025-27.pdf

## Arithmetic

- $421 million / $42.36 billion net 2027-29 General Fund revenue = 0.99%, about 1%.
- No official all-in 2027-29 gap figure was found. The Medicaid number covers the Oregon Health Plan only; SNAP cost shifts under H.R. 1 and ordinary cost growth are not in it. The Governor's Recommended Budget (December 2026) should be the first full number.

## Page wording notes

- The governor page's "2027–29 budget gap" card says "(9.7% of the general fund)"; the forecast says 9.7% "of General Fund revenues."
- The taxes board mentions the "$400 million 2025–27 ending balance," which is a surplus for the current biennium, not the Medicaid gap; the two $400 million figures are easy to confuse.
