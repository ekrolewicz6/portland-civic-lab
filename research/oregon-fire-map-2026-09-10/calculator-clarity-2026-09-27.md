# Cost example clarity — September 27, 2026

The earlier calculator asked readers to compare an expense with “expected damage avoided” before explaining the uncertain outcomes. This revision uses one made-up project over 20 years and explains the sequence explicitly.

1. Three numbered questions ask for total project spending, the chance that wildfire reaches the work while it is effective, and damage prevented conditional on that fire arriving. Help text distinguishes avoided damage from total incident damage. Amounts use “million” or full dollar values rather than M/K abbreviations.
2. A 100-future diagram shows the assumed probability. These are possible futures for the same place, not a set of observed Oregon fires or parcels.
3. Two outcome cards show that the work is paid for in either outcome, while wildfire damage is avoided only in the assumed fire-encounter outcome.
4. The average is explained in words before the equation. The result subtracts project spending and states whether average damage avoided exceeds, matches or falls short of that spending.
5. The break-even encounter probability is displayed. If conditional avoided damage is lower than project cost, the result explains that even certainty of the assumed wildfire cannot cover the expense in this damage-only calculation.
6. A reset button restores the initial example. The no-JavaScript page retains the complete starting explanation, possible outcomes, diagram and arithmetic. Habitat and other objectives are acknowledged separately.

The assumptions remain hypothetical. This revision adds no local financial forecast, real cost record, risk estimate, quantified habitat benefit or savings claim. Existing research explains the records needed for a real comparison.
