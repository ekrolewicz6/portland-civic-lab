## How speakers were attributed
The captions have no speaker labels, only turn breaks. With two candidates and a moderator who names who answers first and who gets each rebuttal, every turn the guide uses is certain. Each candidate also refers to the other by name. The captions misspell both names and some words ("Cotch," "Drezen," "rescend," "egg overtime" for ag overtime). The guide quotes only short phrases whose wording is unambiguous; everything else is paraphrased.

## Used in the guide (state pack, `debate(<seconds>)` evidence)
- gov-climate-protection, Kotek (78:47): "100% supportive" of the current program; supports the Legislature taking up alignment with Washington, California and Quebec next year; "in the meantime" the program stays.
- gov-climate-protection, Drazan (37:01): will rescind the program on day one; says it will "drive up the cost of utilities and power"; a political agenda that "harms families."
- gov-data-center-moratorium, Kotek (55:47): "there has to be a moratorium on new development of data centers in our state until we get this right"; a day-one bill setting rules.
- Kotek climate "how" step (56:08): the day-one bill: produce their own clean energy, union construction, community benefits, treat workers fairly.
- gov-power-act, Drazan (56:53): calls the POWER Act "window dressing" and an "election year gimmick"; says utility regulators could already have set a rate for large users; says it also reaches small-town data centers for school districts and fire stations. Her two House votes (yes April 22, 2025; no June 5, 2025) stay in the entry from OLIS.
- gov-vote-by-mail, Drazan (72:28): backed HB 3872 over concerns about the U.S. Postal Service, not the trustworthiness of the system; "I've always been a strong supporter of vote by mail"; wants people able to vote in person. Her description of a long-term absentee option was checked against HB 3872 as introduced: the bill allows a mail ballot to a voter who asks at least 21 days before an election and is "for any reason unable" to vote in person; no standing absentee status was found in the text. The board context states the bill's terms.
- gov-vote-by-mail, Kotek (73:51): vote by mail for more than 25 years; "It is trusted. It is convenient."; urged voters to read the bill.
- New board gov-farm-overtime. Kotek (50:19): "fair and moral" that farmworkers get paid overtime; supporting farms "does not mean overlooking" overtime. Drazan (49:17): farmers need relief from the overtime rules to stay in business; ag overtime "clearly needs to be addressed." She opened with "Yes, thank you for that question"; the guide relies on the substance, not the "Yes."
- New board gov-strike-benefits. Kotek (75:14): stands with union workers "when they are out on strike, making sure that they can put food on the table." Drazan (76:08): union members have strike funds; the law makes the unemployment insurance fund "less stable."

## Verified against the record (October 3, 2026)
- HB 4002 (2022), OLIS history via /Measures/Overview/GetHistory/HB4002: House passed 37–23 (March 1, 2022), Senate 17–10 (March 3), governor signed April 15, 2022, chapter 115. Phase-in (55 hours in 2023–24, 48 in 2025–26, 40 from 2027) and the refundable employer tax credit for tax years 2023–2028 are from the staff measure summary for HB 4002-B (Joint Committee on Farm Worker Overtime, February 24, 2022; apps.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2022R1/Downloads/MeasureAnalysisDocument/64810) and the OLIS measure summary.
- SB 916 (2025), OLIS history via /Measures/Overview/GetHistory/SB916: Senate passed 16–12 (March 20, 2025); House passed 33–23 (June 4) with Drazan among the nays; after conference, Senate repassed 16–12 and House repassed 35–22 (June 12) with Drazan among the nays; governor signed June 24, 2025; chapter 432; effective January 1, 2026. Enrolled text: a striker is disqualified for the first week, then eligible for up to 10 weeks (8 when the trust fund is on tax schedule V–VIII); no exception for public employees was found in the text.
- HB 3872 (2025) introduced text, OLIS /Downloads/MeasureDocument/HB3872/Introduced: chief sponsor Representative Drazan; section 1 amends ORS 254.465 (in-person voting at a booth on election day; mail ballot on request at least 21 days ahead for a voter "for any reason unable" to vote in person).
