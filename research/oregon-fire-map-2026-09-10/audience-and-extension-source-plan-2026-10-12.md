# Fire in Oregon: audience and Extension-source priorities

October 12, 2026. This is an editorial decision memo, prompted by a private conversation and checked against the public sources below. The conversation transcript remains outside the repository. It is not publishable testimony, a formal scientific review, an offer of photographs for publication, or an OSU partnership. Transcription errors make exact quotations especially unsafe. Public claims must cite the independent sources, and any attributed contribution needs specific permission.

## Decision

Keep the true north: one readable Oregon fire guide connected to a documented landscape investigation. Improve **who the guide serves and what a reader can do with it** before expanding the atlas simply because another layer is available. The primary reader is an Oregon resident or student who wants to understand a real place and decision. “Readable by a tenth grader” is a clarity standard, not an assumption that readers know nothing. Homeowners, landowners, community planners, and practitioners can enter the same continuous story and follow relevant deeper links.

[OSU Extension's community-engagement primer](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9467-community-engagement-fire-preparedness-its-how-matters) argues for accounting for existing knowledge, local context, and action-based learning. Make that concrete: ask readers to apply the guide to a permit, a fire perimeter, a local project, or a spending line. Test whether they can explain what the record establishes and what remains unknown. Count that as success, rather than counting time on page or vocabulary recalled.

## Changes to prioritize

| Priority | Reader question and site change | Source and acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| 1. A clear entry | Open with a one-minute visual answer: fire has long shaped Oregon, but its role, effects, and useful management choices vary by place. Show a small set of real landscape contrasts and three ways into the same story: how fire works, what people can change, and what records show near me. Retain the current long guide and question index for a ten-minute read, with deeper pages for an hour or a class. | Use [OSU Extension's *Not all flame's the same*](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/em-9459-not-all-flames-same) as an Oregon-specific editorial reference. Its seven geographic regions and the site's four ecological archetypes serve different purposes; do not present the four as a full map of seven regions or assume a one-to-one crosswalk. A reader should be able to state why the same prescription is inappropriate everywhere. |
| 2. A place-specific learning path | At the landscape/atlas handoff, offer a concrete exercise: select a place, read its vegetation/history, identify a documented objective, then distinguish a planned unit, a completed burn, and later observations. The page should adapt to the reader's question without splitting into disconnected mini-sites. | The existing guide, place search, permit-versus-accomplishment examples, and project dossiers supply parts of this. Test on a known, documented place and show an explicit “not documented” state when a unit-to-outcome link is absent. |
| 3. A burn that can be followed through time | Add **one** fully sourced visual burn dossier with unit map, manager's documented goal, preparation, actual burn date and acreage, matched before/after viewpoints if available, and goal-specific follow-up. Photos show visible change; they are not substitutes for measuring outcomes. | [OSU's prescribed-fire monitoring guide](https://extension.oregonstate.edu/catalog/pub/em-9388-prescribed-fire-basics-monitoring-evaluation) describes matched photo points and how measurements depend on objectives. Seek an existing, identifiable photo set, then record viewpoint, dates, photographer, captions, reuse permission, seasonal differences, unit ID, and objective. No photos were transferred or cleared in the conversation. |
| 4. A complete money account | Expand the current ODF example into a two-agency starting ledger: ODF landscape work and OSFM community risk reduction/home protection. Explain the path **law/appropriation → fund → award → actual expenditure → completed activity → maintained result**. Put the stage and accounting period beside every amount. | [HB 3940's official measure record](https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2025R1/Measures/Overview/HB3940), the [Governor's 2025–27 funding table](https://www.oregon.gov/gov/Documents/HB%203940%20%2B%20HB%205006%20Funding%207.7.25.pdf), [OSFM investments and recipient lists](https://www.oregon.gov/osfm/wildfire/pages/investments-for-oregon.aspx), [OSFM dashboards](https://www.oregon.gov/osfm/about-us/pages/dashboards.aspx), and ODF's existing 2025 implementation report. These represent different periods and accounting stages. Verify adopted allocations and actual disbursements; never add transfers and downstream spending twice or call an award an expense. |
| 5. An actionable ending | End each audience path with the decision it can inform: a resident can examine home ignition routes and local CWPP priorities; a landowner can locate an appropriate practitioner; a community can ask what project is ready but unfunded; a student can test a claim against the record. Describe opportunities actually available in a named place. | Use official OSFM, OSU Extension, and local CWPP resources with current dates. Do not imply that one action fits every home, landowner, or ecosystem. |

## What to acquire next

1. **OSU publication and media audit:** Read all seven regional briefs and the underlying citations in *Not all flame's the same*. Make a comparison matrix against the guide's four archetypes. Identify any missing Oregon region or misleading generalization. Request reusable figures or geographic boundaries only if the site needs those exact assets; public access is not a reuse license.
2. **One matched photo example:** First inspect publicly documented burn cases already in the project. If no matching, rights-cleared pair exists, ask a practitioner for *one* existing photo-point sequence linked to a specific unit and objective. Record dates, viewpoint and permissions before designing a slider. Do not assume a collection of fire photos contains matched before/after views.
3. **OSFM finance discovery:** Inspect downloadable dashboard CSVs, 2025–27 program reports and recipient lists, and map each to budget, award, payment, completion or outcome. Reconcile to ODF and other state accounts before a statewide comparison. A financing law or press release is not a record of completed work.
4. **Reader review:** Ask five people with different experiences (including an educator/student if feasible) to read the one-minute explanation and trace one case. Ask what they already knew and whether they can distinguish purpose, authorization, accomplishment, observed outcome, and money stage. Use their errors to revise visuals and wording.

## Practitioner review protocol

An Extension team may offer feedback, but that is a prospective review opportunity, not an endorsement or committed partnership. Send a concise link and a one-page description only if useful after checking the existing thread. Ask for the **two passages that oversimplify Oregon's landscape differences or overlook readers' knowledge**, plus one example of a visual record that would help. Do not ask a whole regional team to audit every dataset. If a specific photo set or data custodian is identified, follow up on that one item and its rights. Keep any team comments private until their authors approve attribution.

## Keep out of this release

- A generic statewide “percent good burned” or an acreage target derived from historical fire regimes.
- A photo gallery without matching dates, location, objectives, credits and rights.
- A single prevention-versus-suppression total created by adding appropriations, grants, agency spending and incident costs from incompatible periods.
- Claims that OSU Extension has checked, partnered on, or endorsed the atlas.

The next editorial release should make the existing guide more teachable and add an appropriately scoped OSFM money pathway. The first detailed burn-photo dossier and statewide financial totals wait for case-specific records and reconciliation.
