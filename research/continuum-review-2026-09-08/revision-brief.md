# A clearer, more defensible continuum page

Research and page review: September 8, 2026. Reviewed the live page, its source code and research memo, current budgets, audits, provider documents, and local research. This is an editorial and design recommendation; production page code has not been changed.

## Recommendation

Rebuild the page around **the journey from needing help to receiving a suitable placement that lasts**. Make the reader see the obstacles between services, the resources needed to overcome each one, and who can act. Keep the fourteen-stage reference material as a supporting atlas.

The existing page has valuable breadth, a recognizable visual identity, an unusually useful interest in handoffs, and extensive citations. Its weakness is that it asks visitors to learn the model before they can understand the problem. It sometimes turns uncertain or incompatible figures into confident conclusions. Making those conclusions more visually forceful before correcting them would make the page less trustworthy.

The central explanation should be:

> A shelter bed provides safety. A suitable home ends homelessness. Continuing support helps that home last. The system has to connect all three.

That is proposed editorial language, not a quotation from a source.

## 1. The story a reader should understand in two minutes

### Opening: people want a home; why is the path so difficult?

Suggested title: **“From a bed to a home: where Portland’s homelessness system breaks.”**

Suggested deck: “Follow the path from first contact to a lasting home. See where access, housing resources, care, and handoffs break down—and which changes could make a placement work.”

Use people’s expressed goals to introduce the problem. The April 2026 PSU Pathways report found **98% of 350 respondents asked wanted permanent, stable housing**. Its full analytic sample was 541 adults surveyed during 2025; the housing-preference denominator was a subset. This was a purposive sample, not a census or representative estimate for all unhoused residents. The same study provides evidence that household relationships, pets, and permanence affect whether a housing option is acceptable. Avoid inventing an individual’s name or history around an anonymous research quote. [Pathways report, pp. 20–28, 44–49](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Pathways-Survey-Findings-Published-4.9.2026.pdf).

Then introduce one system result: **roughly half of the shelter exits in the County’s FY25 review had unreported destinations**. Do not yet create a precise 100-person graphic: the report contains conflicting exit denominators and unknown-outcome percentages. Reconcile them with HSD first. A missing destination does not establish either a return to the street or a successful housing exit. [Adult Shelter Review, pp. 13 and 35](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Adult-Shelter-Review-FY25.pdf).

### Six narrative chapters

| Chapter | Question the reader should be able to answer | Main visual |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The goal | What do people need and want? | One carefully scoped finding and a consented person’s story or clearly labeled illustrative journey |
| 2. The system | How do housing, shelter, and care connect? | A branching map with housing and care in parallel |
| 3. The breaks | Where is progress blocked, and what evidence shows that? | Clickable failure points; documented, inferred, and unknown distinguished |
| 4. The price | What are we paying for, and what does that price include? | Comparable shelter costs plus a separate housing-cost receipt |
| 5. Better placement | What makes an offer usable and a handoff complete? | A resource-and-fit checklist attached to three illustrative journeys |
| 6. The next decisions | What should change first, who owns it, and how will we know? | A short action register with funding source, dependency, and outcome |

Offer a concise default reading path. Put detailed stage definitions, clinical literature, legal discussion, methodology, objections, and source inventories behind clearly named reference links. Keep important limitations beside the relevant claim.

## 2. Five visuals worth building

### A. A map of connections and failure points

Make this the dominant visual. Draw physical living situations separately from services and queues. Someone can be in a shelter, waiting for a housing match, and receiving outpatient care simultaneously. Assessment, prevention, outreach, and case management are functions, not mutually exclusive physical places.

The housing path should allow direct movement into a home, successful prevention that keeps a household housed, and optional interim shelter. Medical care, recovery services, personal assistance, and tenancy support run alongside that path. Crisis care is a branch when indicated, not a universal prerequisite for housing.

Use stable visual meanings:

- Solid labeled connection: a documented service or process.
- Interruption with a named cause: a documented barrier.
- Neutral question mark or hatching: the outcome or capacity is not established by public data.
- Clearly labeled proposed connection: a recommended change.

Do not make arrow widths proportional without matched flow data. Do not use the same red treatment for measured low performance, missing data, and a service that does not exist.

Selecting a failure opens four concise answers: **evidence; what it means; who can change it; what result to measure**. Use examples such as a closed intake window, a setting unable to meet mobility needs, a missing subsidy, a referral without transport, or support ending after move-in. Label illustrative scenarios as illustrative.

### B. “What happened after the offer?”

The City already publishes a useful small piece of this chain. Its August 3–9, 2026 report lists **11 interested in a shelter referral, 3 accepting, and 3 using a bed for at least one night**. It separately lists **361 active campsites visited**. The existing page’s roughly 1% comparison divides people by campsites. Replace that with the actual referral measures and their definitions. These are weekly aggregate counts, not a verified longitudinal cohort. [City weekly report](https://www.portland.gov/homelessness-impact-reduction/news/2026/8/11/weekly-street-services-report-august-3-9-2026).

The future cohort visual should follow: suitable offer → accepted → arrived → remained safely placed → permanent move-in → housing status at 6/12 months. Show pending, declined, ineligible, unavailable, other known destinations, and unknown separately. Put reasons for failed handoffs on the connections. Use enough weeks to avoid presenting one small week as typical performance.

### C. “There is a bed. Can this person use it?”

Replace a generic capacity chart with a resource matrix: physical/design capacity; staffed capacity; occupied or reserved; accepting intake at the relevant time; suitable for the household and support needs; held placement and transport arranged. Do not fabricate the quantities for intermediate filters.

An hours chart must distinguish a building being open from new intake being open, and a phone line answering from a bed being available. Every directory entry needs a last-verified date. Without a live feed, label it **published access information**, not “beds open tonight.”

Add an explicit timeline for announced changes. The City’s August 24 update still lists Northrup open, with closure scheduled September 18; its 580 overnight-bed figure is a projected winter inventory after changes. Beds, shelter units, person capacity, flex beds, and future inventory require separate units and dates. [City shelter changes](https://www.portland.gov/shelter-services/news/2026/7/21/changes-city-shelter-services).

### D. “What does the money buy?”

Use separate views for reported operating costs, rent benchmarks, and current budgets. A housing receipt should show rent, resident contribution, subsidy, one-time move-in assistance, services, administration, and landlord/vacancy reserves. Missing costs stay visibly missing.

For shelter performance, compare cost per available bed-night, occupied bed-night, people served, and housing exits within similar service models and populations. Show additional housing assistance beside the shelter operation. Do not promise cash savings equal to average bed costs: leases, staff, and overhead may continue after a person moves out.

### E. “Did the placement last?”

Show a placement cohort at 3, 6, and 12 months with confirmed housing, observed return to homelessness, other known outcomes, and unknown status. Someone not observed returning is not automatically confirmed housed. Include follow-up coverage.

The County’s May 2025 budget follow-up illustrates the need: it published a retention figure but also reported substantial unknown housing status in a placement cohort. A page claiming no retention numbers exist misses the more useful question of whose outcome is known and how each percentage is calculated. [Budget follow-up, slide 11](https://multco.us/file/fy_2026_homeless_services_department_budget_worksession_follow-up/download).

## 4. How better triage and placement should be explained

The public page should explain **matching a person’s housing goals, safety, and support needs to a real, usable resource**. It should not diagnose a stranger or assign someone permanently to a clinical lane.

Use three illustrative journeys: a household facing rent loss; someone leaving hospital who needs help with daily activities; a person outside whose previous offer did not fit. Give each two simultaneous questions: where can they safely live, and what assistance makes that placement workable?

The proposed placement process:

1. Address immediate safety needs through qualified responders and current protocols.
2. Establish the household’s goals, confidentiality needs, accessibility and practical constraints. Offer housing problem solving and support without making participation a condition for basic help.
3. Determine housing and financial options while qualified staff assess required clinical/personal support. Keep both processes active together.
4. Match to a setting that can actually meet those needs, with current funding, admission authority, appropriate staffing, and available space.
5. Confirm the receiving person, place hold, transport, necessary documentation and continuity of support. Record the actual arrival and resolve failed handoffs.
6. Continue tenancy and care support after move-in. Reassess fit as needs change; record outcomes and missing follow-up.

The County already has coordinated-access rules, including housing problem solving, matching preferences, provider contact/enrollment expectations, and denial tracking. The opportunity is to show compliance, coverage, delays, and exceptions across the process. [November 2025 policies](https://hsd.multco.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/1.0_CA_Policies_FINAL_2025.pdf).

For greater medical needs, include accessible housing, home care, long-term support, and suitable facility care. Current respite criteria require independent daily activities and medication management; a respite bed cannot stand in for all higher-care needs. [CCC recuperative care](https://centralcityconcern.org/health-care-location/recuperative-care/).

If the existing field algorithm remains, obtain documented clinical and legal review. Some categorical commitment-law statements conflict with current statutory provisions; simplify the public explanation and link to authoritative operational guidance. [Current ORS chapter 426](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors426.html), [ORS chapter 430](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors430.html).

## 5. Missed opportunities and the decisions to put on the page

| Proposed priority | Existing foundation / opportunity | Proposed responsible parties | Measure that would demonstrate progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attach housing resources to shelter stays | County analysis identifies uneven access to housing assistance | HSD, City, operators, Home Forward | Time from housing request to funded match and move-in; outcomes by support need |
| Resolve housing crises before shelter | Existing housing problem solving; improve cash availability and processing speed | HSD, Human Services, community providers | Time to payment; verified housing outcomes; subsequent homelessness with coverage disclosed |
| Help eligible people access Medicaid rent support | FY27 includes a $565,000 eviction-prevention in-reach team | Human Services, Home Forward, health plans | Eligible households reached → applications approved → rent paid → housing stability |
| Connect housing and ongoing care | Existing cross-sector case conferencing and the spring 2026 navigation pilot | HSD, health plans, providers, Aging/Disability services | Completed connections, unresolved needs, continuity after discharge, client-reported fit |
| Make existing units usable faster | Test the size of vacancy, subsidy, inspection, documentation and support bottlenecks | Housing providers, Home Forward, HSD | Vacancy days by reason; match-to-lease time; failed matches |
| Prevent unsupported institutional exits | Build on discharge planning and bridge services; establish actual coverage gaps | Hospitals, corrections/courts, HSD, care providers | Confirmed destination and care connection; failed handoff reasons |
| Improve offers and physical access | Household, pet, mobility, language, storage and transport needs affect fit | City, HSD, operators | Declines by specific offer/reason; suitable-offer and arrival rates |
| Maintain stability after placement | Tenancy response, benefits continuity, clinical and personal assistance | Placing programs, landlords, care teams | Whole-cohort housing status, unknown rate, crisis use and resident experience |

These are proposals, not established causal estimates or claims that each intervention is currently absent.

One particularly concrete opportunity: the County’s adopted-budget release describes the $565,000 team as intended to help access **an expected $7.8 million in Medicaid rent vouchers**. That is a forecast, not demonstrated return on investment. It is well suited to a public benefit-delivery pipeline that shows whether authorized help actually reaches households. [June 5, 2026 adopted-budget release](https://multco.us/news/news-release-multnomah-county-board-commissioners-closes-significant-spending-gap-adopts).

Existing Cross Sector Case Conferencing brings housing-service providers together with health and disability systems; it explicitly provides no housing resources. The page can make that remaining connection visible while acknowledging the work already underway. [Program documentation](https://hsd.multco.us/cross-sector-case-conferencing/), [2026 navigation resources](https://hsd.multco.us/healthcare-resources/).

Avoid rewarding only fast housing exits: doing so can discourage serving people with greater needs. Fund baseline capacity and necessary support, review comparable cohorts, include safety and resident experience, and investigate performance before attaching payment consequences. Do not close interim capacity on the assumption that proposed future housing exits will materialize.

## 6. Corrections before publication

| Existing framing | Revision |
|---|---|
| “Seven thousand ... outside tonight” | State the observation date and method; January 2025 data are not a live count |
| Fourteen sequential physical stages | Separate living situation, services, queues, and optional branches |
| 640 newly engaged ÷ 6,912 outside = 9% coverage | Remove: annual program output and historical population stock are not a linked cohort |
| 361 camp visits → 3 placements = 1% conversion | Remove: campsites and people are different units; use reported referral counts |
| Assessment count minus placement count = current backlog | Require linked people, time windows, prior assessments, other pathways and unresolved status |
| Approximately 200 “open tonight” beds | Historical occupancy applied to a later inventory does not establish current vacancies |
| No diversion / no bridge budget line | Existing policies and adopted bridge program contradict categorical absence; describe coverage gaps |
| “Nobody” records arrivals or retention | Acknowledge existing reporting, then specify the unlinked steps and incomplete follow-up |
| 80/10/10 shares identify current clinical need | Remove as a Portland estimate; historical shelter-use typology does not establish current acuity prevalence |
| “RCT” supports a whole ordered pathway | Attach evidence to the tested intervention/outcome; label local sequencing as a proposal |
| Campaign projections beside measured cost bars | Keep proposal assumptions in an explicitly separate planning view |
| Source citations prove every adjacent inference | Link each claim to its exact page/table; label inference and uncertainty |
| Seven frontline/clinical/legal reviewers | Verify real reviewer participation and provenance; if these are simulated perspectives, label them that way |

The source itself can be inconsistent. Do not repair a conflict by choosing the number that makes the sharpest headline. Add a visible unresolved-source note and a dated correction log.

## 7. Data needed for a definitive account

Prepare targeted requests; none have been sent during this review. Seek aggregate outputs or securely handled deidentified analysis, never a public person-level location or health-record map.

| Request | Likely holder | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Reconciled FY25 shelter outcome worksheet, definitions and revision history | HSD | Resolves conflicting totals before the opening outcome graphic |
| FY26 actual expenses plus occupied/available bed-days and allocation rules | HSD, City, operators | Replaces budget-derived “prices” with comparable costs |
| Referral-stage cohorts, timestamps, pending outcomes, denial/decline reasons | HSD, City, coordinated-access providers | Separates supply, access, fit, transport and handoff failures |
| Inventory with staffed/operable capacity, held spaces, intake rules and effective dates | City, County, treatment/care providers | Establishes what can actually be offered |
| Housing units/vacancies, match dates, reason-coded delays, subsidy and services attached | Home Forward, providers, HSD | Tests whether the binding constraint is units, money, care or process |
| Follow-up cohorts, observed returns, unknown status and linkage coverage | HSD, placing programs | Makes retained housing measurable without overstating success |
| Institutional discharge destinations and completed receiving-provider connections | Hospitals, corrections, care systems | Identifies which transitions lack suitable placements and why |

Every displayed metric should carry: geography, population, unit, period, denominator, source/page, source publication date, retrieval date, calculation, and evidence status. Costs additionally need payer, inclusion/exclusion rules, capital/operating split, actual/budget status, and inflation year. Showing these consistently would distinguish the page from advocacy built on disconnected headline numbers.

## 8. Implementation order and acceptance criteria

**First pass:** correct time and denominator claims, remove unsupported prices, qualify nonexistent-service statements, and resolve reviewer provenance. Preserve the current palette and editorial tone.

**Second pass:** build the opening narrative and branching map; replace the thirteen-item first-screen selector with three illustrative journeys and an optional full atlas. Promote the cost receipt, access matrix, and evidence-linked failure points.

**Third pass:** populate a reconciled cohort outcome visual and comparable cost ledger; publish the action register and unresolved evidence requests. Add dated changes and reusable source-linked figure cards.

Test whether a first-time reader can explain: the difference between a shelter bed and a lasting housing outcome; one specific documented barrier; what information is unknown; the amount and scope of one actual cost; and one action with an accountable owner. On mobile, use a vertical map, readable labels, no forced horizontal swiping, keyboard-operable controls, and visible source details. Provide a static accessible alternative and downloadable figure data where available.

## 9. Quotable language

Proposed original editorial lines:

- **“An empty bed is not an available placement.”**
- **“The handoff is part of the service.”**
- **“A housing offer needs a place, funding, a way in, and support that lasts.”**
- **“We should be able to follow the result as carefully as we follow the spending.”**

Every factual quote card should include its scope, date, denominator, source, and direct anchor. For publication-ready figures, prefer an export with those details embedded so screenshots cannot strip away the qualification. Do not attribute these proposed lines to providers, researchers, or residents.
