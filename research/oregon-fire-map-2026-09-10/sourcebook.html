Data interpretation and public page

FPA FOD is the historical occurrence baseline; Oregon Explorer is the historical perimeter baseline. Recent WFIGS supplements them. ODF occurrence and NWCC are enrichment/reconciliation sources. These are different representations, and their counts are not additive fire totals.

Keep treatment units, registrations/permits, individual ignitions, accomplishments and observations separate. Verified IDs can link records; proximity alone cannot merge them. Keep source dates and precision, repeated treatments, partial burns, reported treatment acreage, reported fire size and polygon area distinct. Unknown dates are explicitly included and labeled. Source-specific counts and freshness accompany results and export.

The page emphasizes prescribed burning, with selectable plans and wildfire context, year/location/agency/method/purpose/status filters, synchronized map/list, shareable selection and filters, record details, source coverage and private correction submissions. Default years: 2021–present. The Woodpecker burns supply the first explanatory story, without an invented map location. OSU account.

Smoke measurements do not by themselves identify the responsible burn. MTBS excludes many small burns under its western size threshold. Spatial overlap with later wildfire does not establish treatment effectiveness. EPA AQS/AirData, AirNow, NOAA HMS, intrusion investigations, MTBS/RAVG/FTEM and treatment monitoring remain separate workstreams, alongside mechanical treatment, vegetation, land management, CWPP priorities, funding, incident impacts, weather and utility ignition records.

Oregon fire map: data inventory

Compiled September 10, 2026 for the Oregon fire map project (Edan Krolewicz, Dominic Kuklawood). Updated September 11, 2026. Verification depth varies; some entries remain untested leads. The dated audit supersedes the original interpretation. "Queried" means the endpoint was hit and its schema and record count read; "link only" means the page was confirmed to exist but not exercised. Machine-readable versions of the endpoints are in endpoints.csv. Contacts are in contacts.md, email drafts in outreach-drafts.md.

The short version

1. Prescribed fire on state, private, and most federal forestland in Oregon is registered in one system: ODF's Smoke Data System (SDS). Federal units register there too (the 2025 annual report lists Deschutes NF, Mt. Hood NF, BLM districts and Crater Lake NP by name). The registration record includes latitude and longitude, acres, burn type, a burn reason code, fuel loading, and then, after the burn, acres and tons actually burned, ignition time, fuel moistures, days since rain, and wind. In 2025 that was 2,946 registered units and 2,717 units burned across 179,230 acres. The system needs a login, and the public map only shows a rolling one-day and three-day window. The single most valuable request is an export of the SDS registration and accomplishment tables for all years. This is the email to write first.

2. Two federal datasets are fully public and go back decades. BLM's Oregon/Washington prescribed fire polygons (36,966 regional treatment records (36,965 completed, one active), with a REASON field and a NEPA plan ID) and the Forest Service FACTS hazardous fuel treatments (20,531 Oregon fire-treatment records, with NEPA document names and a purpose code). Coverage is incomplete: 364 FACTS records describe treatments consumed by wildfire and 16,476 describe pile burning. BLM has 33,558 missing or common-placeholder PLANID values, so most document links cannot be automated. They can be pulled today.

3. Wildfire history is well covered by public feeds. ODF fire occurrence points 1960 to 2024 (69,744 fires with cause and timing), NIFC/IRWIN incident locations and perimeters (all agencies, refreshed every five minutes, and they include prescribed fires as a category), NWCC large fire perimeters 2000 to present, and Oregon Explorer's cleaned perimeter history through 2025. MTBS gives burn severity for fires over 1,000 acres.

4. The "why this location" answer lives in three places, in descending order of usefulness: the NEPA documents behind federal burns (linked by ID from FACTS and BLM records), the burn reason code and comments in ODF's SDS, and landscape-scale priority layers (ODF's 20-Year Strategy priority geographies, county CWPPs, the Forest Service Wildfire Crisis Strategy landscapes). The narrative versions come from practitioners, which is what the certified burn manager directory and the prescribed burn associations are for.

5. Important records still need acquisition: DEQ habitat/wetland restoration permits, LRAPA ecological permits and accomplishments, refuge/park histories, additional treatment documentation, the ODA Willamette Valley field burning program (grass seed residue, up to 15,000 acres a year, registered by April 1), and anything historical from ODF's SDS. Backyard and debris burning permits are issued by local fire districts and are not centralized anywhere; treat that as out of scope for a first version.

Notes on building

- Archive rolling feeds every six hours. Publisher retention is unconfirmed. Absence from a later feed does not prove cancellation or completion; a valid empty response differs from a failed request.

- Join keys: IrwinID (NIFC locations to perimeters, and NWCC history), RegistrationNumber (ODF feed to SDS export), nepa_project_id and PLANID (treatments to documents), NFPORS_TRTID (BLM to DOI reporting).

- ODF fire occurrence is in EPSG:2992 (Oregon Lambert, feet). BLM and FACTS declare NAD83. Explicitly request outSR=4326 from ArcGIS, preserve source spatial-reference metadata, and label geometry accuracy. Federal polygons are not inherently surveyed units.

- Use a full object-ID inventory and bounded requests, splitting truncated responses; validate final inventories before atomic publication. Do not stop at a service record limit.

Use FPA FOD v7 for historical occurrences, Oregon Explorer for historical perimeters, and recent WFIGS for provisional additions. ODF occurrence and NWCC are reconciliation/enrichment sources; do not add their counts to the baseline. Verified IRWIN IDs link records without erasing their separate geometry meanings. What each group actually does

ODF Smoke Management Program (Salem). Runs the statewide registry for prescribed burning on forestland under the Oregon Smoke Management Plan. Its meteorologists issue the daily burn instructions that decide whether registered units may ignite. Its staff maintain the Smoke Data System (registrations and accomplishments), produce the daily and weekly PDFs, the public map, and the annual report. It also runs the Certified Burn Manager program and the Prescribed Fire Liability pilot. This is the office that holds the per-burn history we want.

ODF district offices. Protection districts (Astoria, Tillamook, Forest Grove, West Oregon, North Cascade, South Cascade, Western Lane, Southwest, Coos, Douglas FPA, Central Oregon, Northeast, Klamath-Lake) do the field work: they take registrations from landowners, inspect units, relay burn instructions, and enter accomplishments. If the Salem office says the history is held locally, these are the next stop.

Forest Service ranger districts (fuels staff). Plan and run burns on national forests: write the burn plans, carry the NEPA decision, register with ODF, and post the planned units to the Region 6 tracker. Each unit in the tracker lists its district phone. The Region 6 office in Portland coordinates and publishes the annual fire summaries.

BLM district fuels programs. Same role on BLM land. The OR/WA state office publishes the prescribed fire polygon dataset and the season's planned burns.

Certified Burn Managers. People ODF has certified (24 hours of coursework, three training burns, five-year certification) to plan and lead prescribed burns on ODF-protected land. Certification supports qualified burn planning and operations; it does not imply blanket liability protection. Any legal explanation needs separate current review. A CBM writes the burn plan (objectives, weather prescription, ignition pattern, holding resources, contingencies), files the smoke management registration with the ODF district, waits for the daily instruction, runs the burn as burn boss, and files the accomplishment. They know why each unit was chosen, and they hold the plans and the photos. The directory columns mean: Insured (carries their own liability insurance), For hire (will burn for other landowners), Willing to mentor (will take trainees on burns, which is how new CBMs get their three training fires).

Prescribed burn associations. Landowner cooperatives that pool equipment, people, and know-how to burn each other's land, usually with an OSU Extension fire specialist helping. OSU lists ten associations as of September 11, 2026. They are the route to private-land burns and to the landowners' own reasons.

OSU Extension Fire Program. Regional fire specialists across the state who help PBAs form, teach the CBM course, and publish the guides. The program manager coordinates them. Neutral, well connected, and used to explaining fire to the public; a natural advisor for the map.

OSU Institute for Natural Resources and Oregon Explorer. Built the Oregon Wildfire Risk Explorer, the CWPP planning tool, and the cleaned statewide perimeter history. They have already solved the data-cleaning problems we will hit.

The Nature Conservancy Oregon. Runs prescribed fire at Sycan Marsh and elsewhere, hosts research, and employs two of the 23 CBMs including its fire program director. The most experienced non-agency burn program in the state.

Tribal fire programs. The Klamath Tribes Wildland Fire Program burns on tribal and adjacent federal land. Warm Springs, Grand Ronde, and Siletz are restoring ecocultural burning. Approach as partners whose burns may or may not belong on a public map, on their terms.

NWCC (Portland). The Northwest Geographic Area Coordination Center. Compiles large fire perimeters and daily situation reports for Oregon and Washington and runs Predictive Services.

DEQ Air Quality. Co-administers the smoke management plan, designates smoke sensitive receptor areas with ODF, and runs the air monitoring network. Holds the monitor history.

ODA Smoke Management Program. Registers and permits Willamette Valley field burning and makes daily burn calls with ODF meteorologists. Holds field-level registrations and accomplishments that are not published.

Oregon PUC Safety Division. Reviews the three investor-owned utilities' wildfire mitigation plans and PSPS reports. The route to utility ignition and risk-zone data.

Oregon Department of Emergency Management. RAPTOR, the OEM hub, and (through ODHS's contract) Genasys evacuation zones for all counties and nine tribes.

Oregon Hazards Lab (UO). Operates the Oregon part of the ALERTWest camera network.

Files and endpoints

- drizzle/0014_oregon_fire.sql: additive fire schema, source catalog, immutable import versions, normalized records, private rejected records, verified links, approved explanations and boundary reference. RLS prevents anonymous direct table reads.

- src/lib/oregon-fire/: source registry, classification, geometry checks, ArcGIS pagination, resumable imports and read queries.

- src/components/oregon-fire/: client-only map, filters, list and details. URL query parameters preserve year, layer, search, agency, method, purpose, status, bounds, zoom, list cursor and selection.

- GET /api/oregon-fire/records: filtered list (50 per page), full matching count, source counts, aggregated statewide map or individual geometries, options and data status. Pagination is explicit through nextCursor.

- GET /api/oregon-fire/records/[id]: geometry, meaning, original sanitized attributes, date precision, separately reported acreages, observed history, verified related records and approved explanations.

- GET /api/oregon-fire/sources: per-source coverage, actual last success, overdue state, import counts, unlocated and held records.

- GET /api/oregon-fire/export: all filtered source records as CSV, including provenance, observed timestamps and coverage note. Exports do not stop at the list page or map display limit. Formula-leading cells are escaped.

- GET /api/cron/sync-oregon-fire: existing cron authorization; six-hour schedule configured in vercel.json. Long imports checkpoint and resume; BLM/FACTS refresh when weekly cadence is due.

A leased run is already owned by another worker; do not start concurrent workers for the same source. A failed source should be retried with the same command after its issue is resolved. FOD uses the official seventh-edition SQLite archive, verifies the catalog SHA256, then produces an Oregon JSONL extract and manifest in runtime-data/oregon-fire/ (ignored by Git). It imports 1992–2024 occurrence points. Source coordinates are approximate; the Census generalized Oregon boundary check can exclude source-coded Oregon points outside that geometry. The original release remains the reference when reviewing such discrepancies.

ArcGIS imports first capture a complete object-ID inventory. Requests explicitly ask for WGS84 coordinates and only allowlisted public fields. Oversized queries and transfer-limited responses are split. All IDs must be retrieved; the final inventory and source edit timestamp must match before publishing. Short transient service failures get bounded retries. Genuine empty inventories can publish successfully. Missing source geometry is retained privately in fire.rejections and reported in coverage. It is never given an invented point.

The importer records source schemas, coordinate metadata, query scope, retrieval dates, per-record SHA256 hashes and failures. An active-source pointer changes transactionally only when the import succeeds. Read queries retain the previous successful source snapshot after a failure. Rolling sources keep observed history even when a record disappears; an archived observation is labeled as absent from the latest feed, without inferring cancellation. Full-inventory history remains in storage and in a selected record’s observed history.

Interpretation rules

BLM and FACTS polygons are treatment areas, not measured burned footprints. FACTS activities consumed by wildfire appear in wildfire context. Pile-burning methods are prominent. PNW status codes are retained, and “In Progress” remains ambiguous. No statewide unique-fire total is presented.

Verified IRWIN IDs can connect occurrences; FOD takes precedence over a matching recent WFIGS occurrence. Historical perimeters remain a separate view. ODF occurrence/NWCC enrichment and uncertain spatial matching are not blindly added to counts. Verified project/unit/document links can be added to fire.links with an evidence URL; no inferred NEPA URL is presented as verified.

Original attributes and geometry are preserved alongside normalized fields and simplified display geometry. Acres reported for treatment, reported fire size and mapped polygon area stay separate. Source-reported years outside 1800 through five years beyond the current year are retained as unverified dates, with the original year visible in details, rather than entering the timeline. This handles observed values such as 8888 and 1001 without deleting records.

Future acquired data can use distinct registration, permit, ignition and accomplishment record kinds. Source-specific adapters and verified join keys are required before importing those records; the public catalog currently labels these acquisitions as pending.
