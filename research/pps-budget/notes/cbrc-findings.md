### FY2026-27 Annual Budget Review (presented May 12, 2026)

Signed by 17 members including 5 students; co-chairs Karanja Crews and Adriel Person [2026-27 p.14].

**Headline concerns, verbatim:**
- "This timeline provided nine working days to evaluate a budget of $2.8 billion." [2026-27 p.2]
- "We wish to state plainly that this timeline is inadequate for the responsibility entrusted to this body." [2026-27 p.2]
- "Budget oversight that cannot be conducted thoroughly risks becoming symbolic rather than substantive." [2026-27 p.2]
- "The CBRC is concerned that the proposed budget may make unrealistically optimistic assumptions about key revenue streams and expenses." [2026-27 p.4]
- "The cumulative effect is that PPS students may be receiving meaningfully fewer instructional resources than the district's substantial per-pupil spending would imply." [2026-27 p.5]

**Dollar figures and assumptions challenged:**
- 1% COLA assumption: "significantly lower than any COLA the district has negotiated with its labor partners in recent memory, and significantly lower than the amounts labor partners have proposed in the early stages of their current negotiations"; being off "by even one or two percentage points would significantly increase the budget deficit" [2026-27 p.4].
- Local property tax revenue assumptions questioned; called "one of the factors that drove the mid-year deficit in the 2025-26 budget" [2026-27 p.4-5]. The report warns against "a second consecutive mid-year budget deficit — which we believe would cause significant long-term damage to the public's trust in PPS" [2026-27 p.5].
- Per-pupil disconnect: Volume 2 shows $11,100 per student reaching schools directly; Volume 1 shows approximately $26,300 per student once construction and debt service are set aside from the full budget: "more than twice as much" [2026-27 p.3].
- Instructional share: PPS approximately 53% of operating budget on instruction vs a 61% national average per NCES, "an eight-point gap"; Oregon's above-average teacher salaries and PERS costs are inside the 53%, so "each instructional dollar purchases fewer teacher resources than it would in a typical district" [2026-27 p.5].
- Purchased Services: "a 61.9% increase in Purchased Services over the prior year, from $269.9 million to $437.0 million," itemized as: Management Services +$74,361,000 (199%); Non-Instructional Personal/Professional Services +$16,724,000 (95%); Other Property Services +$14,115,000 (393%); Legal Services +$10,280,000 (750%); Professional Moving Services +$2,366,000 (1,452%); Audit Services +$1,119,000 (493%); Advertising +$460,000 (631%). "The overall proposed increase to Purchased Services is roughly triple the $56.3 million budget gap that the District identified in its proposed budget as needing to be closed." [2026-27 p.9]
- Budget gap stated two ways in one report: "the projected $50 million budget gap and the reality that 82% of expenditures are personnel-related" [2026-27 p.6] vs "the $56.3 million budget gap that the District identified" [2026-27 p.9]. Internal inconsistency worth chasing in the budget book.
- Local Option Levy: proposed budget "reports total levy income of ~$104 million, but does not provide a detailed breakdown of how those specific funds are being expended across functions and objects" [2026-27 p.4].
- PERS: rates "projected to skyrocket this year from just over 4% of each eligible employee's salary to nearly 23%" due to expiring side accounts [2026-27 p.10].
- Special education: state caps SPED funding at 11% (a 1990s limit) while "17% of our students receive Special Education services" [2026-27 p.11].
- MAP assessments: "the District has spent on average $218,000 per year" over the past three years for services ODE can provide at no cost for grades 3-8 [2026-27 p.10].
- Blended classrooms: 16 schools with 44 multi-level classrooms in 2025-26; RAA report interviewed 13 principals and 6 educators and no families [2026-27 p.7-8].
- Outlook: PSU Population and Research Center predicts enrollment decline through at least 2029-30; "a significant budget gap is already identified as likely for the year 2027-28" [2026-27 p.12].

**Recommendations (16 listed at [2026-27 p.12-14]), condensed:** substantially more CBRC time plus complete documentation (multi-year forecasts, object-level detail by function); fall 2026 engagement for 2027-28; a Board feedback loop reporting which CBRC recommendations were acted on; transparency separating operational from capital/debt spending and explaining the per-pupil figures; role definitions (fully/partly/non-student-facing); levy detail by function and object; more conservative or clearly explained assumptions (COLA, property tax); public benchmarking every three years; a proportionate equity-centered reduction framework (protect ELD, mental health, ACCESS transportation, weighted lens for high-need schools); before/after staffing levels for key programs; preserve enrollment-retaining programs; continue blended classroom evaluation; a Community Investment Strategy (CBAs, in-kind partnerships, asset reuse, pooled fundraising); reduce Purchased Services via renegotiation or lower-cost alternatives including ODE assessments replacing MAP; state advocacy (separate PERS from SSF, raise the 11% SPED cap, make SIA additive, Education Stability Fund dollars); continue the long-term fiscal strategy with milestones and candor.

Notable tone shift: the committee "would like to commend the Board and District staff for what the CBRC believes was a concerted effort to implement such a long-term strategy" while calling this year's structural changes "merely a first step" [2026-27 p.11-12].

### FY2025-26 Annual Budget Review (May 6, 2025)

**Headline concerns, verbatim:**
- "The reduction of 247 positions ... to help close this year's $40 million budget shortfall will be felt across all service areas and throughout our community." [2025-26 p.2]
- "As a committee representing our communities, we find it difficult to support a budget process that continues to result in cuts, the loss of educator talent and reduced student access to a well-rounded education—especially in the absence of a clear, actionable plan or even a timeline." [2025-26 p.2]
- "The district cannot afford to make temporary programs permanent without permanent funding." [2025-26 p.10]
- "In our last report, we noted the need for more time. That recommendation remains unaddressed." [2025-26 p.12]

**Dollar figures and assumptions challenged:**
- $40 million shortfall; 247 positions cut; third consecutive year of reductions [2025-26 p.1-2].
- Context stats: 10.8% enrollment drop since 2020; chronic absenteeism over 36% ("ten points above the national average"); "$1.4 billion maintenance backlog" [2025-26 p.1] — but page 10 says deferred maintenance "has led to a $1 billion maintenance backlog." Internal inconsistency.
- Title 1: anticipated 25% federal reduction; Title schools average under 1% enrollment decline vs 4% non-Title, yet staffing cuts average 9.9% at Title schools vs 8.1% non-Title [2025-26 p.2, p.3 footnote 3].
- Staffing variance: 4.4 to 14.1 FTE per student across K-5 schools [2025-26 p.3].
- Equity allocation: reduced from 8% to 4% for K-8 in the prior year's budget and maintained at 4%; 9% flat for all high schools regardless of demographics, which the committee questions [2025-26 p.2 footnote 1, p.5].
- TAG: elimination of the 0.5 FTE coordinator after a two-thirds budget cut in 2024-25, while PPS is under an ODE Corrective Action Plan for TAG [2025-26 p.5].
- Blended classrooms: about 18 more in 3rd/4th and 4th/5th configurations; savings unquantified [2025-26 p.5].
- Teacher overload pay: "from $1.7 million in 2021-22 to over $4 million projected in 2025-26" [2025-26 p.5].
- MAP: "$655,000 over three years ... $215,000 in the 2024-25 school year alone," vs free ODE interim assessments; recommend discontinuing MAP [2025-26 p.6].
- Central office: budget cites 65 central office positions cut but "it remains unclear how much central office spending has changed overall"; Function 2000 declined only ~$2.2 million (0.5%), from $453.7 million to $451.5 million [2025-26 p.6 + footnote 5].
- New senior administrative position at $224,000-$239,000 "added in the same budget that eliminates 247 staff roles" [2025-26 p.6].
- Bond assumption: budget assumes passage of a $1.83 billion, eight-year capital bond on the May 2025 ballot [2025-26 p.8].
- Roosevelt bleachers: $2.5 million non-bond capital spend in March 2024 flagged as no longer feasible practice [2025-26 p.8].
- Reserves: "Last year, the superintendent substantially drew down reserves leaving PPS at the minimum 5% threshold" [2025-26 p.10].
- Food service costs per student up 16% ($583.69 to $677.33 per ADMr) [2025-26 p.10 footnote 7].
- Headline budget optics: total budget down 15% ($2.39B to $2.04B) mostly from bond completion, while the general fund rose from $1.366B to $1.391B; distinction "not clearly communicated in public-facing documents" [2025-26 p.12].
- Engagement: 3,929 total responses but only 202 tied to the key budget feedback form, 56 from certified educators [2025-26 p.13].
- Timeline: "the committee received just nine days to analyze more than 500 pages of budget content" [2025-26 p.12].

**Recommendations, condensed:** targeted structural reductions to whole programs/initiatives/locations and a long-term fiscal strategy with milestones, timelines, stakeholder input [p.2]; QEM benchmarking of class sizes [p.3]; analyze Title 1 vs non-Title staffing cuts and stabilize Title staffing relative to enrollment [p.4]; clarify TAG compliance [p.5]; report blended classroom savings and evaluate effectiveness; compare overload pay to EA cost [p.5]; discontinue MAP for free ODE assessments [p.6]; disclose central office cuts separately [p.6]; enrollment marketing, exit interviews, outreach [p.7]; attendance-focused staffing and calendar with fewer short weeks (only 16 of 38 weeks are five-day) [p.7-8]; bond scope transparency [p.8]; revenue diversification (public-private partnerships, leasing two unused buildings, CUB revenue from $100K to $250K) [p.9]; avoid one-time funds without exit strategy; use HB 3435 nutrition funds to replenish reserves; rebuild reserves above 5% [p.10-11]; addendum: percent changes in budget tables, spreadsheet publication, disaggregated engagement data, and a review timeline of at least two weeks pre-release [p.12-14].

### FY2024-25 Annual Budget Review (May 3, 2024)

**Headline concerns, verbatim:**
- "This year, significant budget reductions and a shortened budget review timeline has made it challenging to provide thoughtful analysis and feedback to guide the board, yet the need for community input has never been greater." [2024-25 p.1]
- "Despite record funding of nearly $2.4 billion to serve PPS's 44,000 students, of which approximately 46% is available for annual operations, PPS continues to see rising class sizes and cuts to staffing." [2024-25 p.2]
- "Nine days to review and provide a meaningful analysis of a more than 500 page of budget content is a heavy lift for volunteers." [2024-25 p.4]
- On the 2023-24 contract negotiations (footnote): "We specifically asked if funds allocated for negotiations were sufficient to cover the anticipated cost of the contracts ... As we did not receive information sufficient to we were not able to advise on these significant and foreseeable impacts to the district budget in 2023-24." [2024-25 p.11] (sic; sentence garbled in the original)

**Dollar figures and assumptions challenged:**
- General Fund up 2.3% ($19.3M), $833.8M to $853.1M, "this has not kept pace with labor costs in 2023-24 and associated benefits, PERS obligations" [2024-25 p.3].
- Reserve trajectory: current trajectory "would deplete reserves if no additional state funding is allocated"; recommend maintaining 5% [2024-25 p.3].
- Deferred maintenance: FCI "poor"; staff estimate $992 million over 10 years to address all FCA deficiencies; ~$700 million to move from poor to fair [2024-25 p.4].
- Teacher FTE 2,177 (2018-19) to 2,138 (proposed 2024-25); the report says per-1,000 "has declined (from 47.6 to 50.5)" [2024-25 p.5] — sic: those numbers rise, and the 2023-24 report described the same figures as an increase [2023-24 p.3].
- Instructional Coaches (not student-facing) 64.8 to 134.8 FTE, "a 108% percent increase" [2024-25 p.6]; the 2023-24 report called the identical change "70.0 percent" [2023-24 p.3]. 108% is arithmetically right.
- Library/media FTE cuts: elementary -31% (45.35 to 31.30), middle -34.5% (15.7 to 10.3), high -24.6% (14.2 to 10.7); libraries closed up to three hours a day at secondary, multiple days a week at elementary [2024-25 p.6].
- Equity allocation cut in half, 8% to 4% [2024-25 p.6].
- TAG: questioned the "66% reduction in support for Talented and Gifted (TAG) program funding to .5 FTE" when enrollment fell under 3% [2024-25 p.7].
- Central office: cut "by approximately $55 million over the last three years"; information "not readily available in the current budget" [2024-25 p.10].
- Lost instruction: ODE waiver sought for 14 instructional hours after January weather closures [2024-25 p.3].

**Recommendations, condensed:** advocate state funding to QEM; enrollment outreach; do not rely on unknown funding increases; maintain 5% reserve; aggressive maintenance planning including leasing/selling non-student properties; prioritize repairs over new construction in the bond [p.3-4]; share school summaries and survey detail; more review time; publish SCIPs [p.4]; recruit/retain Black, brown and indigenous student-facing staff [p.5]; prioritize student-facing positions and analyze redundant service contracts; reconsider library cuts [p.6]; ROI data on equity allocations; manage the 8%-to-4% transition [p.7]; SAA access below the 10th percentile; sustain CBO funding; restore TAG to enrollment-matched levels [p.7]; prefer class size and student supports over more instructional coaches [p.8]; review SPED staffing shifts and the neighborhood-model transition [p.8]; QEM library staffing; K-2 early literacy FTE if funds allow [p.9]; more information on Multiple Pathways to Graduation [p.10]; review central office cut impacts [p.10]. An addendum by departing 15-year member Roger Kirchner urges QEM lobbying, early budget consultation with unions and CBRC, fiscal notes on board policies, caution with board "find funding" directives, and 5-year boundary adjustments [2024-25 p.12-13].

**Headline concerns, verbatim:**
- "CBRC is concerned that these additional funding sources are hiding the looming shortfall due to ongoing enrollment decline from the general public." [2023-24 p.3]

**Dollar figures and assumptions challenged:**

- Deferred maintenance "grew in excess of $400 Million in FY 2022-23. The cumulative total for deferred maintenance now exceeds $1 Billion." [2023-24 p.4]
- MAP testing: "The current budget includes $1M for MAP testing"; recommends the free, ODE-aligned Oregon Statewide Assessment System components instead [2023-24 p.6].
- SIA allocations questioned: over $3 million on Arts Pathways and over $4 million on Instructional Coaches distributed "equally, not equitably" [2023-24 p.7].
- ESSER: school grants of $100K-$120K each totaling $8,759,614 distributed equally not equitably; only 20 Learning Acceleration Specialist FTE at $2,250,000, funded on expiring one-time ESSER [2023-24 p.7-8].
- Equity allocation static at 8% since FY16-17 despite pandemic-magnified need [2023-24 p.8].
- FTE data (sic warnings): Instructional Coaches 64.8 to 134.8 "a 70.0 percent increase" (actually 108%); Counselors 143.7 to 161.2 "17.5 percent" (2024-25 report says 12% for same figures); SPED FTE 595.5 to 724.0 "a 128.5 percent increase" (actually ~21.6%) [2023-24 p.3]. The committee's own percentages are unreliable; the underlying FTE pairs are consistent across reports.
- No designated K-2 early literacy intervention funds; high-dosage tutoring starts at grade 3 [2023-24 p.11].

**Recommendations, condensed:** proactive enrollment outreach; analyze non-student-facing spending and redundant service contracts; reconsider central office contracts; maintenance plan including leasing/selling surplus property; recruit/retain Black, brown and indigenous staff; 90% CBO pass-through; school-level budget summaries; participatory budgeting; drop MAP for state-aligned assessments; raise the equity allocation and measure its ROI; more social workers and lower counselor ratios (1:100 at highest-need schools); target instructional coaches equitably; expand Learning Acceleration Specialists from 20 to at least 40 with at least 15 in K-3; K-2 class size caps at high-CU schools [2023-24 p.3-11].

## 4. FY2026-27: press-reported claims vs the primary document

**(a) "unrealistically optimistic" revenue assumptions — VERIFIED with a scope caveat.** The exact phrase appears, but it covers both revenue and expense assumptions, and the lead example is an expense assumption: "The CBRC is concerned that the proposed budget may make unrealistically optimistic assumptions about key revenue streams and expenses. Most notably, the assumption of a 1% cost of living adjustment (COLA) for employees is significantly lower than any COLA the district has negotiated with its labor partners in recent memory" [2026-27 p.4]. On revenue specifically the report is softer: "We also have questions about whether the district has made appropriately conservative adjustments to expected revenue from local property taxes" [2026-27 p.4-5]. Press framing that narrows this to "revenue assumptions" understates the COLA point, which the committee itself calls the most notable.

**(b) ~nine working days to review — VERIFIED verbatim.** "Members convened for the first time to discuss the budget on April 29th, 2026, and were asked to deliver its review by May 8. This timeline provided nine working days to evaluate a budget of $2.8 billion." [2026-27 p.2]

**(c) third-party contract growth — VERIFIED and stronger than press suggested.** "The proposed budget reflects a 61.9% increase in Purchased Services over the prior year, from $269.9 million to $437.0 million," with seven itemized line increases and the observation that the overall increase "is roughly triple the $56.3 million budget gap that the District identified" [2026-27 p.9]. The recommendation: "explore opportunities to reduce this spending on third party service providers, including through renegotiating existing contracts" [2026-27 p.9].

**(d) "meaningfully fewer instructional resources" — VERIFIED verbatim (with tense nuance).** "The cumulative effect is that PPS students may be receiving meaningfully fewer instructional resources than the district's substantial per-pupil spending would imply." [2026-27 p.5] Press paraphrase "may receive" vs actual "may be receiving" is immaterial. Context press may omit: the committee labels this a preliminary finding ("We present these as preliminary findings" [2026-27 p.5]) resting on its 53% vs 61% NCES instructional-share comparison, and asks the district to run an official benchmarking study rather than asserting the conclusion.

Net: no press-vs-primary discrepancy of substance on these four claims; the only correction is that "unrealistically optimistic" is aimed at revenue AND expense assumptions, chiefly the 1% COLA.
